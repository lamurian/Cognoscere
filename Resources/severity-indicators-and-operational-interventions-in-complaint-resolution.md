## Summary

Systematic complaint resolution relies on precise indicators to measure grievance severity and disproportionality. Under the HCAT framework, complaints are evaluated along a severity scale (1 = low/minor inconvenience, 2 = medium/moderate disruption, 3 = high/severe harm or negligence) alongside a clinical harm scale (1 = no harm to 5 = catastrophic harm) [@gillespie2016]. Furthermore, health systems utilize disproportionality metrics—such as Reporting Odds Ratios (ROR)—to detect statistical spikes in specific complaint types across hospital departments [@biebogh2023].

Operational interventions leverage these analytics to complete the quality improvement loop. Low-severity, high-volume issues (e.g., waiting times, noise) trigger process redesigns in scheduling and admission/discharge workflows [@odowd2019]. High-severity grievances trigger immediate root cause analyses (RCA), staff communication re-training, and clinical governance interventions to prevent recurrences and protect patient safety [@gillespie2016; @biebogh2023].

## Key Points

- **Severity and Harm Scoring**: HCAT evaluates problem severity (1-3) and clinical harm (1-5) to prioritize interventions [@gillespie2016].
- **Disproportionality Metrics**: Statistical methods identify clinical units with abnormal complaint spikes relative to baseline patient volume [@biebogh2023].
- **Closed-Loop Interventions**: Combines administrative workflow redesign for high-volume delays with clinical governance for high-severity safety risks [@odowd2019].
