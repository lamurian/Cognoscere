## Summary

The Indonesian Red Cross (PMI) with IFRC and USAID support implemented Community-Based Surveillance (CBS) via the Community Epidemic and Pandemic Preparedness Programme (CP3) since 2019. Starting in 8 villages, now scaled across multiple districts. Volunteers use SatuSBM mobile app (transitioned from KoBo in 2022-23) to report unusual health events (dengue, COVID-19, TB, leptospirosis) to supervisors who escalate to health authorities. Permenko No. 7/2022 provides national legal framework for CBS.

## Key Points

### Category: Contributory (structured volunteer reporting)
- **Citizen role**: Trained volunteers detect and report via standardized app; health authorities analyze and respond
- **Design**: Top-down protocol with community adaptation; volunteers do not design case definitions

### Impacts
- High predictive accuracy for dengue, COVID-19, TB, leptospirosis in pilot villages
- Early detection: e.g., 10-year-old dengue case in Pandeglang (2024) reported via SatuSBM, rapid referral saved life
- Weekly 'Clean Saturday' community clean-ups reduce breeding sites
- Government coordination: PMI partners with MoH and MoA for One Health integration
- SatuSBM links to national surveillance systems (EWARS, SITB)

### Methodology
- **Volunteer recruitment**: PMI volunteers (Bu Susi, Bu M'Bai) trained on case definitions and SatuSBM app
- **Reporting**: Mobile app sends alerts to supervisor → Puskesmas → District Health Office
- **Case definitions**: Standardized for priority diseases (dengue, COVID-19, TB, leptospirosis)
- **Community engagement**: House visits, school sessions, Clean Saturday events, 3M promotion
- **Digital platform**: SatuSBM (adapted from Nyss); potential One Health integration with MoH/MoA

### Points of Improvement
1. **Volunteer retention**: Unpaid volunteers; competing livelihood demands
2. **Digital literacy**: Some volunteers prefer paper; app usability challenges
3. **Case definition gaps**: VPDs and leptospirosis lack community reporting procedures
4. **System integration**: SatuSBM not fully interoperable with MoH systems
5. **Sustainability**: Dependent on external funding (USAID, DFAT, Australian Red Cross)
6. **Geographic coverage**: Urban/rural disparities in volunteer density
