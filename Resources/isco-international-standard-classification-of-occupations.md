## Summary

The International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO) is a publicly accessible hierarchical taxonomy maintained by the International Labour Organization (ILO) for classifying occupations across countries. ISCO provides the foundational framework for health worker specialization archetypes used by many national and international classification systems.

ISCO-08 (the 2008 revision) is organized into 10 Major Groups, 43 Sub-major Groups, 130 Minor Groups, and 436 Unit Groups. Healthcare workers are classified under Major Group 2 (Professionals) with Sub-major Group 22 (Health Professionals) and Major Group 3 (Technicians and Associate Professionals) with Sub-major Group 32 (Health Associate Professionals). This hierarchical structure enables granular specialization classification from broad categories to specific roles.

ISCO is freely available for public use and serves as the backbone for many national occupational classification systems including the EU's ESCO, the US Standard Occupational Classification (SOC), and Canada's National Occupational Classification (NOC). It provides standardized definitions and criteria for occupational classification, enabling international comparability of health workforce data.

## Key Points

- **Structure**: 4-level hierarchy (Major Groups → Sub-major Groups → Minor Groups → Unit Groups)
- **Healthcare Coverage**: Major Group 2 (Professionals), Sub-major 22 (Health Professionals), Sub-major 32 (Health Associate Professionals)
- **Access**: Freely available at https://www.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/
- **Revision**: ISCO-08 (International Labour Organization, 2008)
- **Scope**: International standard used by 100+ countries for occupational statistics
- **Maintenance**: International Labour Organization (ILO)
- **Use Cases**: Labor force statistics, workforce planning, international comparisons, migration data
