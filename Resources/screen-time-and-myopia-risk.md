## Summary

Screen time is significantly associated with increased myopia risk in children and adolescents. A meta-analysis found that more than 2 hours/day of screen exposure was associated with 2.24 times higher myopia risk (OR 2.24; 95% CI 1.47-3.42) @gonzlezprez2025. Another meta-analysis of 268 studies found children spending more time on screens had 40% higher risk of developing myopia (OR 1.40; 95% CI 1.31-1.50) @gonzlezprez2025.

Continuous smartphone use (20-minute episodes without interruptions) showed significant association with refractive error (β=-0.07; 95% CI -0.13 to -0.01) and axial length (β=0.004; 95% CI 0.001-0.008) in teenagers @gonzlezprez2025. Computer screens showed stronger impact on myopia than other devices (OR 8.19; 95% CI 4.78-14.04) @gonzlezprez2025.

The mechanisms involve both blue light exposure and the near-work nature of screen use. Increased time outdoors appears protective against myopia development @souza2025. The global increase in childhood myopia has been linked to increased screen time and reduced outdoor activity.

## Key Points

- 2+ hours/day doubles myopia risk (OR 2.24)
- Computer screens show strongest association (OR 8.19)
- Continuous 20-minute use episodes measurable impact
- Outdoor time is protective against myopia
- Near-work and blue light are primary mechanisms
