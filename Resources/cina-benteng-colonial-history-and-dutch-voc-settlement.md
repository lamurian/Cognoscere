## Summary

The Cina Benteng community's development was shaped by Dutch colonial policy in Tangerang. A VOC fort was constructed in the 1670s along the Cisadane River as a defensive position against the Banten Sultanate. The Chinese community that gathered around this fort became known as 'Orang Benteng' (people of the fort). The Dutch pursued a deliberate policy described as 'One harmony between 3 races, under one loyalty to the Dutch colonial Empire' — merging Chinese, Dutch, and Sundanese-Betawi populations into a hybrid Peranakan class.

This colonial project created a feudal landowner system where Chinese captains (kapitein Tionghoa) administered territories. The Seven Great Families (setsei) — House of Oey, Ong, Li, Lim, Teng, The, and Tan — controlled vast estates across Tangerang. These families arrived in the 18th century with Qing dynasty backing, maintaining loyalty to the Chinese emperor while serving Dutch colonial interests. They contributed significantly to the colonial economy as cotton and rice farmers, and some served as colonial soldiers.

The Dutch withdrawal from Tangerang in 1950 — the last area in Java to be handed to the Republic — triggered a catastrophic transition. The wealthy Chinese landowner class lost their estates through confiscation and violence. Many families who had prospered under colonial rule found themselves impoverished overnight. This trauma drove a significant exodus to the Netherlands, California, and Louisiana.

## Key Points

- VOC fort built 1670s along Cisadane River as defense against Banten Sultanate
- Dutch 'One harmony between 3 races' policy: Chinese-Dutch-Sundanese hybrid Peranakan
- Seven Great Families (setsei): Oey, Ong, Li, Lim, Teng, The, Tan
- Chinese captains (kapitein Tionghoa) administered feudal landowner system
- 1950 Dutch withdrawal triggered estate confiscation and exodus to Netherlands/USA
- Tangerang was last area in Java returned to Republic (early 1950s)
