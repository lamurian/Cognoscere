## Summary

The National Uniform Claim Committee (NUCC) Health Care Provider Taxonomy is a publicly accessible classification system specifically designed to identify and categorize healthcare providers by their type, classification, and area of specialization. It is the primary taxonomy used in the United States for provider identification in HIPAA-mandated transactions and the National Provider Identifier (NPI) system.

The taxonomy uses 10-character alphanumeric codes structured into three hierarchical levels: (1) Provider Grouping (e.g., Allopathic & Osteopathic Physicians), (2) Classification (e.g., Internal Medicine), and (3) Area of Specialization (e.g., Cardiovascular Disease). This three-level structure directly addresses the core archetype of health worker specialization by providing granular classification of provider roles and competencies.

The NUCC maintains the code set and processes requests for new codes or modifications. The taxonomy is freely available for public use, with CSV and PDF downloads accessible through the NUCC website. It is recognized as a federal standard under HIPAA and is used in electronic health transactions, provider credentialing, and workforce analytics.

## Key Points

- **Code Structure**: 10-character alphanumeric with three hierarchical levels (Provider Grouping → Classification → Area of Specialization)
- **OID**: 2.16.840.1.113883.6.101 (official identifier for use in health information systems)
- **Access**: Freely available at https://www.nucc.org/index.php/code-sets-mainmenu-41/provider-taxonomy-mainmenu-40
- **Scope**: Covers all healthcare provider types including physicians, nurses, allied health professionals, and support staff
- **Use Cases**: HIPAA transactions, NPI applications, provider credentialing, workforce analytics, billing systems
- **Maintenance**: National Uniform Claim Committee (NUCC) Code Subcommittee
