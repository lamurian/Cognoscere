## Summary

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) is the most frequently applied motivational framework in gamified health interventions, providing the theoretical mechanism through which game elements influence intrinsic motivation and sustained behavior change.

SDT posits three basic psychological needs: autonomy (sense of volition and choice), competence (sense of mastery and effectiveness), and relatedness (sense of connection and belonging). When gamification elements satisfy these needs, they foster intrinsic motivation, which is associated with more durable behavior change than extrinsic motivation alone.

In a scoping review of 15 RCTs, Bas-Sarmiento et al. (2025) found that only 40% of gamified eHealth interventions explicitly reported a theoretical framework. Among those that did, SDT was the most commonly cited, alongside the RAMP model (relationships, autonomy, mastery, purpose), the ARCS model (attention, relevance, confidence, motivation), and the Transtheoretical Model. Studies that clarified their motivational approach reported better outcomes than those that did not specify [@bassarmiento2025].

## Key Points

- **Autonomy in gamification**: Choice of tasks, customizable profiles, self-paced progression; e.g., participants choosing which health data to collect or report
- **Competence in gamification**: Progressive difficulty, skill trees, mastery badges; e.g., advancing from simple symptom reporting to complex data classification
- **Relatedness in gamification**: Team competitions, community forums, social comparison; e.g., neighborhood health challenges or collaborative data collection
- **Overjustification risk**: Extrinsic rewards (points, leaderboards) can undermine intrinsic motivation when they become the primary driver
- **Tailoring necessity**: Uniform gamification strategies may not maximize benefits for all participants; intelligent adaptation is needed
