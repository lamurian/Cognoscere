## Summary

Liu et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review guided by the dual theoretical framework of risk-protection, analysing 60 core publications (2009-2025) from the Web of Science Core Collection on psychological resilience among cancer patients. Bibliometric analysis was conducted using Bibliometrix, supplemented by qualitative coding through NVivo.[@liu2025c]

The study constructed a risk-protection intersection matrix and thematic ecological map. Research on psychological resilience in cancer patients has increased markedly since 2018, spanning psycho-oncology, nursing, and health psychology. The thematic foci centre on five areas: individual psychological resource mechanisms, family support systems, symptom burden regulation, cultural adaptation experiences, and psychological resilience in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.[@liu2025c]

**Methodological evolution.** The field has progressed from traditional quantitative approaches to mixed methods and qualitative interviews, emphasising integration of subjective experience with systematic interpretation.[@liu2025c]

**Theoretical contributions.** The review clarified the interactive structure between different types of risk factors and multilayered protective mechanisms, emphasising resilience as the outcome of multi-system dynamic regulation. It revealed the mediating and moderating roles of cultural factors, social support, and family structure in resilience formation, and proposed intervention strategies applicable at individual, family, and community levels.[@liu2025c]

## Key Points

- 60 publications analysed (2009-2025) using bibliometric and qualitative coding methods
- Five thematic foci: psychological resources, family support, symptom regulation, cultural adaptation, COVID-19 context
- Methodological shift from quantitative to mixed methods and qualitative approaches
- Resilience conceptualised as multi-system dynamic regulation
- Multi-level interventions proposed: individual, family, community
