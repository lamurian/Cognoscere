## Summary

Four complementary theoretical frameworks explain why citizen science popularization strategies work: Self-Determination Theory (SDT), Social Movement Theory (Klandermans model), Communities of Practice (Lave & Wenger), and Social Identity Theory. These frameworks operate at different levels—individual motivation, collective action, learning trajectories, and group identity—providing a multi-level explanation for participation and persistence.

## Key Points

### Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci, 2000)
- **Core constructs**: Autonomy (self-direction), Competence (mastery), Relatedness (belonging)
- **Application**: Low-barrier entry supports autonomy; scaffolded progression builds competence; forums/teams foster relatedness
- **Evidence**: Frensley et al. (2017) found dropouts cited lack of autonomy (forced project structure), competence struggles (online tools), and limited relatedness (isolation) as key barriers [@frensley2017]
- **Crowding-in effect**: Reputation systems enhance rather than undermine intrinsic motivation when environment is trusting [@nov2014]

### Social Movement Theory (Klandermans, 1997)
- **Three motives**: Collective (project goals), Norm-oriented (social pressure), Reward (reputation, career)
- **Differential effects**: Collective motives drive both quantity AND quality; norm-oriented and intrinsic motives drive quantity but NOT quality [@nov2014]
- **Implication**: Popularization must communicate project mission (collective) while providing reputation pathways (reward), not just social pressure

### Communities of Practice (Lave & Wenger, 1991)
- **Trajectory**: Legitimate peripheral participation → full participation → leadership
- **Mechanism**: Novices learn through observation and gradual responsibility increase
- **Evidence**: Galaxy Zoo volunteers progressed from classifiers to forum moderators to co-discoverers of novel objects [@masters2019a; @wikipediacontributors2026a]
- **Institutional support**: Libraries as Hubs model invests in professional learning communities to build staff capacity as facilitators [@cox2026]

### Social Identity Theory
- **Mechanism**: Shared identity ("Zooites," "eBirders," "Foldit Players") increases commitment and collective efficacy
- **Evidence**: Galaxy Zoo volunteers self-identified as "Zooites"; Foldit players listed as collective authors on Nature paper [@masters2019a; @koepnick2019]
- **Collective efficacy**: Belief that "we can make a difference together" sustains participation through challenges
