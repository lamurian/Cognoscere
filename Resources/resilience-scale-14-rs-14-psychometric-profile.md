## Summary

The Resilience Scale (RS) by Wagnild and Young measures resilience as a positive personality characteristic comprising personal competence and acceptance of self and life. The short form RS-14 (Wagnild, 2009) contains 14 self-report items rated on a 7-point Likert scale (1-7), summed to a total of 14-98. Interpretation bands: below 65 = low resilience, 65-81 = moderate, above 81 = high [@surzykiewicz2019]. The RS-14 correlates r=.97 with the full 25-item version and is the most widely translated resilience measure [@surzykiewicz2019].

Internal validity: the RS-14 is unidimensional. A large Polish validation (N=2,715 across adolescents, young adults, and at-risk youth) confirmed a one-factor model with good fit (CFI=.98-.99, RMSEA=.015-.049), all item loadings above .40, and 31-39% of variance explained [@surzykiewicz2019]. Internal consistency: alpha .82-.87 across the three Polish samples (total .853); the developer reports alpha .90-.96, and cross-cultural values range from .79 (Spain) to .92 (China), with Japan .88, Korea .90, and Italy .88 [@surzykiewicz2019]. Four-week test-retest was r=.88 (ICC=.933) in the Polish study [@surzykiewicz2019]; other studies report .60-.84.

External validity: the RS-14 correlated positively with life satisfaction (SWLS r=.66) and negatively with perceived stress (PSS r=-.56) and depression dimensions (r=-.27 to -.58) in young adults [@surzykiewicz2019]. Consistent with the trait conceptualisation, scores show no gender differences and increase slightly with age [@surzykiewicz2019]. The RS (25-item) scored 6/18 in the Windle et al. (2011) quality review, with top content validity and construct validity ratings [@windle2011].

Licensing: the RS-14 is free for non-commercial research and educational use with permission from the developer (Gail Wagnild / The Resilience Center); commercial use requires a paid license. The original RS-25 is likewise permission-gated [@wagnild1993].

## Key Points

- 14 items, 7-point scale, total 14-98; bands <65 low, 65-81 moderate, >81 high
- Unidimensional; one factor accounts for 31-39% of variance; loadings >.40 [@surzykiewicz2019]
- Internal consistency: alpha .82-.87 (Polish samples), .90-.96 (developer data), .79-.92 cross-culturally
- Test-retest: r=.88 at 4 weeks (ICC=.933) [@surzykiewicz2019]
- Convergent: SWLS r=.66; discriminant: PSS r=-.56, depression r=-.27 to -.58 [@surzykiewicz2019]
- Correlates r=.97 with the RS-25 full version
- Free for research with author permission; commercial use requires license
- Quality rating 6/18 in Windle et al. (2011), max content-validity score [@windle2011]
