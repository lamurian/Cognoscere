## Summary

Health organizations systematically process qualitative patient complaint data using standardized taxonomies such as the Healthcare Complaints Analysis Tool (HCAT) [@gillespie2016]. Developed by Reader and Gillespie, HCAT categorizes unstructured text into three core domains (Clinical, Management, and Relational) and seven problem categories: Quality, Safety, Environment, Institutional Processes, Timing & Access, Listening & Communication, and Respect & Patient Rights [@reader2014; @gillespie2016].

To process large volumes of narrative feedback efficiently, modern health systems increasingly pair HCAT with Natural Language Processing (NLP) and machine learning classifiers [@biebogh2023]. Automated text analysis extracts key entities, assigns sentiment scores, and maps unstructured complaint letters directly into standardized taxonomy codes. This multi-level classification enables rapid sorting, longitudinal tracking, and standardized cross-departmental benchmarking.

## Key Points

- **HCAT Structure**: Three broad domains (Clinical, Management, Relational) divided into seven distinct problem categories [@gillespie2016].
- **Standardized Coding**: Transforms qualitative complaint narratives into structured, quantifiable data for statistical analysis [@reader2014].
- **NLP Integration**: Machine learning models automate complaint classification and real-time triage for quality assurance teams [@biebogh2023].
