## Summary

The Lancet Psychiatry Commission on youth mental health (2024) identified a global crisis in young people's mental health, with anxiety and depression diagnoses increasing by 63% among US young adults from 2005 to 2017 alone [@mcgorry2024]. In 2017, individuals aged 18-25 had the highest rate of reported depressive episodes (13%) compared to older age groups (7.7% and 4.7%). A 69-year longitudinal US study found an overall rise in MMPI scores among college students since the 1930s, indicating a long-term upward trajectory in psychological distress [@osorio2021].

Multiple contributing factors have been identified. Social media engagement is strongly associated with increased depression — young adults using 7+ social media platforms are 3 times more likely to experience elevated depression and anxiety than those using 2 or fewer. Academic stress affects 40% of US young adults attending institutions of higher education, with 45% reporting above-average stress in 2019. Economic stress, particularly student debt (44 million borrowers totaling US$1.5 trillion), is linked to 11.7% higher stress and 13.2% higher depression symptomology. These factors compound: individuals with student debt who also use social media heavily face multiplicative risk.

The consequences are severe. In 2019, 13.3% of US college students seriously considered suicide and 2.0% attempted it. Self-inflicted injuries were the second leading cause of death for ages 10-24 in 2015. Substance abuse co-occurs frequently — 26.8% of young adults aged 18-25 with a substance use disorder also had another mental health disorder. Depression impairs work performance 29% more than chronic physical conditions including arthritis, diabetes, and hypertension.

## Key Points

- Anxiety/depression diagnoses up 63% among US young adults (2005-2017)
- Social media: 7+ platforms = 3x higher depression/anxiety risk
- 13.3% of US college students seriously considered suicide in 2019
- Student debt linked to 13.2% higher depression symptomology
- Depression impairs work performance 29% more than chronic physical conditions
- Long-term trend (69 years) shows rising psychological distress in college students
