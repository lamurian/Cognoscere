## Summary

Solar-powered health infrastructure is not hypothetical -- it has been deployed in Indonesia and other low-resource settings. The German company Solarkiosk implemented two solar-powered health centers in Lampung, Sumatra, Indonesia, serving approximately 720,000 inhabitants who previously had little or no access to health screening and vaccination. These grid-independent centers use solar energy with battery storage for night-time operation, integrating medical testing equipment, vaccine refrigeration, and remote monitoring [@solarkiosksolutionsgmbh2023].

Similarly, Kumar et al. (2025) describe an AI and IoT-enabled solar-powered health kiosk designed for real-time health monitoring and remote consultation in rural and underserved areas. The system integrates sensors for vital parameters (BP, ECG, SpO2, temperature), uses AI for real-time analysis and prediction, and operates on solar power -- making it suitable for off-grid locations [@kumar2025].

The Solarkiosk E-HUBB platform has also been deployed for Connected Solar Clinics in refugee camps in Jordan and Bangladesh, demonstrating the technology's robustness in challenging environments. For the proposed mental health kiosk, which specifies 'bertenaga solar panel' and 'offline first,' these precedents confirm technical feasibility. The key requirements are sufficient solar panel capacity for a touchscreen computer, thermal printer, and audio system, plus battery storage for night operation.

Indonesia's Smart Village framework (6 pillars) includes 'Smart Environment' (sustainable resource management) and 'Smart Living' (digital inclusion), both of which align with solar-powered health kiosks. The Palapa Ring fiber backbone and 4G BTS expansion provide connectivity options for data sync when available [@solarkiosksolutionsgmbh2023].

## Key Points
- Solarkiosk deployed two solar health centers in Lampung, Sumatra (serving ~720,000 people)
- Kumar et al. (2025): solar-powered AI/IoT health kiosk for rural areas validated at IEEE conference
- Solarkiosk E-HUBB used in refugee camps in Jordan and Bangladesh
- Solar panel + battery storage enables 24/7 operation off-grid
- Aligns with Indonesia's Smart Village framework and rural electrification goals
- Confirms technical feasibility of the proposed solar-powered mental health kiosk
