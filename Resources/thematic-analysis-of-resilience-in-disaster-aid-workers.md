## Summary

Sadeghi et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review and thematic analysis of resilience components among disaster and emergency aid workers, adhering to PRISMA protocol and registered in PROSPERO (CRD42024508783). From 3,198 initial records, 17 studies met inclusion criteria, spanning 2015-2023 across multiple countries.[@sadeghi2025]

The inductive content analysis identified five key components of resilience organised into two main groups: **traits** and **processes**. Resilience traits include (1) health status (physical, psychological protective factors, psychological risk factors, spiritual, and social dimensions), (2) essence and personality (self-management, personality characteristics, personal beliefs, self-adequacy), and (3) capability and competency (specialised knowledge, skills, professional experience). Resilience processes include (1) support platforms (family support, social support, support from friends/colleagues, organisational/legal support) and (2) organisation and job (organisational factors, resources/equipment, efficient human capital, nature of the job).[@sadeghi2025]

**Cultural variation.** A key finding was that resilience expression varies across cultural contexts. In collectivist cultures (East Asia, Middle East), resilience is closely tied to family and community support, while in individualist cultures (North America, Europe), personal traits like self-efficacy and optimism are more prominent. Spiritual beliefs played a significant role in predominantly religious societies (Iran, South Africa), whereas secular contexts emphasised psychological and social support systems.[@sadeghi2025]

**Definitional refinement.** The authors reconceptualised resilience as a multidimensional construct encompassing not only recovery from adversity but also positive adaptation, sustained functionality, and growth during or after stressor exposure, operating across individual, organisational, and systemic levels.[@sadeghi2025]

## Key Points

- 17 studies analysed: 9 quantitative, 5 qualitative, 3 mixed methods
- Five resilience components: health status, essence/personality, capability/competency, support platforms, organisation/job
- Resilience traits and processes interact across individual and contextual levels
- Cultural context shapes which resilience components are emphasised
- Organisations play a crucial role through resources, equipment, and human capital
