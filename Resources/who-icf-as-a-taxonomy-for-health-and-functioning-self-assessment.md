## Summary

The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF), endorsed by all 191 WHO Member States in 2001, provides a biopsychosocial framework for describing and measuring health and disability at both individual and population levels [@kostanjsek2011]. It is the WHO's standard classification for health and health-related domains, complementing the ICD (diseases) and ICHI (interventions).

The ICF organises health information into two main parts. **Part 1: Functioning and Disability** covers (a) Body Functions (b) and Body Structures (s) — physiological and anatomical systems, and (b) Activities and Participation (d) — execution of tasks and involvement in life situations. **Part 2: Contextual Factors** covers (a) Environmental Factors (e) — physical, social, and attitudinal environment, and (b) Personal Factors — demographic and individual characteristics (not yet classified in detail). Each component is further divided into chapters and categories with alphanumeric codes (e.g., b152 for emotional functions, d450 for walking).

The ICF is uniquely suited as a self-assessment taxonomy because it moves beyond a purely medical model to include functional, social, and environmental dimensions. This makes it applicable across clinical, research, and policy settings, including patient-reported outcome measurement. The WHODAS 2.0 assessment tool operationalises ICF domains for self-report.

## Key Points

- Two-part structure: Functioning & Disability (Body Functions/Structures, Activities/Participation) and Contextual Factors (Environmental, Personal)
- Biopsychosocial model integrating medical, individual, and social perspectives on health
- Applicable as a coding framework for self-assessment across all health conditions
- Alphanumeric coding system with over 1,400 categories at varying granularity
- WHODAS 2.0 provides a validated self-report instrument mapped to ICF domains
- Environmental factors (products/technology, natural environment, support/relationships, attitudes, services/systems) are explicitly included
