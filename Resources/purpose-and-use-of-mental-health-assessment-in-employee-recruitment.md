## Summary

Mental health assessment in employee recruitment serves three distinct purposes: predictive, supportive, and risk-mitigating. The predictive purpose involves using validated psychological instruments to forecast how candidates will perform, interact with colleagues, and cope with job-related stressors. Pre-employment psychological evaluations typically measure personality traits (conscientiousness, emotional stability, extraversion), cognitive ability (problem-solving, critical thinking), emotional intelligence, integrity, and job-specific psychological fitness. These assessments complement traditional interviews and resume reviews by providing objective, standardised data on psychological characteristics that predict workplace behaviour.

The supportive purpose involves identifying candidates who may benefit from accommodations, early onboarding support, or targeted adjustments. When administered post-offer, mental health screeners such as the PHQ-9, GAD-7, and K10 allow occupational health professionals to flag candidates who might struggle during the transition to a new role. This enables proactive intervention: connecting employees with Employee Assistance Programs, flexible work arrangements, or mentoring before problems escalate.

The risk-mitigating purpose addresses safety-sensitive roles where psychological fitness directly affects public safety or team wellbeing. For occupations involving law enforcement, emergency services, military, aviation, or high-stress healthcare environments, psychological screening ensures candidates can tolerate the specific demands of the role. Additionally, mental health assessment helps organisations fulfil their duty of care obligations by ensuring the work environment does not pose unreasonable risks to individuals with pre-existing conditions.

## Key Points

- Three purposes: predictive (forecast performance), supportive (enable accommodations), risk-mitigating (ensure safety)
- Predictive assessments measure personality, cognitive ability, emotional intelligence, integrity, job-specific fitness
- Supportive assessments identify candidates needing onboarding adjustments or EAP connection
- Risk-mitigating assessments are critical for safety-sensitive roles (law enforcement, emergency services, healthcare)
- Assessments are typically administered post-offer due to ADA restrictions on pre-offer medical inquiries
- Results inform hiring decisions alongside experience, skills, and interview performance — not as standalone criteria
- Modern approach emphasises job fit and support over exclusion
