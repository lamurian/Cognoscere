## Summary

The evidence supports a three-layer reading: a genuine grassroots core, an opportunistic amplification layer, and a heavily contested "hidden agenda" narrative — but no verified centralized conspiracy behind the federalism wave itself.

**Grassroots core.** The movement has named, local, verifiable actors (Said Lukman, LAMR, district leaders), specific material grievances (oil revenue, fiscal injustice), and a constitutional, non-secessionist framing [@lukman2025; @abadi2025]. This matches the long record of regional grievance politics in Indonesia, from PRRI through Aceh and Papua [@bertrand2019].

**Amplification.** A content-creator economy churns AI-generated "WHAT IF federal" scenario videos for engagement, and hashtags (#indonesiafederal, paired with #riau) aggregate genuine regional sentiment with clickbait [@tiktok2025]. This inflates the apparent scale of the phenomenon.

**Hidden-agenda claims — largely unproven, sometimes themselves orchestrated.** A retired BIN officer alleged (Aug 2025) that "Jokowi supporters" held secret meetings planning a Riau Merdeka proclamation — an unverified claim made on a YouTube podcast [@sariyem2025]. More importantly, the Jakarta Post-led media probe (Tempo, Kompas, Suara, Tribun, Drone Emprit, Internews) found that the claim that the August-September 2025 protests were "foreign-backed" was itself a foreign information manipulation (FIMI) campaign — hundreds of posts, nearly half in foreign languages, blaming Soros and NED, with indications of pro-Russia/pro-China actors [@lai2026]. Meanwhile the Prabowo government has pushed a Disinformation and Foreign Propaganda Bill that analysts warn could formalize "foreign agent" labeling of critics, echoing Prabowo's own June 2025 claim that foreign powers fund NGOs to "sow discord" [@farikhah2026].

**Verdict.** The federalism/separation wave is predominantly genuine regional grievance — especially fiscal disenfranchisement — amplified by the content economy and, in places, instrumentalized by political actors against rivals. Documented foreign interference in the related discourse ran in the *opposite* direction: amplifying the claim that protests were foreign-organized, not organizing the federalism advocacy itself. "Hidden agenda" accusations around federalism remain allegations, while the government's counter-framing carries its own demonstrable manipulation problems [@lai2026; @farikhah2026].

## Key Points

- Genuine grassroots core: named local actors, material grievances, constitutional framing.
- Amplification: AI-generated speculative videos and hashtag aggregation inflate scale.
- No verified foreign or domestic centralized orchestration behind the federalism content.
- The "foreign-backed protests" narrative was itself a FIMI campaign (pro-Russia/China indications).
- Government counter-framing ("foreign agents", Disinformation Bill) risks labeling legitimate critics.
