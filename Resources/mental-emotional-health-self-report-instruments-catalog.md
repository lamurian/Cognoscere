## Summary

Mental and emotional health instruments measure depression, anxiety, stress, affect, and psychological distress. This catalog documents 15 widely validated instruments across clinical and research settings. These instruments map primarily to ICF Mental Functions (chapter b1).

## Instrument Catalog

**1. PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9)**
Authors: Kroenke et al., 2001. 9 items aligned with DSM-5 MDD criteria, 0-3 Likert, 2-week recall. Total 0-27. Cutoffs: 5-9 minimal, 10-14 moderate, 15-19 moderately severe, 20-27 severe. Se=0.88, Sp=0.88 at cutoff >=10. alpha=0.86-0.89. Manual: https://www.phqscreeners.com/

**2. GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7)**
Authors: Spitzer et al., 2006. 7 items, 0-3 Likert, 2-week recall. Total 0-21. Cutoffs: 5-9 mild, 10-14 moderate, 15-21 severe. Se=0.89, Sp=0.82 at cutoff >=10. alpha=0.92. Manual: https://www.phqscreeners.com/

**3. BDI-II (Beck Depression Inventory-II)**
Authors: Beck et al., 1996. 21 items, 0-3 scale. Total 0-63. Cutoffs: 0-13 minimal, 14-19 mild, 20-28 moderate, 29-63 severe. alpha=0.91-0.93. Gold standard in clinical psychology. Manual: https://www.pearsonclinical.com/

**4. BAI (Beck Anxiety Inventory)**
Authors: Beck et al., 1988. 21 items describing anxiety symptoms, 0-3 scale. Total 0-63. Cutoffs: 0-7 minimal, 8-15 mild, 16-25 moderate, 26-63 severe. alpha=0.92. Distinguishes anxiety from depression. Manual: https://www.pearsonclinical.com/

**5. HADS (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale)**
Authors: Zigmond & Snaith, 1983. 14 items (7 anxiety, 7 depression), 0-3 scale. Each subscale 0-21. Cutoffs: 0-7 normal, 8-10 mild, 11-14 moderate, 15-21 severe. alpha=0.71-0.93. Designed for medical settings. Manual: freely available in original publication.

**6. CES-D (Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale)**
Authors: Radloff, 1977. 20 items, 0-3 scale. Total 0-60. Cutoff >=16 indicates clinically significant depression. alpha=0.85-0.91. Widely used in epidemiological research. Manual: freely available in original publication.

**7. DASS-21 (Depression Anxiety Stress Scales)**
Authors: Lovibond & Lovibond, 1995. 21 items (7 per subscale), 0-3 scale. Multiply by 2 for full-scale scoring. Depression: 0-42 (cutoff >=20 severe); Anxiety: 0-42 (cutoff >=15 severe); Stress: 0-42 (cutoff >=26 severe). alpha=0.88-0.94. Manual: https://www2.psy.unsw.edu.au/dass/

**8. K-10 (Kessler Psychological Distress Scale)**
Authors: Kessler et al., 2002. 10 items, 1-5 scale. Total 10-50. Cutoffs: 10-19 likely well, 20-24 mild, 25-29 moderate, 30-50 severe. alpha=0.93. Population mental health screening. Manual: freely available.

**9. PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule)**
Authors: Watson et al., 1988. 20 items (10 positive, 10 negative affect), 1-5 scale. Each subscale 10-50. High PA associated with well-being; high NA with distress. alpha=0.86-0.90. Manual: freely available in original publication.

**10. PSS-10 (Perceived Stress Scale)**
Authors: Cohen et al., 1983. 10 items, 0-4 scale. Total 0-40. Cutoffs: 0-13 low, 14-26 moderate, 27-40 high perceived stress. alpha=0.74-0.91. Measures perceived stress over past month. Manual: freely available.

**11. SCL-90-R (Symptom Checklist-90-Revised)**
Authors: Derogatis, 1977. 90 items, 0-4 scale. 9 primary symptom dimensions plus Global Severity Index. T-scores (mean=50, SD=10). alpha=0.77-0.99. Comprehensive psychiatric screening. Manual: https://www.pearsonclinical.com/

**12. WHO-5 Well-Being Index**
Authors: WHO, 1998. 5 items, 0-5 scale. Raw score 0-25; percentage: raw*4 (0-100). Cutoff <=50 indicates poor well-being; <=28 indicates depression screening positive. alpha=0.84. Non-disease-specific. Manual: https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/research-quality-of-life

**13. PCL-5 (PTSD Checklist for DSM-5)**
Authors: Weathers et al., 2013. 20 items, 0-4 scale. Total 0-80. Cutoff >=31-33 indicates probable PTSD. alpha=0.94. Manual: https://www.ptsd.va.gov/

**14. AUDIT (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test)**
Authors: WHO, 1989. 10 items, 0-4 scale. Total 0-40. Cutoffs: 0-7 low risk, 8-15 hazardous, 16-19 harmful, >=20 probable dependence. alpha=0.82. Manual: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9241592345

**15. MDQ (Mood Disorder Questionnaire)**
Authors: Hirschfeld et al., 2000. 15 items (manic symptoms), 1 yes/no; 1 question on co-occurrence; 1 question on functional impairment. Screening for bipolar disorder. Sensitivity=0.73, Specificity=0.90. Manual: freely available.

## Key Points

- PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are first-line screeners in primary care (mapped to ICF Mental Functions)
- BDI-II and BAI remain gold standards in clinical psychology research
- DASS-21 provides simultaneous depression/anxiety/stress assessment
- WHO-5 and PANAS capture positive aspects of mental health beyond pathology
- PSS-10 bridges mental health and stress measurement across contexts
- AUDIT and MDQ extend coverage to substance use and bipolar screening
