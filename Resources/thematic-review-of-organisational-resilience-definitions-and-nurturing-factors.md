## Summary

Barasa, Mbau, and Gilson (2018) conducted a systematic review of empirical literature on organisational resilience, drawing from both health and other sectors. Thirty-four papers met inclusion criteria from searches across PubMed, Econlit, EBSCOHOST, Google, and Google Scholar. Data were analysed by thematic review.[@barasa2018]

Resilience was generally defined as a system's ability to continue meeting its objectives in the face of challenges, emphasising not just shock resistance but also adaptation and transformation. Ten factors influencing organisational resilience were identified: material resources, preparedness and planning, information management, collateral pathways and redundancy, governance processes, leadership practices, organisational culture, human capital, social networks, and collaboration.[@barasa2018]

**Key conceptual advances.** The review established resilience as an emergent property of complex adaptive systems, distinguishing between planned resilience (preparing for future crises) and adaptive resilience (adapting to chronic stresses and acute shocks). A third category, everyday resilience, addresses routine and chronic stress rather than acute shocks.[@barasa2018]

**Health system software.** The authors argued that health system software (governance, leadership, culture, social networks) is as important as hardware (resources, infrastructure) in nurturing resilience, challenging the predominant focus on material resources.[@barasa2018]

## Key Points

- 34 papers reviewed across health and non-health sectors
- Ten factors influencing organisational resilience identified
- Three resilience types: planned, adaptive, and everyday
- Resilience as emergent property of complex adaptive systems
- Software (governance, culture) as important as hardware (resources)
