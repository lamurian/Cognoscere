## Summary

The Ego-Resiliency Scale (ER89) measures ego-resiliency, a stable personality capacity for flexible adaptation to changing environmental demands, originally formulated by Block in personality theory and operationalised by Block and Kremen (1996) [@block1996]. It contains 14 self-report items on a 4-point Likert scale and is scored as a single total. It does not require adversity exposure; it captures general behavioural flexibility, which makes it conceptually distinct from stress-specific resilience scales [@windle2011].

Internal validity: the original scale is treated as unidimensional, with internal consistency alpha .73-.81 across samples [@farkas2015]. Confirmatory factor analyses of the original one-factor structure show poor fit, which motivated revised structures. Alessandri et al. proposed the two-factor ER89-R (Openness to Life Experiences, Optimal Regulation), while Farkas and Orosz (2015) identified a hierarchical three-component model (ER11, 11 items): Active Engagement with the World (AEW), Repertoire of Problem Solving Strategies (RPSS), and Integrated Performance under Stress (IPS), with good fit (CFI=.965-.970, RMSEA=.055-.058) [@farkas2015].

External validity: the ER89 correlates with Big Five traits (most strongly Openness), subjective well-being, and general self-efficacy, and negatively with trait anxiety and negative affect [@farkas2015]. It shows a moderate positive correlation with IQ but remains conceptually distinct from intelligence [@block1996]. Test-retest correlations were .67 and .51 in two groups [@windle2011], and four-week retest correlations of the ER11 and subscales are high [@farkas2015]. Compared with the CD-RISC, the ER89/ER11 better captures the plasticity/flexibility function of resiliency, while the CD-RISC better captures the stability function [@farkas2015]. In the Windle review the ER89 scored 6/18 with maximum construct validity [@windle2011]. Males score higher on ER11, especially on IPS (d=.386) [@farkas2015].

The ER89 items are published in the original article and impose no license or permission fee; the ER11 items are likewise freely available [@block1996; @farkas2015].

## Key Points

- 14 items, 4-point scale, unidimensional total; alpha .73-.81 [@farkas2015]
- Original one-factor CFA fits poorly; revised ER11 three-component model fits well (CFI .965-.970, RMSEA .055-.058) [@farkas2015]
- ER11 components: Active Engagement with the World, Repertoire of Problem Solving Strategies, Integrated Performance under Stress
- Test-retest: .67 and .51 (two groups); ER11 4-week retest high [@windle2011; @farkas2015]
- Correlates with Openness, well-being, self-efficacy; negative with trait anxiety [@farkas2015]
- Measures flexibility/plasticity; CD-RISC better captures stability under stress [@farkas2015]
- Quality rating 6/18 in Windle et al. (2011), max construct validity [@windle2011]
- Free: items published, no license required; validated for young adults [@block1996]
