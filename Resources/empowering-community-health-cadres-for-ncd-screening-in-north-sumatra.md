## Summary

A community-based participatory program in North Sumatra (Deli Serdang, Simalungun, Karo) empowered 45 Community Health Cadres (kader) for hypertension and diabetes early detection and referral. Over 6 months: structured training (epidemiology, measurement techniques, cultural communication), hands-on practice with digital sphygmomanometers/glucometers, co-designed recording tools and referral pathways. Knowledge scores improved +32.2% (54.5%→86.7%, p<0.001); practical competency +44.7% (44.3%→89.0%, p<0.001); referrals to Puskesmas increased 231.9% (47→156 in 3 months).

## Key Points

### Category: Collaborative
- **Co-design**: Cadres identified local challenges, co-designed training curriculum, schedules, materials, referral tools
- **Cultural adaptation**: Local languages (Batak, Malay, Karo), farming-season schedules, kinship systems (dalihan na tolu)
- **Health system bridging**: Designated Puskesmas liaison officers, standardized referral forms, direct communication channels

### Impacts
- 84.4% of cadres achieved adequate competency (≥75%) vs 8.9% baseline
- Referral surge highest in remote Karo (308.3% increase)
- Community trust increased: cadres report neighbors now seek check-ups
- Model aligns with Posbindu PTM national strategy and Ministry of Health NCD priorities

### Methodology
- **Participatory forums**: 5 village-level forums with cadres, traditional leaders (datuk/raja), Puskesmas, village government
- **Training**: 4 days (epidemiology, risk factors, BP/glucose measurement, communication, recording, cultural engagement)
- **Hands-on practice**: Role-play, simulation with portable devices; individual supervision until competency
- **Tools**: Simple illustrated record books; standardized referral forms with unique IDs; tracking system
- **Evaluation**: Pre/post knowledge (30-item questionnaire), practical competency (checklists), referral records, FGDs

### Points of Improvement
1. **Sustainability**: Cadres request periodic refresher training; skills decay without practice
2. **Equipment**: Only 1 device set per village; queues during screening events
3. **Health worker turnover**: Puskesmas staff changes disrupt supervision continuity
4. **Community awareness**: Low NCD literacy; many feel healthy, don't attend screening
5. **Gender imbalance**: 95.6% female cadres; male engagement strategies needed
6. **Scale-up**: 45 cadres in 5 villages; North Sumatra has 33 regencies; needs facilitator pool
7. **Digital integration**: Paper-based records; mHealth potential with 4G coverage but literacy barriers
8. **Broader NCD scope**: Only hypertension/diabetes; cervical cancer, mental health not included
