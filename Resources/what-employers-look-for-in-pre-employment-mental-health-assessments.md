## Summary

Employers use pre-employment mental health assessments to evaluate six core domains, each tied to specific organisational outcomes. These domains are not used to diagnose mental illness but to assess functional fitness for the role.

**Emotional stability and stress tolerance.** Employers assess whether candidates can manage pressure, maintain composure under stress, and recover from setbacks. This is particularly important for high-demand roles (healthcare, emergency services, customer-facing positions) where emotional regulation directly affects job performance. High emotional stability correlates with lower burnout rates and fewer absenteeism episodes.

**Cognitive ability and problem-solving.** Beyond technical skills, employers evaluate critical thinking, learning agility, and the ability to process complex information. These cognitive capacities predict job performance more reliably than experience alone and indicate whether a candidate can adapt to changing requirements.

**Interpersonal functioning and team compatibility.** Emotional intelligence measures assess a candidate's ability to understand and manage their own emotions, read social cues, and collaborate effectively. Employers look for indicators of empathy, communication style, and conflict resolution capacity to predict how the candidate will integrate into existing team dynamics.

**Integrity and reliability.** Integrity tests predict ethical behaviour, trustworthiness, and likelihood of counterproductive workplace behaviours (theft, absenteeism, workplace deviance). These assessments have meta-analytic support for predicting job performance and reducing organisational risk.

**Coping strategies and resilience.** Employers evaluate how candidates manage adversity, whether they use adaptive coping strategies (problem-focused, help-seeking) versus maladaptive ones (avoidance, substance use). This domain indicates long-term sustainability in the role and susceptibility to burnout.

**General mental health status.** Brief screeners (PHQ-9, GAD-7, K10) identify candidates experiencing active depression, anxiety, or psychological distress that may require accommodations or support. The goal is not exclusion but ensuring appropriate onboarding support and connecting candidates with resources.

## Key Points

- Six domains assessed: emotional stability, cognitive ability, interpersonal functioning, integrity, coping/resilience, general mental health
- Emotional stability: predicts lower burnout and absenteeism in high-demand roles
- Cognitive ability: predicts job performance more reliably than experience alone
- Interpersonal functioning: predicts team integration and collaboration
- Integrity tests: predict counterproductive workplace behaviours (meta-analytic support)
- Coping strategies: indicate long-term sustainability and burnout susceptibility
- General mental health (PHQ-9, GAD-7, K10): identify accommodation needs, not exclusion
- All assessments must be validated, job-related, and administered uniformly per ADA
