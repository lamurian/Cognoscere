## Summary

A dose-response relationship exists between screen time and depressive symptoms in children. A meta-analysis of 16 studies found that higher screen time in preadolescents and adolescents was significantly associated with higher depression risk (OR 1.12; 95% CI 1.03-1.22) @gonzlezprez2025. The relationship follows a non-linear pattern with lowest risk at 1 hour/day (OR 0.88; 95% CI 0.84-0.93) @kar2025.

A critical threshold was identified at 4.5 hours/day, where depressive symptoms increased significantly (β=0.161, P<0.05). The most frequent symptom was anhedonia, which also made children more prone to risky behaviors including early substance use @gonzlezprez2025. Among 14-17 year-olds, high screen users (7+ hours/day vs 1 hour/day) were more than twice as likely to have been diagnosed with depression (RR 2.39) or anxiety (RR 2.26) @kar2025.

A Danish longitudinal study found that each additional hour/day of screen viewing in adolescence was associated with greater depression scores in young adulthood (β=1.36; 95% CI 0.73-1.98) @kar2025. Video game playing (β=0.13) and computer use (β=0.17) were associated with more severe depressive symptoms @kar2025.

## Key Points

- Non-linear dose-response: lowest depression risk at 1 hour/day
- 4.5+ hours/day threshold for clinically significant depressive symptoms
- 7+ hours/day doubles risk of depression diagnosis in adolescents
- Longitudinal evidence shows effects persist into young adulthood
- Anhedonia is most common symptom, associated with risky behaviors
