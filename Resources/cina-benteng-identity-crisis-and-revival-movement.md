## Summary

The Cina Benteng face a unique identity crisis: rejected by mainstream Chinese Indonesians for being 'too local' (no Chinese language, darker skin, assimilated customs) while still perceived as 'too Chinese' by indigenous Indonesians. This liminal position has intensified since Reformasi, as younger generations seek to define what being Benteng Chinese means in modern Indonesia.

The self-deprecating term 'hitachi' (hitam tapi Cina — 'tan but Chinese') captures this tension humorously. Tour guide Elsa Novia Sena describes the experience: 'Some totok Chinese say we have lost our Chinese identity because we do not speak the language and our skin is darker. On the other hand, indigenous Indonesians still see us as too Chinese.' This double marginalization has historically pushed some families toward assimilation and cultural forgetting.

A revival movement emerged in the 2020s, driven by young Cibens reclaiming their heritage. Key initiatives include: Elsa's Benteng Walking Tour through Pasar Lama (operating since 2024), showcasing Boen Tek Bio temple, soya sauce factories, and old shophouses; Merry Andayani's MAGIC project translating ritual motifs into fashion designs; the Cide Kode Benteng Foundation preserving culture; and the Benteng Heritage Museum (established by Udaya Halim) housing artifacts from bound feet shoes to Sam Kok statues.

The revival faces structural challenges: young Cibens increasingly live outside traditional neighborhoods, work in modern sectors, and lack fluency in ancestral languages. The Tangerang city government has designated Pasar Lama as a cultural heritage site (2018) and supports heritage walks, but preservation of crumbling Chinese-architecture buildings in Pecinan remains inadequate.

## Key Points

- Identity crisis: 'too Chinese' to Indonesians, 'too local' to Chinese Indonesians
- 'Hitachi' (hitam tapi Cina): humorous self-description of hybrid identity
- Revival since 2020s: walking tours, fashion projects, heritage museum
- Cide Kode Benteng Foundation: cultural preservation organization
- Benteng Heritage Museum: artifacts including bound feet shoes, opium pipes
- Challenge: youth living outside traditional neighborhoods, losing language
- Pasar Lama designated cultural heritage site (2018)
