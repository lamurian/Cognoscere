## Summary

Test-taker acceptance of proctoring is not merely a matter of user satisfaction — it directly affects the psychometric validity of assessments. The Andreou et al. (2021) study of 472 high-stakes medical exam candidates found two underlying factors driving perceptions of proctoring: appreciation of the proctoring software and emotional distress caused by proctoring [@andreou2021]. Emotional distress from surveillance creates construct-irrelevant variance for instruments measuring anxiety, stress, or mood, confounding the intended measurement.

The Woldeab & Brothen (2026) scale development with 444 students identified three dimensions of the proctoring experience: Exam Quality and System Fairness, Proctoring Anxiety and Concerns, and Preference for and Benefits of Online Proctoring [@woldeab2026]. The second factor — Proctoring Anxiety — includes worries about being wrongly flagged for cheating, insufficient information, and technical failure. These anxieties directly threaten test validity by introducing measurement error unrelated to the construct being assessed.

Denker et al. (2023) found across 2,052 test-takers that three Akzept dimensions (face validity, controllability, absence of strain) explained overall evaluation of scholastic aptitude tests. Face validity was the strongest predictor (gamma = 0.35) [@denker2023]. When test-takers perceive proctoring as unfair or invasive, their acceptance drops, which may reduce motivation, engagement, and consequently the predictive validity of the assessment.

Broader implications of low acceptance include equity concerns: Denker et al. found that ~10% of test-takers in proctored conditions had privacy concerns, and those who chose not to be proctored (UP chosen group) scored significantly lower on divergent thinking in Scherrer's study [@scherrer2025; @denker2023]. If proctoring systematically deters or disadvantages specific groups, it introduces selection bias that undermines assessment fairness.

## Key Points

- Emotional distress from proctoring creates construct-irrelevant variance for mood/anxiety instruments
- Three-factor model (Exam Quality, Proctoring Anxiety, Proctoring Benefits) captures acceptance dimensions
- Face validity is the strongest predictor of overall test evaluation (gamma = 0.35)
- ~10% of test-takers have significant privacy concerns, raising equity flags
