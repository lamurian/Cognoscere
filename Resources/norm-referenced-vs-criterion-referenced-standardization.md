## Summary

Scale standardization can follow two fundamentally different approaches: norm-referenced and criterion-referenced. In norm-referenced standardization (norming), raw scores are transformed into scores that indicate a test taker's position relative to a normative group. This approach answers "how does this individual compare to others?" and produces standard scores, percentiles, or age/grade equivalents (@kyriazos2018).

Criterion-referenced standardization, by contrast, interprets scores against a fixed standard or criterion of performance rather than a norm group. This approach answers "has this individual achieved a specific level of competence?" and is common in educational achievement testing, clinical diagnosis, and competency certification. Cut scores are established through methods such as the Angoff method, bookmark method, or contrasting groups method.

The choice between norm-referenced and criterion-referenced approaches depends on the intended use of the instrument. Norm-referenced approaches are appropriate when relative standing matters (e.g., selection, ranking), while criterion-referenced approaches are appropriate when absolute mastery or threshold detection matters (e.g., screening, diagnosis). Some instruments use both approaches: norm-referenced data establish typical performance while criterion-referenced data establish clinical thresholds.

Standardization samples must be representative of the target population. A common limitation in new scale development is that standardization samples are often convenience samples (typically university students), which limits the generalizability of established norms. Cross-cultural standardization requires additional steps to establish measurement invariance across populations.

## Key Points
- Norm-referenced: scores interpreted relative to a normative group (percentiles, standard scores)
- Criterion-referenced: scores interpreted against a fixed performance standard (cut scores, mastery levels)
- Choice depends on intended use: relative standing vs. absolute achievement
- Standardization samples must represent the target population
- Convenience samples (e.g., university students) limit norm generalizability
- Cross-cultural standardization requires measurement invariance testing
