## Summary

Lau et al. (2021) conducted a systematic review of definitions and associated factors of psychological resilience in palliative patients with advanced cancer. Four databases were searched from inception to August 2020, yielding 15 eligible studies (10 qualitative, 5 quantitative).[@lau2021]

From nine studies that included a definition of psychological resilience, five common themes were derived through thematic synthesis: **buffering** (resilience as protection against negative outcomes), **adaptation** (adjustment to changing circumstances), **resources** (internal and external assets supporting resilience), **recovery** (return to baseline functioning), and **growth** (positive transformation through adversity).[@lau2021]

**Quantitative findings.** Resilience was significantly associated with hope, independence, social support, fatigue, emotional distress, and coping strategies. Higher resilience correlated with lower emotional distress and better quality of life.[@lau2021]

**Qualitative findings.** Additional sources of resilience emerged: spirituality, social support, prior experience dealing with illness and life adversity, meaning-making, reconciling with life's finiteness, acceptance of illness, control, determination, positive attitude, dignity, engagement with palliative care, and quality of life being supported by palliative care.[@lau2021]

**Implications.** The authors called for an overarching definition of psychological resilience in palliative advanced cancer patients that acknowledges contextual sensitivity across cultural groups, and for a holistic range of bio-psycho-socio-spiritual factors to be examined.[@lau2021]

## Key Points

- 15 studies reviewed (10 qualitative, 5 quantitative)
- Five definitional themes: buffering, adaptation, resources, recovery, growth
- Quantitative associations: hope, independence, social support, fatigue, distress
- Qualitative sources: spirituality, meaning-making, acceptance, dignity
- Need for culturally sensitive, bio-psycho-socio-spiritual framework
