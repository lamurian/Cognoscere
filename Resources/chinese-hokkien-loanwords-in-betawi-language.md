## Summary

The Betawi language contains a substantial layer of Hokkien Chinese loanwords, reflecting centuries of Chinese settlement and trade in the Batavia/Jakarta area. The most prominent examples are first-person pronouns: *gue* and *gua* (meaning 'I' or 'me') derive directly from Hokkien 我 (góa). The second-person pronoun *lu* ('you') comes from Hokkien 汝 (lí/lú). These pronouns have spread far beyond Betawi into colloquial Jakarta Indonesian and are used throughout Indonesia's urban centers.

Hokkien also contributed numerical terms: *cepé'* ('a hundred' from 百 pa̍h), *gopé'* ('five hundred' from 五百 gōo-pa̍h), and *secèng* ('a thousand' from 千 chheng). These Hokkien numerals are distinct from standard Indonesian numbers and remain in active use in market and commercial contexts.

The Cina Benteng community in Tangerang represents a particularly significant case: these Chinese descendants have abandoned Chinese languages and now speak a Betawi variant with heavy Hokkien influence. Their speech preserves many Hokkien loanwords that have faded from standard Betawi. The Wiktionary entry for *gua* explicitly notes it is 'Borrowed from Hokkien 我 (góa, "I, me, my")' and lists it as a Betawi term borrowed from Hokkien.

## Key Points

- *gue*/*gua* ('I'): from Hokkien 我 (góa) — first-person pronoun
- *lu* ('you'): from Hokkien 汝 (lí/lú) — second-person pronoun
- *cepé'* ('hundred'), *gopé'* ('five hundred'), *secèng* ('thousand'): Hokkien numerals
- Cina Benteng community in Tangerang preserves Hokkien-influenced Betawi variant
- These Hokkien elements spread into colloquial Jakarta Indonesian nationwide
