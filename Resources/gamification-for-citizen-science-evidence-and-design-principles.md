## Summary

Gamification is the second most empirically validated approach for popularizing citizen science, with demonstrated success across astronomy, ecology, biodiversity, and emerging applications in public health. The evidence base draws from both citizen science case studies and health-specific gamification research.

The existing taxonomy of citizen science popularization identifies gamification as a core strategy alongside low-barrier entry, community building, feedback loops, institutional partnerships, and recognition pathways. Successful examples include Foldit (protein folding game), eBird (gamified birdwatching competitions), and various Zooniverse projects. However, most gamification evidence comes from environmental and astronomy domains rather than public health specifically.

The intersection of gamification and citizen science in public health is an emerging field. Public health citizen science projects that incorporate gamification elements (e.g., symptom tracking apps with badges, community health challenges with leaderboards) are growing but lack the rigorous evaluation available for other domains.

## Key Points

- **Evidence gap**: Most gamification + citizen science evidence comes from non-health domains; health-specific citizen science gamification is under-researched
- **Foldit model**: Game encodes expert knowledge in scoring, allowing non-experts to solve complex problems; applicable to health data classification tasks
- **eBird model**: Gamified competitions drove massive participation increases; analogous health examples could include neighborhood health challenges
- **Dual motivation**: Citizen science requires both intrinsic motivation (scientific curiosity, health concern) and extrinsic reinforcement (gamification elements)
- **Data quality concern**: Gamification may increase participation quantity but not necessarily data quality; reputation systems and expert validation are needed
- **Confidence**: MODERATE for general citizen science gamification; LOW for health-specific citizen science gamification (emerging field)
