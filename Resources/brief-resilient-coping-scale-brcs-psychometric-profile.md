## Summary

The Brief Resilient Coping Scale (BRCS) is a 4-item self-report measure of tendencies to cope with stress in a highly adaptive, resilient manner, developed by Sinclair and Wallston (2004) in chronic illness populations [@sinclair2004]. Items are rated on a 5-point Likert scale and summed, giving a range of 4-20 or a mean-based score. It is the shortest of the license-free resilience measures and is used when participant burden matters.

Internal validity: the BRCS is unidimensional, with adequate internal consistency (Cronbach alpha in the .69-.76 range across development and cross-cultural samples). Development studies used two samples of adults with rheumatoid arthritis (n=90 and n=140) and demonstrated adequate internal consistency and test-retest reliability [@sinclair2004].

External validity: convergent validity was established via predictable correlations with personal coping resources (optimism, self-efficacy), lower helplessness, adaptive pain-coping behaviours, and psychological well-being [@sinclair2004]. The BRCS shows buffering effects: resilient coping moderates the impact of high arthritis-related and non-arthritis-related stressors on depressive symptoms [@sinclair2004]. It is sensitive to change following a cognitive-behavioural intervention, supporting responsiveness for outcome evaluation [@sinclair2004]. The authors recommend it for identifying individuals who may need interventions to build resilient coping skills [@sinclair2004].

Licensing: the BRCS is freely available for research and clinical use, with no permission or license fee.

## Key Points

- 4 items, 5-point scale, total 4-20; very brief (under 2 minutes)
- Unidimensional; adequate internal consistency (alpha ~.69-.76) [@sinclair2004]
- Developed and validated in rheumatoid arthritis samples (n=90; n=140)
- Adequate test-retest reliability [@sinclair2004]
- Convergent: optimism, self-efficacy, adaptive pain coping, well-being; negative with helplessness
- Buffers the effect of high stress on depressive symptoms (moderation) [@sinclair2004]
- Responsive to CBT-based intervention change [@sinclair2004]
- Free: no license or permission required
