## Summary

Analysis of five major citizen science projects reveals a consistent three-phase establishment timeline: Startup (0–2 years), Growth (2–8 years), and Maturity (8+ years). Each phase has distinct characteristics, resource requirements, and strategic priorities. Timeline varies by domain complexity and institutional backing, but phase sequence is consistent.

## Key Points

### Phase 1: Startup (0–2 years)
**Characteristics**:
- Prototype development and pilot testing
- Core team (scientists + developers) builds MVP
- Initial volunteer recruitment from existing communities
- High uncertainty; iterative design based on early feedback

**Evidence from projects**:
- **Galaxy Zoo**: Built in months; launched July 2007; 40M classifications in first 175 days [@wikipediacontributors2026a]
- **Foldit**: Evolved from Rosetta@home (2005); Foldit launched 2008 after 3 years of volunteer feedback [@koepnick2019]
- **eBird**: Launched 2002; US-only initially; global expansion 2010 (8 years) [@sullivan2010]
- **Libraries as Hubs**: Arizona pilot with 6 libraries (2017–2019); co-design workshops identified critical success conditions [@cox2026]

**Key activities**: Task design for low-barrier entry; platform usability testing; initial community building (forums, social media); scientific validation of volunteer data quality.

**Resource needs**: Core development team (2–5 FTE); domain scientist time; modest hosting/platform costs.

### Phase 2: Growth (2–8 years)
**Characteristics**:
- Exponential user growth and data accumulation
- Platform scaling and feature expansion
- Institutional partnerships formalized
- Scientific output accelerates (publications, policy impact)
- Volunteer progression: classifiers → advanced users → community leaders

**Evidence from projects**:
- **Galaxy Zoo**: Versions 2–4 (2009–2012); Zooniverse platform launched 2009; 60 papers by 2017 [@masters2019a]
- **eBird**: Global launch 2010; Yard/Patch competitions; animated migration maps; 350K unique visitors in 2010 [@sullivan2010]
- **Libraries as Hubs**: Statewide mini-grants (2020–2021); 10 expansion libraries; professional development webinars begin [@cox2026]
- **Foldit**: Major scientific breakthroughs (2011–2019); Nature papers; player-designed proteins validated [@koepnick2019]

**Key activities**: Gamification/competition features; mobile apps; regional partnerships; volunteer training pipelines; data quality systems; publication pipeline.

**Resource needs**: Expanded team (5–15 FTE); dedicated community management; partnership development; grant funding for scaling.

### Phase 3: Maturity (8+ years)
**Characteristics**:
- Self-sustaining community with distributed leadership
- Platform becomes infrastructure for multiple projects
- Institutional embedding (government, NGO, university adoption)
- Longitudinal scientific datasets enable novel research
- Governance evolution: volunteers assume quality control, moderation, co-design roles

**Evidence from projects**:
- **Galaxy Zoo**: 15 versions by 2017; JWST integration; 100+ Zooniverse projects; 125M classifications [@masters2019a; @wikipediacontributors2026a]
- **eBird**: 1.9B observations; 1M+ users; 2500+ volunteer reviewers; State Wildlife Action Plan integration; 2B observations projected in quarter of time [@teamebird2024]
- **Libraries as Hubs**: National network (900+ libraries); library-led kit creation; 1,200+ community of practice members; embedded in library infrastructure [@cox2026]
- **iNaturalist**: City Nature Challenge global event; GBIF integration; AI-assisted ID; research-grade data pipeline

**Key activities**: Governance decentralization; API/platform openness; policy integration; succession planning; long-term funding diversification.

**Resource needs**: Core infrastructure team; legal/governance support; endowment or diverse funding; international coordination.

## Practical Implications

- **Expect 2+ years** before measurable scientific output
- **Plan for 5–8 years** to reach self-sustaining community
- **Invest early** in volunteer progression pathways (not just recruitment)
- **Budget for phase transitions**: platform scaling (growth), governance redesign (maturity)
- **Leverage existing communities** to shorten startup (birders for eBird, library patrons for Libraries as Hubs)
