## Summary

Parental mediation strategies significantly moderate screen time effects. Co-viewing (watching together) enables cooperative attention, discussion, and contextual framing. Active mediation (discussion and guidance) promotes critical thinking and media literacy. Restrictive mediation (limiting time and content) reduces exposure but varies in effectiveness based on family warmth @kar2025.

A meta-analysis found that active mediation was related to lower levels of aggression (r=-0.08), sexual behavior (r=-0.06), and substance use (r=-0.11). Restrictive mediation showed a small but significant negative relationship with pathological internet use (r=-0.06) @kar2025. The effectiveness of restrictive mediation was stronger when family attachment, communication, and comfort were high @kar2025.

A French cohort study found that children with no screen exposure had lower cognition scores than those with controlled exposure (<1 hour/day) and interactive parental involvement. Parent-child reading serves as a protective factor, mitigating adverse effects of screen time on brain network integration @souza2025. The AAP recommends creating a Family Media Plan with screen-free zones and screen-free times @souza2025.

## Key Points

- Active mediation most effective for reducing negative outcomes
- Co-viewing enhances learning from educational content
- Family warmth moderates effectiveness of restrictive mediation
- Complete screen deprivation may be suboptimal for development
- Screen-free zones (bedroom, meals) are evidence-based strategies
- Parent-child reading mitigates screen time effects on brain development
