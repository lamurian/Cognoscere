## Summary

Research evidence converges on a hardened pragmatist resolution to the personality debate. Pure neutrality is a fiction: every assistant has a voice, so refusing to design it only makes it accidental [@peter2025]. Pure personality is a hazard: human-like design inflates trust, deflates accountability [@oldemburgodemello2026], and enables dependence harms in vulnerable users [@laestadius2022]. The defensible position is disclosed, user-controlled design.

The recommended resolution has five parts. Default: neutral, plain, courteous voice, explicitly disclosed as machine-generated, satisfying EU AI Act Article 50 [@futureoflifeinstitute2024]. Optional: user-selected styles (concise, warm, factual) with warmth behind explicit opt-in. High-stakes domains: factual mode by default [@kummer2026]. Prohibited: simulated emotional states, personal history claims, dependency on the user, manufactured intimacy escalation, and attachment-optimized defaults, especially for minors. Required: auditable persona documentation, boundaries and failure modes.

This design satisfies all five evaluation criteria. It preserves user benefit where warmth is genuinely useful, makes trust transparent rather than engineered, reduces safety risk through bounded personas, is feasible with current technology, and keeps accountability with the vendor through disclosure and documentation.

## Key Points

- Neutrality is a fiction; the real divide is disclosed, user-controlled design versus unexamined, vendor-imposed design.
- Trust gains [@kummer2026] and accountability costs [@oldemburgodemello2026] are separable and should be governed separately.
- Legal disclosure [@futureoflifeinstitute2024] and dependency harms [@laestadius2022] set the floor: no attachment-optimized defaults.
