## Summary

The Dispositional Resilience Scale-15 (DRS-15) is a 15-item self-report measure of psychological hardiness, defined as a personality style associated with stress resilience, derived from the 45-item DRS (Bartone, 1989) [@bartone2007]. It retains the three-factor structure of hardiness: Commitment (engagement and sense of purpose), Control (belief in personal influence over events), and Challenge (viewing change as opportunity), with five items per factor. It is used mainly in military, first-responder, and occupational stress research.

Internal validity: the three-factor structure is supported, but the Challenge subscale has consistently lower reliability. In the 15-item version the Challenge subscale alpha is .70, while in the 30-item version it drops to .32, which led the developer to recommend using the full scale rather than subscales [@windle2011]. Overall internal consistency of the DRS-15 is acceptable [@bartone2007].

External validity: three-week test-retest reliability of the total DRS-15 was r=.78 in a sample of 104 military academy cadets [@bartone2007]; the Windle review reports subscale retest correlations of .78 (Commitment), .58 (Control), and .81 (Challenge) [@windle2011]. Hardiness has demonstrated predictive validity for health and performance under stress in military studies, but the Windle et al. (2011) review rated construct validity evidence as only partial, and the DRS-15 overall received 4/18 — lower than the BRS, RSA, RS, and ER89 [@windle2011]. Conceptually, hardiness is a fixed personality trait, so the DRS-15 fits less well with resilience as a dynamic process [@windle2011].

Licensing: the DRS-15 is freely available for research and educational use from the developer, without a formal license or permission fee.

## Key Points

- 15 items, 3 factors: Commitment, Control, Challenge (5 items each)
- Challenge subscale alpha .70 (15-item) vs .32 (30-item version); use total score [@windle2011]
- 3-week test-retest r=.78 total (n=104 cadets) [@bartone2007]
- Subscale retest: Commitment .78, Control .58, Challenge .81 [@windle2011]
- Hardiness trait focus: less aligned with resilience-as-process definitions [@windle2011]
- Quality rating 4/18 in Windle et al. (2011) — lower than BRS, RSA, RS-14, ER89 [@windle2011]
- Free for research/educational use, no license fee
