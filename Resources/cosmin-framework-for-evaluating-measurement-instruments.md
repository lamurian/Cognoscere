## Summary

COSMIN (COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments) is an international initiative that developed a taxonomy and methodology for evaluating measurement properties of patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). Established through Delphi consensus studies, COSMIN standardized the terminology and definitions of measurement properties that had previously been confused and inconsistently used across the field (@mokkink2016, @terwee2018).

The COSMIN taxonomy distinguishes three domains of measurement properties: (1) reliability (internal consistency, reliability, measurement error), (2) validity (content validity, structural validity, hypothesis testing, cross-cultural validity, criterion validity), and (3) responsiveness. Content validity is considered the most important and most challenging property to assess. COSMIN developed a risk of bias tool specifically for evaluating the methodological quality of studies on reliability and measurement error.

The COSMIN guideline for systematic reviews provides a step-by-step approach for conducting reviews of PROMs. Unlike traditional systematic reviews of interventions, PROM reviews must address multiple measurement properties simultaneously. The COSMIN reporting guideline (@gagnier2021) standardizes how studies on measurement properties should be reported, improving transparency and comparability across studies.

COSMIN is now the de facto standard in healthcare measurement, widely used in systematic reviews to select the most suitable PROM for clinical or research applications. Its methodology has been applied across diverse clinical domains including musculoskeletal conditions, mental health, and chronic disease.

## Key Points

- COSMIN provides consensus-based standards for evaluating PROMs (@mokkink2016)
- Three domains: reliability, validity, and responsiveness
- Content validity identified as the most important and challenging property (@terwee2018)
- Risk of bias tool developed for methodological quality assessment
- COSMIN reporting guideline standardizes study reporting (@gagnier2021)
- De facto standard in healthcare measurement instrument evaluation
