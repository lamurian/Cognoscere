## Summary

The Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) is a 7-item self-report instrument for screening generalized anxiety disorder and assessing severity. Items assess nervousness, inability to control worrying, over-worrying, trouble relaxing, restlessness, irritability, and fear of something awful happening. Each item is rated 0-3 over a two-week recall (total 0-21). Severity thresholds: 5-9 mild, 10-14 moderate, 15-21 severe anxiety.

Spitzer et al. (2006) validated the GAD-7 in 2,740 primary care patients across 15 US clinics, finding sensitivity 0.89 and specificity 0.82 at cutoff >=10 using a mental health professional telephone interview as criterion standard. Systematic review evidence from Martin-Key et al. (2022) reports sensitivity 0.75-0.87 and specificity 0.55-0.78 across settings.

In recruitment contexts, the GAD-7 follows the same legal framework as the PHQ-9: it may be administered after a conditional job offer as part of a uniform post-offer pre-placement assessment. The tool helps identify candidates with anxiety symptoms that may require workplace accommodations. A positive screen (>=10) should prompt a follow-up clinical interview rather than serve as an exclusion criterion. Anxiety disorders are among the most common mental health conditions in working-age adults, and many individuals with managed anxiety perform effectively in demanding roles.

## Key Points

- 7 items, 0-3 Likert scale, 2-week recall, total 0-21
- Validation: Se=0.89, Sp=0.82 at cutoff >=10 (Spitzer 2006)
- Systematic review ranges: Se 0.75-0.87, Sp 0.55-0.78 (Martin-Key 2022)
- Two-factor structure (somatic and cognitive-affective anxiety) confirmed
- In recruitment: post-offer only; uniform administration required
- Positive screen (>=10) requires clinical follow-up, not exclusion
