## Summary

A qualitative study by Surjaningrum et al. (2018) in Surabaya, Indonesia examined the feasibility of task-sharing for perinatal depression detection through community health workers (kader). The study mapped existing referral pathways from CHWs to Puskesmas: CHWs refer cases verbally or via written reports to village midwives, who then refer to Puskesmas midwives, who refer to counsellors or psychologists at the health centre. External referrals from Puskesmas to district hospitals require a doctor's letter [@surjaningrum2018].

Key findings relevant to the proposed project: (1) Only 20% of Puskesmas currently provide mental health services despite policy mandates. (2) CHWs already conduct home visits for maternal health monitoring and could extend this to mental health. (3) Stakeholders identified the need for practical referral guidelines, standardized screening tools, and clear pathways. (4) A specialist recommended against using the word 'depression' to avoid stigma, suggesting 'mood changes' instead. (5) The BPJS capitation fund can be allocated for mental health screening tools and training [@surjaningrum2018].

Other evidence confirms severe referral barriers: up to 90% of referrals to offsite practitioners never result in an appointment, GPs sometimes refuse to provide referral letters for mental health complaints, and patients may travel 60+ km to reach psychiatric units that only exist in large hospitals [@munira2023]. This creates a strong case for automated referral ticket printing that provides clear, standardized referral information and reduces reliance on GP discretion.

The proposed thermal printer referral ticket directly addresses a documented systemic failure: patients being unable to access referral letters. A printed, standardized referral ticket from a self-service kiosk could bypass GP gatekeeping and provide patients with actionable referral information to Puskesmas.

## Key Points
- Surjaningrum et al. (2018): existing CHW-to-Puskesmas referral pathways documented in Surabaya
- Only 20% of Puskesmas provide mental health services
- Up to 90% of referrals to offsite practitioners never result in an appointment
- GP refusal to provide referral letters is a documented systemic barrier
- Automated referral ticket printing could bypass GP gatekeeping
- BPJS capitation fund can finance mental health screening
- Stigma reduction: avoid diagnostic labels, use culturally adapted language
