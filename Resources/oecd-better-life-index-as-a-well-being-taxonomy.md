## Summary

The OECD Better Life Index, launched in 2011, operationalises a multidimensional well-being framework covering 11 dimensions of current well-being and 4 types of resources for future well-being [@oecd2020]. It is designed to supplement economic indicators (GDP) with direct measures of people's living conditions and quality of life.

The **11 current well-being dimensions** are: Housing (housing conditions, affordability); Income and Wealth (household income, financial assets); Jobs and Earnings (employment, job security, earnings); Social Connections (social support networks, relationship quality); Education and Skills (educational attainment, cognitive skills); Environmental Quality (air and water quality, noise pollution); Civic Engagement and Governance (trust in institutions, voter turnout); Health Status (life expectancy, self-reported health); Life Satisfaction (subjective well-being evaluation); Safety (assault rates, feeling safe walking at night); and Work-Life Balance (time off, commuting time).

Beyond current well-being, the framework tracks **resources for future well-being** across four capitals: Natural Capital, Economic Capital, Human Capital, and Social Capital. This sustainability lens ensures well-being measurement extends beyond present conditions to intergenerational equity. Each dimension uses 1-3 headline indicators drawn from over 80 supporting metrics.

## Key Points

- 11 current well-being dimensions covering both material conditions and quality of life
- 4 capital resources for future well-being sustainability
- Interactive Better Life Index allows individuals to weight dimensions by personal preference
- Data covers OECD countries plus partner nations
- Dimensions span objective (income, housing) and subjective (life satisfaction) indicators
- Framework explicitly recognises that well-being is multidimensional — no single indicator suffices
- Each dimension measured by 1-3 headline indicators, aggregated from 80+ supporting metrics [@oecd2020]
