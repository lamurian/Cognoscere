## Summary

Screen time impacts vary significantly by developmental stage. For infants under 18 months, screen time should be avoided except video chatting, as brain development requires sensory-rich interactive play. For 18-24 months, only high-quality content with caregiver co-viewing is recommended. For children 2-5 years, limit to 1 hour/day of high-quality content with active adult involvement. For children 6+ and teens, create consistent limits prioritizing physical activity, sleep, and interpersonal interaction @kar2025.

Preschoolers (2-5 years) show the strongest evidence for developmental impacts: screen time exceeding recommendations is associated with lower white matter integrity, reduced executive function, and language delays @kar2025. A Japanese cohort found that screen use exceeding 4 hours/day before age 1 increased communication delay risk by 4.7 times @souza2025.

School-age children (6-12 years) show elevated cardiometabolic risk at 3+ hours/day and cortical thinning at 7+ hours/day. Adolescents (13-18 years) face unique risks from social media including body image issues, cyberbullying, and FOMO, with evidence of effects persisting into young adulthood @kar2025.

## Key Points

- Under 18 months: 0 hours (except video chatting)
- 18-24 months: limited with co-viewing of high-quality content
- Ages 2-5: 1 hour/day maximum with adult involvement
- Ages 6-12: no fixed limit, but 3+ hours/day raises cardiometabolic risk
- Ages 13-18: social media-specific risks; effects persist into adulthood
