## Summary

A growing body of research examines whether proctoring actually improves the validity of digital psychometric assessment. The most comprehensive meta-analysis to date (Steger et al., 2018, 49 studies) found that unproctored assessments yield slightly higher scores than proctored ones (d = 0.20), with online searchability of test content as the only significant moderator [@scherrer2025]. This suggests cheating is the primary driver of score inflation in unproctored settings, not construct-irrelevant variance from surveillance.

Scherrer et al. (2025) conducted an empirical study testing webcam-based proctoring with a multidimensional intelligence test (reasoning, short-term memory, processing speed, divergent thinking) in a low-stakes research context. They found scalar measurement invariance across proctored and unproctored conditions for reasoning, processing speed, and divergent thinking, but not for short-term memory [@scherrer2025]. Memory tasks showed significant intercept differences, indicating that cheating through note-taking or photographing the screen fundamentally changed what the test measured. Proctoring had no significant main effect on test performance but showed a significant interaction with test type.

Crucially, only 4% of participants showed observable cheating in the proctored condition, and these were primarily memory-oriented strategies (note-taking, screen photography). This low base rate aligns with prior intelligence testing research [@scherrer2025]. However, the measurement non-invariance for memory suggests even low levels of cheating can invalidate comparisons when they target specific item types.

## Key Points

- Meta-analytic evidence shows unproctored scores are slightly inflated (d = 0.20), driven by online searchability
- Scalar measurement invariance holds for reasoning, processing speed, and divergent thinking across conditions
- Memory tasks fail scalar invariance due to note-taking-based cheating
- Low observed cheating rates (4%) can still disrupt measurement equivalence for cheatable task types
