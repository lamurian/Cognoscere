## Summary

Language development is particularly vulnerable to excessive screen time in early childhood. A meta-analysis of 42 studies found that greater quantity of screen use was negatively associated with child language (r=-0.14; 95% CI -0.18 to -0.10), while better quality of screen use (educational programs and co-viewing) was positively associated with language skills @kar2025.

Children who watched TV without parental interaction had an 8.47-fold increase in risk of language acquisition delays @souza2025. Even when following WHO recommendations of 1 hour/day at 36 months, children experienced a reduction of 397 words spoken by adults, 294 vocalizations, and 68 turn-taking exchanges @souza2025. Later age at screen use onset was associated with stronger child language skills (r=0.17; 95% CI 0.07-0.27) @kar2025.

A Japanese cohort found that screen use exceeding 4 hours/day before age 1 increased communication delay risk by 4.7 times (OR 4.78), with delays persisting until age 4 (OR 2.68) @souza2025. Each additional hour beyond recommended screen time was associated with poorer child communication skills in children aged 0-60 months @souza2025.

## Key Points

- Quantity of screen use negatively correlates with language (r=-0.14)
- Quality use (educational, co-viewing) positively correlates with language
- 8.47x increased language delay risk without parental interaction
- Even 1 hour/day reduces parent-child verbal interaction significantly
- Later screen onset associated with stronger language skills
