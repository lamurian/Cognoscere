## Summary

Cross-cutting analysis of 7 Indonesian citizen health research initiatives reveals systemic challenges and improvement priorities. Projects span contributory (Posyandu, Jumantik, PMI CBS, PeduliLindungi/LaporCOVID), collaborative (Desa Siaga TB, North Sumatra NCD, Samarinda PAR), and co-created (LaporCOVID-19 advocacy) typologies. Common barriers: fragmented digital systems, volunteer incentive inequity, training gaps, weak community-to-facility reporting procedures, and sustainability dependence on external funding.

## Key Points

### 1. Digital Fragmentation and Interoperability
- **Multiple parallel apps**: SatuSBM (PMI), PeduliLindungi/SatuSehat (MoH), SP4N-LAPOR! (KemenPANRB), SOBAT TB, e-SMILE, EWARS, SITB
- **Health worker burden**: Double/triple data entry across non-integrated systems (Sitorukmi et al. 2025)
- **Community confusion**: Cadres use paper fallback when apps unfamiliar (SOBAT TB, SatuSBM)
- **Improvement**: National CBS data exchange standard; API-first architecture; single sign-on for cadres

### 2. Volunteer Incentives and Professionalization
- **Current**: IDR 300,000-450,000/year (USD 19-28) vs. minimum wage IDR 2,400,000/month
- **Inequity**: Village vs. Puskesmas vs. NGO incentives differ; performance-linked rare
- **WHO recommendation**: Formal training, accreditation, remuneration (cited in Sitorukmi et al. 2025)
- **Improvement**: Standardized incentive framework; career pathway for kader; integrate into village civil service

### 3. Training Quality and Digital Literacy
- **Gaps**: 57.8% cadres never trained on NCDs (North Sumatra); most kader non-health background
- **Digital literacy barrier**: Elderly cadres prefer paper; apps designed without user-centered design
- **Improvement**: Competency-based certification; regular refresher; co-design apps with cadres; hybrid paper-digital

### 4. Community-to-Facility Reporting Procedures
- **Critical gap**: No formal SOPs for VPDs, leptospirosis, AFP reporting from community (Sitorukmi et al. 2025)
- **Systematized only for**: Dengue (larval indices), TB (SOBAT TB), COVID-19 (WhatsApp groups)
- **Improvement**: Simple case definitions + reporting guidelines for all priority diseases; adapt to paper/digital

### 5. Sustainability and Local Financing
- **External dependence**: PMI CBS (USAID/DFAT), North Sumatra NCD (university/CSO), LaporCOVID-19 (donors)
- **Village Fund (Dana Desa) potential**: TB Alert Villages model; underutilized for health
- **Improvement**: Mandate health allocation in Village Fund; CSR partnerships (palm oil, mining in Sumatra)

### 6. Equity and Inclusion
- **Digital divide**: Smartphone apps exclude elderly, poor, remote, low-literacy populations
- **Gender**: 95%+ female cadres; male engagement absent
- **Geographic**: Urban Java better covered; Eastern Indonesia, remote highlands underserved
- **Improvement**: SMS/USSD/voice options; male cadre recruitment; targeted Eastern Indonesia investment

### 7. Monitoring and Evaluation of CBS Itself
- **Missing**: Acceptability, simplicity, flexibility metrics for community reporting systems
- **Current**: Validity checked (dengue cases verified); participation rates not monitored
- **Improvement**: Routine CBS system evaluation (simplicity, acceptability, flexibility, timeliness)

### 8. Policy and Regulatory Coherence
- **Progress**: Permenko No. 7/2022 legal framework for CBS
- **Gap**: Inconsistent subnational regulations; decentralized governance hinders standardization
- **Improvement**: National CBS technical guide implementation monitoring; harmonize village/district/province rules
