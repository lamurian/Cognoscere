## Summary

No gold-standard resilience measure exists. Windle et al. (2011) rated 15 adult measures against quality criteria and found the Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC), the Resilience Scale for Adults (RSA), and the Brief Resilience Scale (BRS) to be the strongest, while noting all measures still require further validation [@windle2011]. The CD-RISC is the only top-rated instrument that requires written permission from the copyright holder, which excludes it from the license-free set [@windle2011; @connor2003].

License-free instruments and their profile: BRS (6 items, one factor, alpha .80-.91, retest ICC .62-.69, free) [@smith2008; @chmitorz2018]; RS-14 (14 items, one factor, alpha .82-.96, retest r=.88, free for research with permission) [@surzykiewicz2019]; RSA (33 items, six factors, alpha total .88-.90, retest .73-.84, free) [@friborg2003; @morote2017]; DRS-15 (15 items, three hardiness factors, retest r=.78, free) [@bartone2007]; ER89 (14 items, alpha .73-.81, free) [@block1996; @farkas2015]; BRCS (4 items, alpha ~.69-.76, free) [@sinclair2004].

Recommendations for general adult populations:
1. Screening and outcome measurement of bounce-back ability: BRS. It is brief (2-3 minutes), unidimensional, validated in large general population samples, free, and rated among the top three instruments [@chmitorz2018; @windle2011].
2. Comprehensive assessment of protective factors (intrapersonal + family + social): RSA. It is the only top-rated measure covering interpersonal resources, has the best test-retest reliability of the top instruments, and predicts anxiety/depression well, at the cost of 33 items [@morote2017; @windle2011].
3. Trait resilience with published norms: RS-14. One factor, widely translated, with explicit low/moderate/high bands (65/81) and free research licensing [@surzykiewicz2019].
4. Specific needs: DRS-15 for hardiness in high-stress occupations; ER89 for adaptive flexibility rather than adversity recovery; BRCS for very brief coping-resilience screening in clinical or chronic-illness settings [@windle2011].

Practical guidance: choose BRS when recovery speed is the construct of interest; choose RSA or RS-14 when intervention profiling requires knowing which protective resources are present or missing. Combining a brief measure (BRS) with a multidimensional one (RSA) gives both economy and breadth. All six instruments are suitable for non-clinical general adult samples and require no paid license; RS-14 requires research-use permission from the developer.

## Key Points

- No gold standard; CD-RISC (permission required) is excluded from license-free set [@windle2011]
- BRS: best brief screening/outcome measure; alpha .80-.91; free [@chmitorz2018]
- RSA: most comprehensive; six factors incl. family/social; best test-retest; free [@morote2017]
- RS-14: trait resilience with norms (bands 65/81); free for research with permission [@surzykiewicz2019]
- DRS-15: hardiness trait; free; lower psychometric rating (4/18) [@bartone2007; @windle2011]
- ER89: adaptive flexibility; free; weaker one-factor fit, ER11 revised model [@farkas2015]
- BRCS: 4-item coping resilience; free; useful in clinical/chronic populations [@sinclair2004]
- General adult recommendation: BRS (screening) + RSA (comprehensive) covers most needs [@windle2011]
