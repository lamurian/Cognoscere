## Summary

Posyandu (Integrated Health Posts) are the backbone of Indonesia's community health system since the 1980s. Over 173,000 active Posyandu staffed by 500,000+ kader (community health volunteers, mostly women) serve ~100 children under-five each. Kader conduct monthly sessions across five tables: registration, weighing, recording, counseling, and health services (immunization, family planning, nutrition). They also conduct follow-up visits, update data, and report to Puskesmas.

## Key Points

### Category: Contributory (with collaborative elements)
- **Primary role**: Data collection and service delivery per Ministry of Health guidelines
- **Citizen involvement**: Volunteers implement standardized protocols; limited input into design
- **Evolution**: Some kader now participate in co-designing NCD screening (Posbindu PTM) and referral pathways

### Impacts
- 87% basic immunization coverage (2014 MoH data)
- 81% of under-five children weighed regularly
- Contributed to declines in maternal/child mortality and increased life expectancy
- During COVID-19: kader supported contact tracing, health promotion, vaccination outreach

### Methodology
- **Recruitment**: Appointed by village committee (PKK), accountable to village not health system
- **Training**: <1 week formal + on-the-job mentoring by senior kader
- **Supervision**: Technical guidance from Puskesmas; real accountability to village
- **Incentives**: Voluntary; small transport reimbursements (IDR 300,000-450,000/year ~USD 19-28)
- **Data flow**: Paper-based monthly reports to Puskesmas; some digital pilots (e-SMILE, SITB)

### Points of Improvement
1. **Training gaps**: 57.8% of kader in North Sumatra never received NCD training (Sasmito et al. 2026)
2. **Incentive inequity**: No standardized, performance-linked compensation
3. **Workload**: Aging cadres juggle multiple unpaid roles (MCH, TB, NCD, environmental health)
4. **Digital literacy**: Low familiarity with apps (SOBAT TB, SatuSBM) leads to paper fallback
5. **System integration**: Fragmented reporting systems (SP2TP, SITB, EWARS, program-specific)
6. **Professional recognition**: Not formally part of health system; credibility gap with community
