## Summary

A landmark cross-national study establishes the first systematic quantitative link between democratic institutions and social acceptance of LGBTI people [@flores2023]. Using the LGBTI Global Acceptance Index (GAI) alongside Varieties of Democracy (V-Dem) indicators across 175 countries, the research finds a strong positive correlation between liberal democracy and LGBTI acceptance (r=0.67), as well as between free and fair elections and acceptance (r=0.64).

The relationship appears bidirectional. Fundamental aspects of liberal democracy -- rule of law, judicial independence, civil liberties, media independence, and minority rights -- may be necessary for sustained LGBTI acceptance. Conversely, greater acceptance of sexual and gender minorities may itself reinforce democratic values and practices. Previous research notes that while LGBTI rights may not exist in all democracies, there are no cases of strong LGBTI acceptance in non-democracies.

However, the relationship is complex and context-dependent. Using generalized Kernel Regularized Least Squares (gKRLS) models -- a machine learning approach that relaxes linearity assumptions -- the study reveals substantial heterogeneity across countries. In some cases, democratic backsliding preceded or followed reductions in LGBTI acceptance. Country case studies of Indonesia, Brazil, Poland, and Ghana demonstrate that anti-LGBTI rhetoric and policymaking can be both a precursor to and a consequence of democratic backsliding.

In Indonesia, democratic reforms initially enabled both LGBT activism and conservative religious opposition. As democratic institutions weakened after 2014, anti-LGBT rhetoric escalated and legal protections eroded. In Brazil, a decline in LGBTI acceptance may have preceded democratic backsliding, which Bolsonaro then weaponized in a populist electoral strategy. The study concludes that attacks on LGBTI people can signal broader democratic erosion, as targeting minorities violates liberal democratic norms of protecting minority rights.

## Key Points

- Liberal Democracy Index and GAI show strong positive correlation (r=0.67)
- GDP per capita and urbanization also positively correlate with LGBTI acceptance
- Relationship is bidirectional: democracy enables acceptance, acceptance embodies democratic values
- gKRLS models reveal non-linear, context-dependent dynamics across countries
- Anti-LGBTI rhetoric can be a precursor to broader democratic backsliding
- Strong democratic institutions (civil society, independent judiciary) help moderate anti-LGBTI backlash
