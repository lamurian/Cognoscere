## Summary

China has systematically expanded its influence within multilateral institutions — the UN, World Bank, AIIB, and others — as part of a deliberate strategy to reshape international norms. The CSIS analysis documents how Beijing positions itself as the leading advocate for the Global South, articulating a doctrine: "Big powers are the key, China's periphery the priority, developing countries the foundation, and multilateral platforms the stage" (@runde2024).

China's institutional penetration produces tangible diplomatic outcomes. During the March 2022 UN General Assembly vote on the Ukraine resolution, 17 African nations abstained and 8 declined participation — reflecting economic and political ties with both Beijing and Moscow. On human rights votes regarding Xinjiang, most African nations aligned with China. On Taiwan, nearly all Global South countries adopt Beijing's preferred "One China principle" language. Only Eswatini among African nations recognizes Taiwan.

China has established 53 embassies in Africa — three more than the United States — and maintained a 33-year tradition of foreign ministers visiting Africa first each year. Xi Jinping personally visited Africa 10 times between 2014 and 2020. These sustained engagement patterns contrast with inconsistent U.S. attention. The 2022 NDU Strategic Assessment notes that the U.S. faces a "triple challenge" in multilateral arenas: competing with China and Russia while simultaneously maintaining institutional relevance (@lynchiii2025).

RAND research highlights that great-power competition increasingly plays out in secondary theaters — Latin America, Africa, the Middle East — where China and Russia compete for influence through arms sales, infrastructure investment, and diplomatic support. The 2025 DoD China report notes Russia-Ukraine war demonstrated how "mixed competition" based on comprehensive national power is more prominent than before, requiring greater coordination between military and civilian instruments.

## Key Points

- China doctrine: "Big powers key, periphery priority, developing countries foundation, multilateral platforms stage"
- 17 African nations abstained on Ukraine UN vote, reflecting China-Russia economic ties
- China has 53 African embassies vs. 50 U.S. embassies; 33-year tradition of Africa-first diplomacy
- Great-power competition increasingly plays out in secondary theaters (Africa, Latin America, Middle East)
- "Mixed competition" — comprehensive national power — now dominates over purely military rivalry
