## Summary
The Thespian Agent Architecture is a multi-agent framework designed to simulate emotion-focused coping strategies, particularly cognitive reappraisal and depression dynamics. It represents individuals as decision-theoretic agents that evaluate and select coping strategies based on expected emotional outcomes. The architecture operationalizes the Ironic Processes Theory of depression, modeling how attempts to suppress unwanted thoughts can lead to their amplification.

This simulation focuses on the emotional regulation process, examining how individuals might use cognitive reappraisal—reinterpreting a stressful situation—to manage negative emotions. The agents are equipped with decision-making mechanisms that weigh different coping strategies based on their anticipated effects on emotional states.

## Key Points
- Utilizes a multi-agent framework to simulate emotion-focused coping strategies.
- Based on the Ironic Processes Theory of depression.
- Models cognitive reappraisal as a decision-making process within agents.
- Provides detailed simulation of emotional regulation and coping decisions.
- Grounded in psychological theory but may not capture broader resilience factors like social support or homeostatic regulation.
