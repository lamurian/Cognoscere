## Summary

Screen time affects brain structure and function, particularly during critical developmental windows. MRI studies found that children with more than 7 hours/day of screen time showed measurable thinning of the brain's cortex, the region responsible for critical thinking and reasoning @souza2025. For 1-year-olds, exposure to more than 4 hours/day was associated with developmental delays at ages 2 and 4 @takahashi2023.

A study of preschoolers found that screen use greater than recommended was associated with lower measures of microstructural organization and myelination of brain white matter tracts supporting language and emergent literacy skills @kar2025. Prolonged screen exposure reduced frontostriatal activity, predicting delay in inhibitory control development, and negatively modulated frontostriatal connectivity, amplifying reward sensitivity @souza2025.

An EEG study of 437 children found that using screens for more than 4 hours/day at 12 months increased theta wave activity and theta/beta ratio in frontoparietal regions at age 9, suggesting impaired attention and working memory @souza2025. However, interactive screen use with tactile feedback showed different neural activation patterns than passive viewing @souza2025.

## Key Points

- 7+ hours/day associated with cortical thinning in brain regions for critical thinking
- 4+ hours/day at age 1 predicts developmental delays at ages 2-4
- Screen time reduces frontostriatal activity, impairing inhibitory control
- Fast-paced content disrupts executive function more than slow content
- Interactive use shows different (sometimes beneficial) neural patterns
