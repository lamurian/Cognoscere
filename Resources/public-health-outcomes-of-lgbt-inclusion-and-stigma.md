## Summary

The relationship between LGBT inclusion and public health outcomes is well-documented through quantitative evidence. Research demonstrates that structural stigma -- including discriminatory laws and social rejection -- directly harms the physical and mental health of LGBT people, with measurable economic consequences.

At the micro level, Badgett et al. (2014) document multiple health-related mechanisms through which exclusion reduces economic output: LGBT people face barriers to physical and mental health care, which reduces their ability to work and their workplace productivity. Discrimination in healthcare settings leads to avoidance of preventive care, delayed treatment, and poorer health outcomes. The World Bank estimates that the economic cost of LGBT stigma and exclusion includes significant productivity losses from poor health.

The OECD (2024) analysis quantifies the economic benefits of LGBTI+ equality in the United States, finding that inclusive policies improve health outcomes, which in turn increases labor productivity and reduces healthcare costs. States with inclusive non-discrimination policies show better mental health outcomes for LGBT populations, lower rates of HIV stigma, and improved healthcare utilization.

Country-level data shows that nations with stronger legal protections for LGBT people have higher overall life expectancy and lower rates of suicide, though causality is complex given the correlation with broader development factors. The Human Development Index -- which includes life expectancy -- correlates positively with LGBT rights scores [@badgett2019]. Flores et al. (2023) find that countries with higher LGBTI acceptance tend to have higher GDP per capita, which is itself a strong predictor of population health outcomes.

Conversely, the public health costs of exclusion are substantial. In Indonesia, police raids on LGBT gatherings including public health centers impeded HIV outreach to vulnerable communities [@flores2023]. In Brazil, the Bolsonaro administration dismantled LGBT health protections and redefined family policy in conservative terms, directly affecting access to care.

## Key Points

- Structural stigma causes measurable health harms through discrimination in healthcare, violence, and stress
- Inclusive non-discrimination policies improve health outcomes and healthcare utilization
- Health improvements from inclusion translate to economic gains through increased productivity
- OECD analysis confirms economic benefits of health improvements from LGBTI+ equality
- HIV prevention and treatment access is directly affected by LGBT inclusion policies
- Human Development Index (including life expectancy) correlates positively with LGBT rights
