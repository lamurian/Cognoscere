## Summary

Five landmark citizen science projects demonstrate how popularization strategies translate into measurable outcomes across different scientific domains. Each project combines multiple strategies from the taxonomy, with distinct emphases reflecting their scientific goals and participant communities.

## Key Points

### Galaxy Zoo (Astronomy, launched 2007)
- **Core strategy**: Low-barrier visual classification + community forum
- **Launch trajectory**: 70,000 classifications/hour within 24 hours; 40M classifications in 175 days by 150K+ volunteers [@wikipediacontributors2026a]
- **Outcomes**: 60+ peer-reviewed papers; novel discoveries (Hanny's Voorwerp, Green Pea galaxies, red spirals); spawned Zooniverse platform (100+ projects) [@masters2019a]
- **Community**: "Zooites" identity; 650K+ forum posts over 7 years; volunteers became co-authors and project moderators
- **Popularization insight**: Human pattern recognition > algorithms for complex morphology; community discussion enabled serendipitous discovery

### eBird (Ornithology/Biodiversity, launched 2002)
- **Core strategy**: Mobile-first checklist app + gamified competitions + global institutional network
- **Growth trajectory**: 18M checklists in 2010 (8 years post-launch); 1.9B observations by 1M+ users in 2024 (22 years) [@sullivan2010; @teamebird2024]
- **Outcomes**: 1,180+ scientific publications; State of the Birds reports; animated continental migration maps; 2500+ volunteer reviewers in 253 countries [@teamebird2024]
- **Gamification**: Yard/Patch competitions, Global Big Day (1.3M participants), Bird of the Day (Merlin integration)
- **Popularization insight**: Align with existing hobby (birding); provide tools birders want (life lists, hotspots); global expansion through regional partners

### Foldit (Protein Folding/Biochemistry, launched 2008)
- **Core strategy**: Gamification as primary driver + scientific discovery feedback
- **Origin**: Evolved from Rosetta@home (2005) when volunteers wanted active role [@koepnick2019]
- **Outcomes**: Players designed novel proteins matching expert quality with greater structural diversity; 2019 Nature paper with "Foldit Players" as collective authors [@koepnick2019]
- **Mechanism**: Game encodes expert knowledge in scoring; players explore protein space creatively
- **Popularization insight**: Gamification can solve problems algorithms cannot; competitive/cooperative mechanics drive sustained engagement

### iNaturalist (Biodiversity Monitoring, launched 2008)
- **Core strategy**: Mobile observation app + AI-assisted identification + community verification + bioblitz events
- **Growth**: City Nature Challenge (annual global bioblitz) drives massive participation spikes; network analysis reveals core-periphery structure [@wikipediacontributors2026a]
- **Outcomes**: Research-grade observations feed GBIF; supports conservation policy; integrates with eBird/eButterfly
- **Popularization insight**: AI lowers identification barrier; social verification builds learning community; events create recruitment pulses

### Libraries as Community Hubs (Multi-domain, launched 2017)
- **Core strategy**: Institutional intermediaries + mini-grants + professional learning communities + kit-based lending
- **Phased scaling**: Arizona pilot (6 libraries, 2017–2019) → statewide mini-grants (2020–2021) → national network (900+ libraries, 49 states, 2021–2024) [@cox2026]
- **Outcomes**: 223 kit loans in pilot; 92% repeat borrowing; 439 staff trained; 1,200+ network members; 324K+ SciStarter page views [@cox2026]
- **Key mechanisms**: Equitable scaling (local adaptation), adult learning principles (staff autonomy), shift in ownership (library-led kits)
- **Popularization insight**: Trusted local institutions reach underrepresented audiences; capacity-building > one-off events
