## Summary

The four revolts map precisely onto the life cycle of the United States of Indonesia and show why the federal experiment failed. The sequence was: ideological revolt before the RIS (DI/TII, August 1949), a pro-federal coup one month in (APRA, January 1950), two pro-federal revolts during the unitarist sweep (Andi Aziz and RMS, April 1950), and dissolution of the RIS on 17 August 1950 [@wikipediacontributors2025j; @wikipediacontributors2025h; @wikipediacontributors2025i; @wikipediacontributors2025k].

Each revolt was reactive, triggered by a step of unitarist consolidation. APRA's January 1950 attempt to save a federation already losing legitimacy backfired: its failure discredited the federalist camp — Sultan Hamid II was arrested on 5 April and confessed on 19 April — and the coup "increased public agitation for the dissolution of the federal system," leading to the Pasundan state's dissolution by 10 February [@wikipediacontributors2025h]. When most remaining states dissolved into the Republic in March–April 1950, Andi Aziz seized Makassar in the last armed attempt to halt the integration of the State of East Indonesia; his defeat cleared the way for East Indonesia's parliament to vote for integration on 21 April [@wikipediacontributors2025i].

The RMS was proclaimed on 25 April — days after all thirteen other constituent territories of East Indonesia except the South Moluccas had seceded to the Republic — by Ambonese ex-KNIL soldiers who saw the move to a unitary state as a breach of the Round Table federation treaty [@wikipediacontributors2025i; @wikipediacontributors2025k]. The Indonesian invasion of Ambon in late 1950 ended the last territorial claim of the federal order.

The revolts thus accelerated rather than prevented the RIS's end. Every defeat removed a pillar of the federal arrangement: the KNIL as a political actor, the pro-Dutch aristocratic rulers, and federalist cabinet figures. In public memory the violence fused federalism with colonial divide-and-rule and separatism, entrenching the taboo that later made the unitary form constitutionally unamendable under Article 37(5) of the 2002 amendments [@bertrand2019; @tobing2015].

## Key Points

- Timeline alignment: DI/TII (Aug 1949, pre-RIS) → APRA (Jan 1950) → Andi Aziz and RMS (Apr 1950) → unitary proclamation (17 Aug 1950).
- All pro-federal revolts were reactions to the unitarist dissolution, not initiatives to build a working federation.
- Each defeat destroyed a federalist power base and boosted public support for the unitary state.
- DI/TII demonstrates the RIS was also contested on ideological grounds (Islamic state) from its start.
- The revolts cemented the equation of federalism with separatism and colonial divide-and-rule.
