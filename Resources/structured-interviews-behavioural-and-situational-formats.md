## Summary

Structured interviews are standardised employment assessments where every candidate answers the same predetermined questions in the same order, scored against consistent behavioural anchors. Schmidt and Hunter (1998) reported a predictive validity of r = .51 for well-designed structured interviews, comparable to GMA tests and far exceeding unstructured interviews (r = .38). The meta-analytic literature identifies three defining elements: job-analyzed questions, standardized scoring rubrics, and consistent administration across candidates.

### Question Items and Format

Structured interviews use two primary question formats:

**Behavioural questions** ask candidates to describe past experiences using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Example: "Tell me about a time you had to manage a conflict with a coworker. What did you do, and what was the outcome?" These are scored based on the specificity, relevance, and quality of the behavioural evidence provided.

**Situational questions** present hypothetical job-relevant scenarios and ask candidates to describe how they would respond. Example: "If you discovered a coworker was violating safety protocols, what would you do?" Responses are scored against predetermined behavioural anchors that define what constitutes an effective, acceptable, or ineffective response.

### Scoring and Interpretation

Each response is rated on a structured rubric, typically a 1-5 scale with behavioural anchors at each level. Scores are summed across questions to produce a total interview score. There are no universal clinical cutoffs; scoring is relative to the candidate pool and job requirements. The structured format reduces interviewer bias and improves inter-rater reliability compared to unstructured interviews.

### Validity Evidence

The r = .51 validity for structured interviews is the same as GMA tests and higher than integrity tests (r = .41), conscientiousness (r = .22-.31), and work samples (r = .54). When combined with GMA, structured interviews produce a multiple R of .63 — the second-strongest two-method combination after GMA + integrity (R = .65). Meta-analytic evidence from Sackett et al. (2022) confirms that structured interviews remain among the top predictors of job performance.

## Key Points

- Validity of r = .51 for structured interviews, comparable to GMA (Schmidt & Hunter, 1998)
- Two formats: behavioural (STAR/past behavior) and situational (hypothetical scenarios)
- Scored on structured rubrics with behavioural anchors; no universal cutoffs
- Unstructured interviews have lower validity (r = .38)
- GMA + structured interview = R = .63 (second-strongest two-method combination)
- Job-analyzed questions ensure content validity for the specific role
