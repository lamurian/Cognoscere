## Summary

Several publicly accessible ontologies specifically address the core archetype of health worker specialization. The most directly relevant are the NUCC Health Care Provider Taxonomy (US-focused), ESCO (European), and ISCO-08 (international), each providing hierarchical classification systems that map healthcare workers to specialization categories.

For US healthcare systems, the NUCC Taxonomy is the primary ontology, using 10-character alphanumeric codes with three levels: Provider Grouping, Classification, and Area of Specialization. This directly encodes the archetype of health worker specialization through its hierarchical structure.

For international comparability, ISCO-08 (maintained by ILO) provides the foundational framework, with healthcare workers classified under Major Group 2 (Professionals) and Sub-major Group 32 (Associate Professionals). ESCO builds on ISCO-08 with additional mapping to skills, competences, and qualifications.

The choice depends on the use case: NUCC for US billing/NPI systems, ISCO for international workforce statistics, ESCO for European labor markets and skill-based classification.

## Key Points

- **NUCC Taxonomy**: Best for US healthcare provider classification (OID: 2.16.840.1.113883.6.101)
- **ISCO-08**: Best for international comparability and workforce statistics
- **ESCO**: Best for linking occupations to skills and qualifications in European contexts
- **All three are freely accessible** and publicly available for download
- **Hierarchy structure**: All use hierarchical classification from broad categories to specific specializations
- **Directly address archetype**: Each system explicitly classifies health worker specialization through provider type, classification, and area of specialization
