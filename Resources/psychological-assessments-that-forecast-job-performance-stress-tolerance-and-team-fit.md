## Summary

No single mental health assessment tool simultaneously forecasts job performance, stress tolerance, and team fit with equal precision. Instead, industrial-organizational psychology uses a multi-method assessment battery combining validated instruments, each targeting one or two outcomes. Three assessment categories have the strongest meta-analytic evidence for direct forecasting: personality inventories, cognitive ability tests, and person-environment fit measures.

**Personality inventories based on the Big Five model** are the most widely used personality-based predictors. The Big Five dimensions — Conscientiousness, Emotional Stability (low Neuroticism), Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Openness — are measured by instruments such as the NEO Personality Inventory (NEO-PI-R), the Big Five Inventory (BFI-2), and the Hogan Personality Inventory (HPI). The landmark meta-analysis by Barrick and Mount (1991) established that Conscientiousness is the most consistent personality predictor of job performance across all occupational groups and criterion types (job proficiency, training proficiency, and personnel data). Emotional Stability is the second strongest general predictor, and Extraversion predicts performance specifically in sales and management roles. The mechanism works through trait-behaviour-performance chains: high Conscientiousness leads to goal-setting, persistence, and careful planning behaviours, which in turn drive task performance.

**General Mental Ability (GMA) tests** — measuring abstract reasoning, learning speed, and problem-solving — are the single strongest predictors of job performance. The Schmidt and Hunter (1998) meta-analysis of 85 years of research found that GMA tests have a corrected validity of .51 for job performance across all jobs, far exceeding personality measures (.10-.31), structured interviews (.51 when well-designed), or experience (.18). The mechanism is that GMA determines how quickly an individual learns job knowledge, and accumulated job knowledge is the proximal cause of job performance. GMA maintains its predictive power across different job complexity levels and job experience durations.

**Person-environment fit assessments** measure the congruence between individual characteristics and organisational demands. Two distinct constructs are assessed: person-job fit (matching skills and abilities to task requirements) and person-organisation fit (matching values and personality to organisational culture). These are measured through structured interviews, situational judgement tests, and value congruence questionnaires. Meta-analytic evidence shows that person-organisation fit predicts job satisfaction (rho = .51), organisational commitment (rho = .51), and turnover intentions (rho = -.35), while person-job fit predicts task performance and job satisfaction.

**Resilience and stress tolerance** are assessed through instruments like the Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC), the Brief Resilience Scale (BRS), and the Dispositional Resilience Scale (DRS). The CD-RISC measures three dimensions: tenacity (maintaining composure under pressure), self-empowerment (growth through adversity), and optimism (positive outlook). In workplace settings, resilience is predicted by social support (beta = .371), work stress (beta = -.195), and risk perception (beta = -.135). Higher resilience predicts lower burnout, reduced absenteeism, and better performance under stress.

**Integrity tests** predict counterproductive workplace behaviours (theft, absenteeism, deviance) and overall job performance. Two types exist: overt tests (direct questions about dishonesty) and personality-based tests (measuring conscientiousness, agreeableness, emotional stability). The meta-analytic validity of r = .41 (Ones et al., 1993) makes integrity tests a core component of selection batteries, particularly when combined with GMA (R = .65 — the strongest two-method combination).

## Key Points

- No single tool forecasts all three outcomes — multi-method battery is standard practice
- **Big Five personality**: Conscientiousness is the most consistent personality predictor of job performance across all jobs (Barrick & Mount 1991, meta-analysis)
- **Emotional Stability** is the second strongest general personality predictor; Extraversion predicts sales/management performance
- **GMA tests**: Corrected validity of .51 for job performance — strongest single predictor (Schmidt & Hunter 1998)
- GMA mechanism: determines learning speed, which determines accumulated job knowledge, which determines performance
- **Person-organisation fit**: Predicts job satisfaction (rho = .51), commitment (rho = .51), turnover intentions (rho = -.35)
- **Person-job fit**: Predicts task performance and job satisfaction directly
- **CD-RISC resilience**: Measures tenacity, self-empowerment, optimism; predicts lower burnout and better stress performance
- **Structured interviews**: When well-designed, achieve validity of .51 — comparable to GMA
- **Integrity tests**: Predict counterproductive workplace behaviours (theft, absenteeism, deviance)
- All instruments must be validated, job-related, and administered uniformly per ADA

## How Each Mechanism Works

**Personality → Behaviour → Performance chain:**
1. Conscientious individuals set goals, plan carefully, persist through obstacles
2. These behaviours lead to higher task performance and fewer errors
3. The trait is stable across situations but manifests as consistent behavioural tendencies

**GMA → Learning → Knowledge → Performance chain:**
1. Higher GMA enables faster acquisition of job-relevant knowledge
2. Accumulated knowledge allows more effective problem-solving and decision-making
3. This manifests as superior job performance across virtually all occupations

**Fit → Motivation → Performance chain:**
1. Person-organisation value congruence increases intrinsic motivation and identification
2. Person-job skill match enables effective task execution from day one
3. Both reduce turnover intentions and increase organisational citizenship behaviours

**Resilience → Coping → Sustained Performance chain:**
1. Resilient individuals appraise stressors as challenges rather than threats
2. Adaptive coping strategies (problem-focused, help-seeking) preserve cognitive resources
3. This maintains performance under pressure and accelerates recovery from setbacks
