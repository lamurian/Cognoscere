## Summary

The Bedwell et al. (2012) taxonomy of nine game attributes provides a structured framework for analyzing which game elements drive outcomes in health gamification. Applied to 15 RCTs of gamified eHealth interventions, three attributes appear universally: assessment (feedback), conflict/challenge, and rules/goals.

Bas-Sarmiento et al. (2025) classified game attributes across all included studies using Bedwell's taxonomy:

1. **Assessment** (100% of studies): Feedback through scores, rewards, badges, and progress indicators.
2. **Conflict/challenge** (100% of studies): Tasks, puzzles, and competition systems that create productive tension.
3. **Rules/goals** (100% of studies): Clear objectives and progress indicators.
4. **Environment** (78% of studies): Context for the game, including metaphors/fantasy settings and real-world scenarios.
5. **Human interaction** (47% of studies): Interpersonal interaction and social interaction mediated through technology.

The authors noted that most interventions relied heavily on extrinsic motivation elements and that game design should incorporate more intrinsic motivation elements through autonomy-supportive mechanics [@bassarmiento2025].

## Key Points

- **Assessment + challenge + goals** form the universal core of health gamification
- **Social elements** are underutilized (47%) despite evidence that human interaction enhances belonging and sustained engagement
- **Environmental immersion** (fantasy, narrative, VR) shows promise for behavioral outcomes, especially in preoperative anxiety and physical activity contexts
- **Extrinsic dominance**: Most designs rely heavily on points, badges, and leaderboards, risking the overjustification effect
- **Design implication**: Effective health gamification should balance extrinsic feedback with intrinsic motivation support
