## Summary

Anthropomorphism's trust payoff is bounded by context. The same human-like cues that raise trust in low-stakes settings can backfire where the consequences of error are serious, such as medical, legal or financial advice [@kummer2026]. The meta-analytic evidence agrees: the impact of anthropomorphism on adoption depends on robot type and on the service category, with effects differing between possession-processing and mental stimulus-processing services [@blut2021].

This duality is consistent with Computers Are Social Actors theory, where social responses to AI are real but context-dependent: users apply social heuristics that can either increase or decrease trust depending on perceived stakes [@kummer2026].

For design practice this means persona is not a single lever. High-stakes domains should default to factual, restrained voice, because a warm persona in a medical setting can inflate trust in output that deserves scrutiny.

## Key Points

- Human-like cues increase trust and advice-following only in low-stakes contexts; they backfire in high-stakes ones [@kummer2026].
- Meta-analysis confirms context moderation by service type and agent characteristics [@blut2021].
- Implication: factual default for high-stakes domains, warmth reserved for low-stakes interaction.
