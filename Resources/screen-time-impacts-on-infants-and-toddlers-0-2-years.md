## Summary

Screen time for children under 2 carries the highest developmental risk of any age group. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends avoiding screen media entirely for children younger than 18 months, with the exception of video chatting. For children aged 18 to 24 months, limited screen use is acceptable only with high-quality programming and caregiver co-viewing. The WHO echoes this guidance, recommending no screen exposure for children under 2.

The primary concern is that screens displace critical developmental interactions. Ninety percent of brain development occurs in the first five years, and this process depends on face-to-face talking, playing, and exploring. Screen time replaces these real-world interactions, particularly affecting language acquisition. Research shows that infants exposed to more than four hours of daily screen time at age 1 showed delays in communication and problem-solving skills at ages 2 and 4 @takahashi2023. A large cohort study by Madigan et al. @madigan2019 found that higher screen time at ages 24 and 36 months was associated with poorer performance on developmental screening tests at subsequent assessments.

Screen-based media also overstimulate the developing brain disproportionately. The visual cortex receives excessive input while the auditory cortex, critical for social skills and language, is underdeveloped by comparison. Passive screen viewing does not provide the interactive conversational input that infant brains require for language development. Additionally, fNIRS research demonstrates that children's brains function differently during screen-based storytelling compared to live book reading, with reduced engagement of socially oriented cognitive processes @pecukonis2025.

## Key Points

- Recommended maximum: 0 minutes/day (avoid screens entirely under 18 months)
- Ages 18-24 months: limited use permitted only with co-viewing of high-quality content
- Short-term risks: delayed communication, reduced social engagement, overstimulation
- Long-term risks: persistent developmental delays in language, fine motor, and social skills
- Video chatting (e.g., with grandparents) is the sole exception for infants under 18 months
- Caregiver mediation is essential; solo screen use is particularly harmful at this age
