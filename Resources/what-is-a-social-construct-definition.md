## Summary

A social construct is any object, fact, idea, or human kind whose existence, character, or distribution is grounded in, caused, constituted, or controlled by social or cultural factors rather than by nature alone. The core idea, per the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, is that some objects are "grounded in, or caused, constituted, or controlled by, social or cultural factors rather than natural factors," with the core motivation being to show that such objects "could be, or might have been, otherwise" [@mallon2024]. That last point — contingency — is the heart of the concept.

Claims that "X is socially constructed" have the two-part relational form "X socially constructs Y" [@mallon2024]. They vary along three dimensions: what constructs (impersonal agents such as culture, institutions, and social structures, or personal agents whose choices and interests produce the object), what is constructed (representations such as theories and concepts, non-representational facts, or human traits and kinds), and the nature of the construction relation (causal or constitutive) [@mallon2024].

Diaz-Leon, following Hacking, decomposes any constructionist claim into three sub-claims: a contingency claim (the instantiation and distribution of X depends on social arrangements and could differ), a badness claim (the current arrangement is unjust or inferior), and a reformation claim (X could and should be radically transformed) [@diazleon2015]. Construction claims come in two broad forms: idea constructionism, about concepts and theories (trivially true and philosophically uninteresting), and object constructionism, about things in the world [@diazleon2015]. They also divide into overt constructions (a U.S. Senator, a licensed dog — obviously social) and covert constructions (race, gender, mental illness — believed natural but produced by social practice) [@mallon2024]. Berger and Luckmann supply the classic sociological formulation: society is produced by "subjective meanings" becoming "objective facticities" [@berger1966].

## Key Points

- Core idea: constructed objects are grounded in, caused by, constituted by, or controlled by social factors rather than natural ones [@mallon2024]
- Contingency is the defining feature: a social construct could have been otherwise [@mallon2024]
- Hacking's triad: contingency, badness, and reformation claims [@diazleon2015]
- Idea constructionism (concepts) vs object constructionism (worldly things) [@diazleon2015]
- Overt constructions (senators, money) vs covert constructions (race, gender, mental illness) [@mallon2024]
- Berger and Luckmann: subjective meanings become objective facticities [@berger1966]
