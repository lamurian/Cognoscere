## Summary

Excessive screen time is consistently associated with increased risk of overweight and obesity in children. A meta-analysis by Fang et al. (2019) found that screen time exceeding 2 hours/day was positively associated with overweight/obesity risk (OR 1.67; 95% CI 1.48-1.88), meaning children with more than 2 hours daily exposure have 67% higher risk compared to those with less exposure @gonzlezprez2025.

A systematic review analyzing 40 articles found an 11% increased risk (OR 1.766; 95% CI 1.104-2.562) between screen time and increased adiposity, BMI, abdominal circumference, and body fat percentage @gonzlezprez2025. Reducing TV time was associated with a BMI decrease of -0.89 kg/m² (95% CI -1.467 to 0.11) in clinical trials @gonzlezprez2025.

The mechanisms include displacement of physical activity, increased caloric intake through mindless snacking, and metabolic dysregulation. Children under 5 spend approximately 50% of their time in sedentary behaviors, with screen time being a major contributor @souza2025.

## Key Points

- 2+ hours/day screen time increases obesity risk by 67%
- 3+ hours/day associated with clinically meaningful cardiometabolic risk
- Displacement of physical activity is primary mechanism
- Mindless snacking during screen use contributes to weight gain
- FTO gene polymorphism (rs9939609) interacts with screen time to increase waist circumference @gonzlezprez2025
