## Summary

The Content Validity Ratio (CVR) is a quantitative index developed by @lawshe1975 for determining the degree to which experts agree that an item is essential to the content domain of a construct. It transforms subjective expert ratings into a numerical statistic that can be used to make objective decisions about item retention or deletion during scale development.

The CVR procedure requires an expert panel (minimum 5-10 members) to rate each item on a 3-point scale of relevance: Essential, Useful but Not Essential, or Not Necessary. The formula is:

**CVR = (ne - N/2) / (N/2)**

where ne = number of panelists rating the item as "Essential" and N = total number of panelists. The resulting value ranges from -1 to +1, where zero means half the panel rated the item as essential. Negative values indicate fewer than half considered the item essential.

Lawshe's original table provides minimum CVR values needed for statistical significance based on panel size. For a panel of 5, the CVR must be 1.00; for 7 panelists, 0.99; for 10 panelists, 0.62; and for 15 panelists, 0.49. Items falling below these thresholds should be rejected. The CVR is most appropriate for early-stage content validation when the goal is to eliminate clearly irrelevant items before statistical item analysis.

@kyriazos2018 note that the CVR is part of the expert panel review phase (Phase D) of scale development, complemented by sensitivity reviews for potential item bias and standard setting for cutoff scores. The method is widely used in healthcare, education, and social science instrument development, though it relies entirely on expert judgment and does not provide statistical evidence of item performance with actual respondents.

## Key Points

- CVR formula: (ne - N/2) / (N/2), ranging from -1 to +1 (@lawshe1975)
- Expert panel rates items as Essential, Useful but Not Essential, or Not Necessary
- Minimum CVR thresholds depend on panel size (0.99 for 7 panelists to 0.49 for 15)
- Items below threshold are rejected as content-irrelevant
- Part of expert panel review phase in scale development (@kyriazos2018)
- Relies on judgment, not empirical item performance data
