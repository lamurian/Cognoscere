## Summary

Two complementary taxonomies address psychological well-being from different traditions. Ryff's model operationalises eudaimonic well-being (fulfilment of human potential), while the WHO-5 captures hedonic and affective well-being (positive mood and vitality).

**Ryff's Psychological Well-Being (PWB) model** identifies six core dimensions of eudaimonic well-being: Self-Acceptance (positive attitude toward oneself and past life); Positive Relations with Others (warm, trusting interpersonal relationships); Autonomy (self-determination, independence, internal locus of evaluation); Environmental Mastery (competence in managing one's life and surrounding world); Purpose in Life (goals, direction, sense of meaning); and Personal Growth (continued development, openness to new experiences) [@ryff1995]. These dimensions emerged from factor analysis of theoretical integration across lifespan development, clinical, and existential psychology.

The **WHO-5 Well-Being Index** provides a five-item measure of current subjective well-being capturing positive mood (cheerful, in good spirits), vitality (active, vigorous), and general interest (woken up feeling fresh, interested in things) over the past two weeks [@topp2015]. It is a unidimensional scale (single total score 0-100), widely used as a screening tool for depression and as an outcome measure in clinical trials. Unlike Ryff's multidimensional eudaimonic framework, WHO-5 focuses on the hedonic/affective dimension.

## Key Points

- Ryff's PWB: 6 eudaimonic dimensions (Self-Acceptance, Positive Relations, Autonomy, Environmental Mastery, Purpose in Life, Personal Growth) [@ryff1995]
- WHO-5: 5 items measuring positive mood, vitality, and interest over 2 weeks [@topp2015]
- Ryff's model captures what it means to flourish psychologically beyond mere happiness
- WHO-5 is a unidimensional screening tool, validated across languages and cultures
- Together they cover both hedonic (WHO-5) and eudaimonic (Ryff) aspects of psychological well-being
- Both are self-report measures suitable for population surveys and clinical assessment
