## Summary

The rate at which new measurement instruments are published in social science is substantial and has been described as a growing concern. @jebb2021 note that "a recent review suggests that in the span of just a few years, dozens of scales are added to the psychological science literature" (citing @colquitt2019). This rapid accumulation of instruments contributes to the construct proliferation problem.

While no single study has comprehensively quantified the annual rate of new scale publication across all social sciences, the phenomenon is widely recognized. @rutherford2022 frame redundant measurement instrument development as a form of research waste, alongside asking the wrong questions, using inappropriate study designs, and failing to report results properly. They argue that resources spent developing instruments that duplicate existing ones could be better allocated elsewhere.

The proliferation is partly driven by the structure of academic publishing and career advancement. Developing and validating a new instrument constitutes a discrete, publishable contribution, making it an attractive research activity. This creates a market dynamic where the supply of new scales may exceed the genuine demand for them.

## Key Points

- Dozens of new scales are added to the psychological literature in just a few years (@jebb2021, @colquitt2019)
- Redundant instrument development is classified as research waste (@rutherford2022)
- Academic publishing incentives drive continued scale development
- No comprehensive quantification of annual new scale publication exists
- The proliferation of instruments may exceed genuine measurement needs
