## Summary

This executive summary synthesises the causal mechanisms linking four distinct but interacting constructs in resilience science: trait resilience, state resilience, resilience factors, and resilience process. The synthesis draws on systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and theoretical frameworks published between 2007 and 2025.

### The Four Constructs and Their Relationships

**Trait resilience** is the relatively stable dispositional foundation — hardiness, ego-resiliency, self-efficacy, optimism — formed by psychological capital and internal representations of the world. It constitutes the latent potential from which adaptive capacity is drawn during adversity. Trait resilience is measured by instruments like the CD-RISC, DRS-15, and ER89, which capture personality-level characteristics that predispose individuals toward adaptive functioning [@helmreich2017; @southwick2014; @denckla2020].

**Resilience factors** are the empirically validated protective determinants — self-efficacy, optimism, social support, cognitive flexibility, meaning in life, sense of coherence — that constitute the resource pool from which individuals draw during adversity. These factors are conceptually distinct from trait resilience: they are the inputs and antecedents, not the trait itself. Evidence from Helmreich et al. (2017) identifies Level 1 factors (strong evidence from SRs and MAs) including active coping, self-efficacy, optimism, social support, cognitive flexibility, and religiosity. The umbrella review by Abate et al. (2024) quantifies their pooled effects: self-regulation d = 0.43, positive self-perception d = 0.34, availability of support d = 0.26 [@helmreich2017; @abate2024].

**State resilience** is the situation-specific, fluctuating manifestation of trait-level resources during adversity. It is not merely "activated" from trait to state through a simple switch. Rather, state resilience emerges through a dynamic process of resource mobilisation. Luthans et al. (2007) position resilience as state-like within PsyCap — open to development, influenced by context, and fluctuating across situations. The same individual may exhibit high state resilience in one context and low in another, depending on which resources are accessible, how the situation is appraised, and what coping strategies are available at that moment [@luthans2007; @farchi2025].

**Resilience process** is the mechanism that connects trait-level resources to state-level outcomes. It is the dynamic adaptive process where an individual selects the most efficient strategy to resolve adversity. Kalisch et al. (2015) frame this as the operation of an internal resilience mechanism involving prefrontal-amygdala circuits, HPA axis regulation, and cognitive appraisal. Troy et al. (2023) emphasise that the process requires flexible deployment of regulatory strategies matched to stressor characteristics — reappraisal for controllable stressors, acceptance for uncontrollable ones. Inflexible strategy use, even of adaptive strategies, produces poorer outcomes [@kalisch2014; @troy2023; @stainton2018].

### The Causal Chain

The causal association between trait and state resilience follows a three-part chain:

1. **Trait resilience and resilience factors form the latent potential.** Psychological capital (self-efficacy, hope, optimism, resilience) and internal representations of the world (schemas, attributional styles, sense of coherence) create a resource pool that exists prior to adversity. This is the dispositional foundation [@luthans2007; @helmreich2017].

2. **The resilience process mobilises and deploys these resources during adversity.** Through the ART mechanism (Acknowledge resources, Reframe threats as challenges, Tailor responses to situational demands), individuals dynamically select and implement the most efficient coping strategy for the specific stressor. This is not a simple activation of a fixed trait but a context-dependent, flexible operation [@farchi2025].

3. **State resilience is the observable outcome of this mobilisation.** When the process operates successfully — when resources are adequately mobilised and matched to demands — the individual maintains adaptive functioning despite adversity. This is what is measured as state resilience: the manifest expression of latent potential through dynamic process [@stainton2018; @abate2024].

### Key Mechanistic Insights

- **Resilience is not simply activated from trait to state.** The process involves dynamic resource mobilisation, strategy selection, and real-time adjustment. The ART framework demonstrates that trait-like resources become accessible through a three-stage process of acknowledgement, reframing, and tailoring, not through passive activation [@farchi2025].

- **Efficiency of resource mobilisation distinguishes resilient from non-resilient individuals.** Resilient individuals are highly efficient at deploying psychological resources to resolve adversity. This efficiency depends on the richness of the resource pool (resilience factors), the flexibility of the appraisal and coping process, and the match between available resources and situational demands [@troy2023; @kalisch2014].

- **The process is modifiable and trainable.** Interventions targeting resilience factors produce significant improvements (pooled d = 0.42 for all interventions, d = 0.52 for resilience-specific interventions). This confirms that resilience is not fixed but can be strengthened through systematic training of cognitive flexibility, emotion regulation, and resource mobilisation skills [@abate2024; @helmreich2017].

- **Neurobiological substrates support the dynamic character.** Prefrontal-amygdala circuitry, HPA axis flexibility, and heart rate variability provide the physiological infrastructure for the resilience process. These systems fluctuate moment-to-moment, consistent with state-like rather than trait-like characterisation at the mechanistic level [@kalisch2014; @stainton2018].

## Key Points

- Trait resilience = latent potential (dispositional resources + internal world representations)
- Resilience factors = empirically validated protective inputs (self-efficacy, optimism, support, cognitive flexibility)
- Resilience process = dynamic adaptive mechanism (Acknowledge -> Reframe -> Tailor)
- State resilience = observable outcome of resource mobilisation during adversity
- Causal chain: trait resources -> dynamic process -> state manifestation
- Resilience is not simply activated but dynamically mobilised through context-dependent strategy selection
- Interventions significantly improve resilience (d = 0.30-0.52), confirming modifiability
