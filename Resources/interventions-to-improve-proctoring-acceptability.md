## Summary

Denker et al. (2023) provide the most comprehensive evidence on what improves proctoring acceptability. Prior information was a strong predictor of all acceptance dimensions — controllability (beta = 0.29), face validity (beta = 0.27), absence of strain (beta = 0.21), and overall evaluation (beta = 0.18). They recommend: (a) providing sufficient preparation material, (b) transparently informing about testing conditions, and (c) for proctoring tests specifically, making participants aware of how to create a suitable home environment [@denker2023].

Test specificity is another key lever. Tests with higher subject-specific content (e.g., pharmacy test scoring 4.57/6 overall) far outperformed general ability tests (GSAT scoring 4.02/6). Face validity was rated 3.90/6 for subject-specific tests vs. 2.65/6 for general tests [@denker2023]. Making test content clearly relevant to the field improves perceived legitimacy and acceptance.

The Andreou et al. (2021) study found that clear instructions and an animation movie explaining the proctoring software helped reduce stress. They also note that a direct connection with the university through the software provided reassurance to some candidates [@andreou2021].

Jiang et al. (2023) found that social influence was the strongest predictor of acceptance (beta = 0.44). This means institutional endorsement — clear policies, visible support from respected authorities, and positive framing from teachers — significantly improves acceptance. They also recommend building a positive group atmosphere to enhance social presence [@jiang2023].

Denker et al. (2023) found that providing alternatives is critical. While proctoring was evaluated positively overall, test centres were still rated more favourably. For the ~10% of test-takers with privacy concerns, offering an alternative (test centre, on-campus, or unproctored with statistical detection) is essential for equity. Privacy ratings in proctored settings showed lower overall evaluation (2.92/6 vs. 3.91/6 for test centre), even though the Akzept dimensions were not significantly different [@denker2023].

Summing up, effective interventions: (1) comprehensive prior information and preparation materials, (2) subject-specific test content with clear relevance, (3) institutional endorsement and clear policies, (4) positive group atmosphere and social presence, (5) alternative testing options for privacy-concerned test-takers, and (6) transparent privacy and data protection communications.

## Key Points

- Prior information is the strongest modifiable predictor of acceptance
- Subject-specific content dramatically improves face validity and acceptance
- Institutional endorsement (social influence) drives acceptance more than technology factors
- Offering alternatives for the ~10% with privacy concerns is essential for equity
- Clear, transparent privacy communications improve trust
