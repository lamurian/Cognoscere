## Summary

The Brief Resilience Scale (BRS) is a 6-item self-report measure of the ability to bounce back or recover from stress, developed by Smith et al. (2008) [@smith2008]. Items are rated on a 5-point Likert scale (1-5) and scored as the mean of all six items; items 2, 4, and 6 are negatively worded and reverse-scored. It is the only widely used resilience measure that assesses recovery itself rather than resilience-promoting resources [@chmitorz2018].

Internal validity: the scale is unidimensional. Confirmatory factor analyses in large German population samples (n=1,481 and n=1,128) found a method-factor model with excellent fit (RMSEA=.01-.07, CFI=.99-1.00, SRMR=.01-.02), which accounts for item-wording effects; the authors recommend using only the unidimensional total score [@chmitorz2018]. The Spanish validation (n=620) confirmed a monofactorial structure (RMSEA=.067, CFI=.984) [@rodrguezrey2016]. Internal consistency: alpha .80-.91 in the original samples [@smith2008], .85 (omega .85) in both German population samples [@chmitorz2018], .83 in the Spanish sample [@rodrguezrey2016], with .76-.93 across other translations.

External validity: the BRS correlates moderately with the CD-RISC (r=.59) and ER-89 (r=.51), indicating related but distinct constructs [@smith2008]. In the German population validation, BRS scores correlated positively with well-being, social support, and optimism, and negatively with somatic symptoms, anxiety/insomnia, social dysfunction, and depression; average variance extracted (.55) exceeded all squared latent correlations, supporting discriminant validity [@chmitorz2018]. Spanish data showed positive correlations with the CD-RISC and positive emotions, and negative correlations with perceived stress, anxiety/depression (HADS), and posttraumatic stress [@rodrguezrey2016].

Interpretation: descriptive bands (not clinical cutoffs) are <3.00 low, 3.00-4.30 normal, >4.30 high. German general population means were 3.58 and 3.37 (SD ~0.7); women score moderately lower (d=0.30), and scores decline slightly with age [@chmitorz2018]. The BRS received a top psychometric rating (7/18) in the Windle et al. (2011) methodological review together with the CD-RISC and RSA [@windle2011]. No license is required; the scale is freely usable with citation.

## Key Points

- 6 items, 5-point scale, mean total 1-5; items 2, 4, 6 reverse-scored
- Unidimensional; use total score only, not wording-based subscales [@chmitorz2018]
- Internal consistency alpha .80-.91 (original), .85 (German population) [@smith2008; @chmitorz2018]
- Test-retest: ICC .62-.69 (original), .69 at 4-6 weeks (Spanish) [@rodrguezrey2016]
- Convergent: r=.59 with CD-RISC; r=.51 with ER-89 [@smith2008]
- Discriminant validity supported: AVE=.55 exceeds squared correlations with mental-health constructs [@chmitorz2018]
- Bands: <3.00 low, 3.00-4.30 normal, >4.30 high (descriptive)
- Free: no permission or license required; cite Smith et al. (2008)
- Top-3 psychometric rating (7/18) in Windle et al. (2011) review [@windle2011]
