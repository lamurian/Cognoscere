## Summary

Redundant development of measurement instruments is increasingly recognized as a form of research waste. @rutherford2022, in their introduction to a special section on reducing research waste in quality of life research, identify the creation of duplicate instruments as a significant inefficiency. Each new scale development study consumes resources including researcher time, participant burden, and funding that could be directed toward substantive research questions.

The consequences extend beyond wasted development resources. When multiple scales measure the same construct differently, it becomes difficult to compare findings across studies, conduct meaningful meta-analyses, and build cumulative knowledge. This fragmentation of measurement hampers the scientific enterprise by making it harder to establish whether findings replicate across different operationalizations of the same construct.

@steger2022 demonstrate that even well-intentioned scale construction can produce instruments that are either too narrow (high reliability but poor coverage) or too broad (good coverage but poor reliability). This suggests that research waste occurs not only from redundant development but also from poor construction practices that produce instruments of limited utility.

## Key Points

- Redundant instrument development is classified as research waste (@rutherford2022)
- Each new scale consumes researcher time, participant burden, and funding
- Multiple scales measuring the same construct hinder cross-study comparison and meta-analysis
- Poor construction practices also produce instruments of limited utility (@steger2022)
- Fragmentation of measurement impedes cumulative scientific knowledge
