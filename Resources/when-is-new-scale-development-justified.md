## Summary

New scale development is justified under specific circumstances. @boateng2018 outline that scale developers should first confirm no existing instrument adequately serves the same purpose, and where a similar instrument exists, justify why a new instrument is appropriate and how it will differ from existing instruments. This is Step 1 of their nine-step scale development framework.

Legitimate justifications include: (1) measuring a genuinely new construct that has no established measure; (2) cultural or contextual adaptation where existing scales fail to capture construct meaning in a new population; (3) significant psychometric deficiencies in existing instruments; and (4) theoretical advancement that fundamentally reconceptualizes a construct.

@houts2022 argue instead for evaluating and supporting expanded uses of existing scales, noting that scale development requires significant investments of time and money. They suggest that validity theory and psychometrics can be used to support using existing scales in new contexts rather than developing new ones from scratch. This approach conserves resources and enables better cross-study comparison.

@jebb2021 recommend that before developing a new scale, researchers should pay close attention to constructs already established in the literature and engage in critical and honest reflection on whether their target construct is meaningfully different from existing ones.

## Key Points

- Best practice: confirm no existing instrument serves the same purpose first (@boateng2018)
- Legitimate reasons include genuinely new constructs, cultural adaptation, and psychometric deficiencies
- Existing scales can often be validated for expanded uses rather than developing new ones (@houts2022)
- Critical reflection on construct distinctiveness is essential before new development (@jebb2021)
- New scale development should be a last resort, not a first response
