## Summary

Functional capacity instruments measure ability to perform activities of daily living, instrumental activities, work functioning, and independence. This catalog documents 12 widely validated instruments. These map directly to ICF Activities and Participation (chapters d4-d9) and are central to rehabilitation and geriatric assessment.

## Instrument Catalog

**1. WHODAS 2.0 (12-item)**
Authors: WHO, 2010. 12 items covering 6 ICF-based domains: Cognition, Mobility, Self-Care, Getting Along, Life Activities, Participation. 5-point Likert (1-5). Score: sum/12*100 or sum/60*100 (0-100). Cutoffs: 0-4 no difficulty, 5-24 mild, 25-49 moderate, 50-94 severe, 95+ extreme. alpha=0.73-0.96. Manual: https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/data-research/who-disease-assessment-schedule

**2. WHODAS 2.0 (36-item)**
Authors: WHO, 2010. 36 items across same 6 domains with greater detail. Score: sum/36*100 (0-100). Same cutoffs as 12-item version. alpha=0.97. Maps fully to ICF framework. Manual: https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/data-research/who-disease-assessment-schedule

**3. Katz Index of Independence in ADLs**
Authors: Katz et al., 1963. 6 items: bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, feeding. Each 0-1 (independent/dependent). Total 0-6. Cutoff: 6 independent, 4-5 moderate dependence, 0-3 severe dependence. Widely used in geriatric assessment. Manual: freely available.

**4. Lawton Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL) Scale**
Authors: Lawton & Brody, 1969. 8 items: using telephone, shopping, food preparation, housekeeping, laundry, transportation, medication management, finances. Each 0-2 (depends on gender). Total 0-8. Higher = more independent. alpha=0.85. Manual: freely available.

**5. Barthel Index**
Authors: Mahoney & Barthel, 1965. 10 items covering self-care and mobility. Each scored with varying weights (0-15). Total 0-100. Cutoffs: 61-90 mild dependence, 41-60 moderate, 21-40 severe, 0-20 total dependence. alpha=0.89. Manual: freely available.

**6. Functional Independence Measure (FIM)**
Authors: Uniform Data System, 1987. 18 items across Motor (13 items) and Cognitive (5 items) subscales. Each 1-7 (total assistance to complete independence). Total 18-126. alpha=0.93-0.95. Used in rehabilitation settings. Manual: https://www.udsmr.org/

**7. SF-36 Physical Functioning Subscale**
Authors: Ware et al., 1992. 10 items assessing ability to perform physical activities. 3-point scale. Transformed score 0-100. alpha=0.81-0.92. Part of the broader SF-36. Manual: https://www.optum.com/quality-and-advancement/health-surveys

**8. PROMIS Physical Function CAT**
Authors: Cella et al., 2007. Computer Adaptive Testing using IRT. 4-12 items administered adaptively. T-score (mean=50, SD=10). ICC=0.92-0.96. More efficient than fixed-length forms. Manual: https://www.healthmeasures.net/explore-measurement-systems/promis

**9. Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS)**
Authors: Krupp et al., 1989. 9 items, 1-7 scale. Mean score >=4.0 indicates significant fatigue. alpha=0.89-0.92. Originally developed for MS and lupus, now used across chronic conditions. Manual: freely available.

**10. Patient Activation Measure (PAM-13)**
Authors: Hibbard et al., 2004. 13 items, 4-point scale (1-4). Score converted to activation level 0-100. 4 levels: 1 (disengaged) to 4 (patient activation). alpha=0.87-0.88. Measures patient self-management capacity. Manual: https://www.insigniahealth.com/

**11. Self-Efficacy for Managing Chronic Disease Scale**
Authors: Lorig et al., 1989. 6 items, 1-10 scale. Mean score (6-60). Higher = greater self-efficacy. alpha=0.91. Predicts health behaviors and outcomes in chronic disease. Manual: freely available through Stanford Patient Education Research Center.

**12. Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire (PSEQ)**
Authors: Nicholas, 2007. 10 items, 0-6 scale. Total 0-60. Higher = stronger self-efficacy for functioning despite pain. alpha=0.92. Predicts disability beyond pain intensity. Manual: freely available.

## Key Points

- WHODAS 2.0 is the WHO's official ICF-based functioning measure, linking directly to ICF chapters
- Katz ADL and Lawton IADL are the most widely used functional assessment tools in geriatrics
- Barthel Index remains the standard for acute stroke rehabilitation assessment
- PROMIS PF CAT offers superior precision with fewer items through adaptive testing
- PAM-13 measures capacity for self-management, bridging functional capacity and health behaviors
- Self-efficacy scales predict functional outcomes beyond impairment measures
