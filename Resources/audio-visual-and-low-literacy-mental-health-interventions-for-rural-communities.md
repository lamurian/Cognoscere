## Summary

Audio-visual delivery of mental health content addresses two critical barriers in rural Indonesian communities: low literacy and stigma. The mPareshan study in rural Pakistan found that community health workers using mobile apps with visual and audio components enabled consistent service delivery despite low literacy levels, and the technology was perceived as 'modern and confidential,' reducing stigma [@akhtar2025]. The WHO STARS chatbot uses videos, audio, text, and quizzes to deliver CBT-based content to youth in LMICs, with users preferring videos over written information and short sessions (5-10 minutes ideal) [@keyan2025].

For rural Indonesia specifically, the critique of one-size-fits-all interventions highlights that Western diagnostic categories may not capture local idioms of distress -- psychological distress is often expressed somatically rather than as sadness or hopelessness [@osborne2026]. This means audio-visual content must be culturally adapted to local expressions of mental distress. A specialist in the Surjaningrum study recommended against using psychiatric labels (e.g., 'depression'), using 'mood changes' instead [@surjaningrum2018].

The Javanese cultural context, where distress is expressed through somatic complaints and relational terms, requires audio-visual content that uses local narratives rather than clinical language. The WHO PFA guide emphasizes respecting dignity, culture, and abilities [@worldhealthorganization2011]. For the proposed kiosk, this means: (1) content in Bahasa Indonesia with local language options; (2) use of culturally familiar scenarios and characters; (3) audio narration for low-literacy users; (4) visual icons rather than text-heavy screens; (5) non-stigmatizing, non-diagnostic framing of mental health.

The Satu Persen platform's success (3.63M YouTube subscribers) demonstrates that audio-visual mental health content resonates strongly with Indonesian audiences, though their model focuses on education rather than clinical intervention.

## Key Points
- Audio-visual delivery overcomes literacy barriers: mPareshan study confirmed in rural Pakistan
- WHO STARS chatbot: users prefer videos over text, short sessions (5-10 min)
- Stigma reduced when technology is perceived as modern and confidential
- Javanese cultural context: distress expressed somatically, avoid psychiatric labels
- Content must be culturally adapted with local languages, scenarios, and narratives
- Satu Persen's YouTube success (3.63M subs) shows strong Indonesian appetite for video mental health content
