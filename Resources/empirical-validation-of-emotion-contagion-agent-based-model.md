## Summary

Van Haeringen et al. [@vanhaeringen2024] present one of the few empirically validated agent-based models of a psychological process relevant to resilience: emotion contagion. The model was validated at the level of individual emotional responses, moving beyond aggregate pattern matching to test whether simulated agents produce realistic emotional trajectories.

The validation approach compared model outputs against empirical data from controlled laboratory studies of emotional contagion in groups. The model demonstrated that agents could reproduce the temporal dynamics and intensity patterns of emotional spread observed in human groups, providing evidence for the ecological validity of emotion contagion mechanisms in simulation.

While not directly a resilience model, this work validates a core psychological mechanism that underlies social support and collective coping processes central to resilience theory. The emotion contagion mechanism is particularly relevant for understanding how resilience propagates through social networks and how group interventions might amplify individual resilience training effects.

## Key Points

- One of few empirically validated ABMs of a psychological mechanism (emotion contagion)
- Validated at individual-level emotional response trajectories, not just aggregate patterns
- Demonstrates feasibility of validating psychological process models against laboratory data
- Relevant to resilience through social support and collective coping mechanisms
- Provides methodological template for validating other psychological process ABMs
