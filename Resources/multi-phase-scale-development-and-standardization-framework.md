## Summary

The scale development and standardization process follows a multi-phase framework that can be decomposed into five sequential phases: (A) Instrument Purpose and Construct Definition, (B) Response Scale Specifications, (C) Item Generation, (D) Item Evaluation, and (E) Psychometric Property Testing (@kyriazos2018). Test development and standardization (or norming) are two related processes where test development comes first and standardization follows. During test development, after item assembly and analysis, the items which are strongest indicators of the latent construct are selected, whereas in standardization, standard norms are specified.

Phase A requires the developer to review all existing scales that could serve the purpose of the intended instrument, judging their use without maximizing deficiencies before proceeding. Feasibility considerations include time, cost, scoring, administration method, intrusiveness, and consequences of false-positive and false-negative decisions. The construct must be operationalized by specifying a model of internal structure, external relationships, potential indicators, and construct-related processes.

Phase B involves choosing between open or closed-ended response formats, with Likert scaling being the most common in psychology. The number of response options (typically 5 to 9 points), midpoint inclusion, and anchor labeling all affect measurement quality. Phase C generates an initial item pool (typically 3-4 times the final scale size) from sources including focus groups, theory, existing research, expert opinion, and clinical observation. Phase D evaluates items through expert panel review using the Content Validity Ratio (CVR) and pilot testing. Phase E conducts validation studies in large, representative samples to assess dimensionality via exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis.

The overall process is iterative: scales are administered, analyzed, revised, and readministered multiple times before psychometric properties are acceptable. A set of standards for assessing standardized tests has been published jointly by the AERA, APA, and NCME (1999, 2014).

## Key Points

- Five-phase framework: Construct Definition, Response Specifications, Item Generation, Item Evaluation, Psychometric Testing (@kyriazos2018)
- Test development precedes standardization; norming follows item selection
- Initial item pool should be 3-4 times larger than the final scale
- The process is inherently iterative with multiple revision cycles
- AERA/APA/NCME jointly publish authoritative standards for test assessment
