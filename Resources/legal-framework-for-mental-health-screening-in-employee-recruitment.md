## Summary

Mental health screening in employee recruitment is governed by strict legal frameworks that restrict when and how such assessments can be used. Under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) in the United States, employers may not ask job applicants to answer medical questions or take a medical exam before making a conditional job offer. After a job offer, medical examinations (including mental health screenings) may be administered, but only if all individuals entering the same job category are required to undergo the same assessment.

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) classifies mental health screening tools as medical examinations when they seek information about physical or mental impairments or health status. This means the same ADA restrictions apply: pre-offer screening is prohibited, post-offer screening must be uniform, and results must be kept confidential in separate medical files. Personality tests and cognitive tests used for hiring purposes must be validated as job-related and consistent with business necessity under Title VII.

In the European Union, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requires explicit informed consent for processing health data, data minimization, and purpose limitation. Employers must demonstrate a lawful basis for collecting mental health information, and candidates have the right to withdraw consent at any time. The UK Equality Act 2010 similarly prohibits pre-employment health questions unless they relate to a genuine occupational requirement.

## Key Points

- ADA: No medical questions or exams before conditional job offer; post-offer must be uniform
- EEOC classifies mental health screeners as medical examinations under ADA
- Post-offer screenings apply to ALL candidates in the same job category (not selective)
- Results must be confidential and stored in separate medical files
- Tests must be validated as job-related and consistent with business necessity (Title VII)
- GDPR: Explicit consent required for health data processing; data minimization applies
- UK Equality Act 2010: Pre-employment health questions prohibited unless genuine occupational requirement
- Screening must not be used to discriminate based on disability status
