## Summary

A social construct is any object, fact, or human kind whose existence, character, or distribution depends on collective social practices, beliefs, or institutions rather than on nature alone; its defining feature is contingency — it could have been otherwise [@mallon2024]. Constructionist claims decompose into contingency, badness, and reformation sub-claims [@diazleon2015]. See [[What Is a Social Construct: Definition]].

Something becomes a social construct through two mechanisms: causal construction (social factors cause its features) and constitutive construction (social-conceptual activity is metaphysically necessary for it to be what it is) [@haslanger2012]. Searle specifies the constitutive mechanism: collective acceptance of status functions expressed by rules of the form "X counts as Y in C" [@searle1995]. Berger and Luckmann add the sociological process: reciprocal typification of habitualized actions institutionalizes, and legitimation makes institutions plausible [@berger1966; @fraser2025]. Hacking adds looping effects: classifications make up people, and human kinds respond to being classified [@hacking1999]. See [[What Constitutes a Social Construct: Mechanisms]].

The boundary runs between brute facts (independent of collective acceptance: mountains, electrons, H2O) and institutional facts (dependent on it: money, presidents, cocktail parties) [@searle1995]. Global constructionism collapses into regress, so only local claims are defensible [@boghossian2006]. Being constructed implies nothing about ease of reform, and being natural implies nothing about immutability [@diazleon2015]. See [[The Boundary of Social Construction]].

## Bottom Line

A social construct is an entity whose existence and nature depend on collective human practice rather than on nature alone. Its constitutive conditions are: (1) social or conceptual factors are causally or metaphysically involved in producing it, (2) collective acceptance or reciprocal typification sustains it, and (3) it is contingent — it could have been otherwise. The boundary is drawn by dependence on collective acceptance: brute facts fall outside; institutional facts and human kinds fall inside; global constructionism is self-undermining.

## Key Points

- Definition: grounded in, caused by, constituted by, or controlled by social factors [@mallon2024]
- Constitution: causal vs constitutive mechanisms, Searle's status functions, B&L institutionalization, Hacking's looping effects [@haslanger2012; @searle1995; @berger1966; @hacking1999]
- Boundary: brute vs institutional facts, local vs global claims, reformation fallacy [@searle1995; @boghossian2006; @diazleon2015]
- Confidence: HIGH — SEP, Searle, Berger and Luckmann, Hacking, and Haslanger converge on the same distinctions
