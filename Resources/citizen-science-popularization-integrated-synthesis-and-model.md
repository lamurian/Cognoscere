## Summary

This synthesis integrates the taxonomy of popularization approaches, theoretical frameworks, case study evidence, establishment timelines, and practical checklist into a unified model for citizen science popularization. The core insight: successful popularization requires aligning six strategic levers across three establishment phases, with theoretical mechanisms explaining why each lever works.

## Key Points

### Unified Popularization Model: Six Levers × Three Phases

| Lever | Startup (0–2 yr) | Growth (2–8 yr) | Maturity (8+ yr) |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **1. Low-Barrier Entry** | MVP task validation; 30-sec micro-tasks | Mobile apps; offline mode; multilingual | API for third-party tools; legacy task archive |
| **2. Gamification** | Basic scoring; immediate feedback | Competitions; badges; leaderboards (quality-weighted) | Cross-project reputation; volunteer-designed challenges |
| **3. Community** | Forum at launch; 5–10 founding volunteers | Mentor program; regional chapters; CoP for staff | Distributed governance; volunteer working groups |
| **4. Feedback Loops** | Real-time classification counts | Migration maps; discovery alerts; annual reports | Policy dashboards; longitudinal trend visualizations |
| **5. Institutional Partners** | 1–2 pilot sites (libraries, clubs) | Mini-grant network; train-the-trainer | Embedded in infrastructure; policy mandates |
| **6. Recognition** | Volunteer acknowledgment page | Co-authorship policy; advisory board | Collective authorship; governance roles |

### Why It Works: Theoretical Integration

**Individual level (SDT)**: Low-barrier entry → Autonomy; Scaffolded progression → Competence; Community → Relatedness [@frensley2017; @nov2014]

**Collective level (Klandermans)**: Mission communication → Collective motives; Reputation system → Reward motives; Social norms via community → Norm-oriented motives [@nov2014]

**Learning trajectory (CoP)**: Peripheral participation (simple tasks) → Full participation (advanced tasks) → Leadership (moderation/co-design) [@frensley2017; @cox2026]

**Identity (SIT)**: "Zooite"/"eBirder" identity → Collective efficacy → Persistence through challenges [@masters2019a; @koepnick2019]

### Evidence-Based Popularization Principles

1. **Start with the volunteer's existing practice**: eBird succeeded because it enhanced birding (existing hobby); Galaxy Zoo tapped into wonder (existing curiosity) [@sullivan2010; @raddick2009]

2. **Quality requires reputation, not just fun**: Intrinsic motivation drives volume; reputation drives accuracy [@nov2014]

3. **Community is infrastructure, not afterthought**: Galaxy Zoo's forum enabled serendipitous discoveries; Libraries as Hubs' CoP enabled equitable scaling [@masters2019a; @cox2026]

4. **Institutional intermediaries reach new audiences**: Libraries reached underrepresented groups; Cornell Lab reached global birders [@cox2026; @teamebird2024]

5. **Plan for 8+ years to maturity**: Galaxy Zoo (10 yr), eBird (17 yr), Foldit (10 yr), Libraries as Hubs (8 yr target) [@masters2019a; @teamebird2024; @koepnick2019; @cox2026]

6. **Governance must evolve**: Projects that don't empower volunteers stall [@nov2014]

### Practical Decision Framework

**For new initiatives**: Use the Phase-Gated Checklist (companion note) with weekly early-warning metrics.

**For funders**: Fund 3-year cycles aligned to phases; require governance evolution plan; evaluate on volunteer progression metrics, not just user counts.

**For platforms**: Build for volunteer progression (not just task efficiency); support cross-project reputation; enable volunteer-led project creation.

**For policy**: Recognize citizen science infrastructure as public research infrastructure; fund long-term maintenance, not just pilots.
