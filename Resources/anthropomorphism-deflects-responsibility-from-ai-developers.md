## Summary

Anthropomorphism has an accountability cost. In two studies, anthropomorphic language increased trust in an LLM chatbot across all measures, and participants who attributed more responsibility to the AI attributed less to the company that created it, with a strong negative correlation (r = -0.68) [@oldemburgodemello2026]. The authors describe this as a perverse incentive: anthropomorphism simultaneously boosts trust and deflects accountability from developers [@oldemburgodemello2026].

At scale, the danger compounds. When LLM-based agents mimic human conversation so convincingly that users cannot tell human from machine interlocutors, the threats of deception, manipulation and disinformation emerge at scale, without the agents possessing any true empathy or social understanding [@peter2025].

This is the empirical backbone of the neutrality position: persona is not merely a trust feature, it is a mechanism that redistributes blame and can manufacture false competence in exactly the domains where accountability matters most.

## Key Points

- Responsibility displacement: more blame on the AI, less on the developer (r = -0.68) [@oldemburgodemello2026].
- Perverse incentives: trust and accountability move in opposite directions [@oldemburgodemello2026].
- Indistinguishable human-like conversation enables deception, manipulation and disinformation at scale [@peter2025].
