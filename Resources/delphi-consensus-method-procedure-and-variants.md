## Summary

The Delphi method is a structured technique for obtaining group consensus from a panel of experts through multiple rounds of anonymous questionnaires with controlled feedback between rounds. Originally developed in the 1950s by researchers at the RAND Corporation for military forecasting, it was named after the Oracle of Delphi from ancient Greek mythology (@shang2023, @nasa2021).

The method has four defining characteristics: (1) Anonymity — panelists do not know who else is participating, preventing dominance by senior or outspoken members; (2) Iteration — the process involves multiple rounds (typically 2-3), allowing participants to revise their judgments; (3) Controlled feedback — after each round, researchers present aggregated statistical summaries (usually medians and interquartile ranges) of group responses to each panelist; (4) Statistical aggregation — group responses are summarized using measures of central tendency and dispersion (@shang2023).

The classic Delphi proceeds as follows: Round 1 typically involves open-ended questions or a literature-derived item pool. Panelists rate items on Likert scales (commonly 5 or 9 points). After Round 1, researchers calculate medians, interquartile ranges, and percentage agreement for each item. Items meeting the predefined consensus threshold (commonly 70-80% agreement or median >=7 on a 9-point scale) are retained; others may be revised or dropped. Subsequent rounds present the aggregated results alongside each panelist's own previous responses, allowing reconsideration. The process stops when consensus is reached or a predetermined number of rounds is completed (@nasa2021, @diamond2014).

Several variants exist. The "modified Delphi" incorporates in-person elements (workshops, interviews) typically in Round 1, then follows classic procedure. The "policy Delphi" intentionally promotes dissensus rather than consensus, useful for exploring controversies. The "e-Delphi" is conducted entirely online, offering geographic flexibility and cost savings. @shang2023 note that despite widespread use, methodological quality varies greatly: approximately 26% of Delphi studies do not define consensus, and over 70% do not use consensus achievement as a stopping criterion, instead running for a predetermined number of rounds.

## Key Points

- Four defining features: anonymity, iteration, controlled feedback, statistical aggregation (@shang2023)
- Originated at RAND Corporation in the 1950s for military forecasting (@nasa2021)
- Typically 2-3 rounds; consensus commonly defined as 70-80% agreement or median >=7 on 9-point scale
- Modified Delphi adds in-person elements; policy Delphi promotes dissensus; e-Delphi is fully online
- Consensus definition is inconsistently reported — 26% of studies do not define it (@diamond2014)
- Panel size typically 8-23 for stability; heterogeneous panels preferred for broader topics (@shang2023)
