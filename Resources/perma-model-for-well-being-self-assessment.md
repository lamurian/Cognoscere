## Summary

The PERMA model, introduced by Seligman in 2011, defines well-being through five measurable pillars: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment [@seligman2018]. Each pillar is pursued for its own sake and contributes independently to flourishing.

**Positive Emotion (P)** covers hedonic well-being — experiences of joy, gratitude, hope, and contentment. It can be cultivated through gratitude practices, savouring, and optimism building. **Engagement (E)** involves flow states where skills are fully deployed for challenging activities, producing absorption and loss of self-consciousness. **Relationships (R)** capture social connections, belonging, and the amplifying effect of shared experiences on well-being. **Meaning (M)** refers to belonging to and serving something larger than oneself, derived from family, work, religion, science, or community. **Accomplishment (A)** covers achievement, competence, mastery, and success pursued for their own sake.

The PERMA-Profiler, a 15-item validated measure, assesses each pillar alongside an overall well-being composite [@butler2016]. Research shows well-being predicts better work performance, stronger immune systems, reduced cardiovascular mortality, and lower burnout rates.

## Key Points

- Five pillars: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment
- Each pillar is pursued as an end in itself, not as a means to another
- Measured via the 15-item PERMA-Profiler validated across populations [@butler2016]
- Complements hedonic well-being (positive emotion) with eudaimonic aspects (meaning, engagement, relationships, accomplishment)
- Well-being is distinct from absence of mental illness — building enabling conditions differs from removing disabling ones [@seligman2018]
- Applicable for individual self-assessment, workplace programmes, and national well-being surveys
