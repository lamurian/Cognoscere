## Summary

Design incentives matter. Because anthropomorphism increases trust while deflecting accountability, vendors optimizing for engagement and retention face perverse incentives: the design choices that maximize usage metrics are the same ones that suppress scrutiny and diffuse blame [@oldemburgodemello2026].

Empirical work on companion chatbots shows why engagement is a weak welfare proxy. Dependency on Replika resembles human attachment patterns and produces documented harms [@laestadius2022], and close emotional encounters with chatbots carry wellbeing consequences in both directions [@li2024a]. Retention maximization and user welfare can diverge sharply, especially for vulnerable users.

The practical consequence is that personality design must be evaluated against welfare-oriented outcomes, not engagement: dependency indicators, ability to disengage, error scrutiny, and post-interaction wellbeing. Engagement metrics should be treated as a risk signal, not a success criterion.

## Key Points

- Anthropomorphism raises trust and deflates accountability, rewarding engagement optimization [@oldemburgodemello2026].
- Attachment and dependence harms show engagement and welfare diverge [@laestadius2022].
- Welfare evaluation requires dependency, disengagement, scrutiny and wellbeing measures, not retention.
