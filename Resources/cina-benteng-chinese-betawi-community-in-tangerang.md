## Summary

The Cina Benteng (Fort Chinese) are descendants of Chinese traders who settled in Tangerang, west of Jakarta, over six centuries ago. The name derives from a Dutch VOC fort built in the 1670s along the Cisadane River, around which the community gathered. Today, they form the largest Peranakan Chinese sub-group in Indonesia, with an estimated population of 400,000-480,000 concentrated in Tangerang and surrounding areas.

The community's history begins with the legendary landing of Tjen Tjie Lung (Halung) at Teluk Naga in 1407, leading approximately 300 junks and 30,000 followers. According to the Sundanese chronicle "Tina Layang Parahyangan," these settlers intermarried with local Sundanese women, creating the Peranakan tradition. A second wave arrived in the 18th century as Qing dynasty loyalists, forming the "Seven Great Families" (setsei): Oey, Ong, Li, Lim, Teng, The, and Tan.

The Cina Benteng played a complex role in Dutch colonial history. The Dutch pursued a policy of "One harmony between 3 races" (Chinese, Dutch, and Sundanese-Betawi), creating a hybrid Peranakan class loyal to colonial authority. Some Cina Benteng served as "kapitein Tionghoa" (Chinese captains) and were prominent landowners. However, post-independence brought upheaval: the 1946 anti-Chinese riots destroyed homes and property, and after the Dutch withdrawal in 1950, many families lost their estates. A significant exodus followed to the Netherlands and the United States.

Under Suharto's New Order (1967-1998), public expressions of Chinese culture were suppressed. The Cina Benteng preserved their traditions quietly at home. Since Reformasi, a cultural revival has emerged through walking tours, heritage museums, fashion projects, and community foundations like Cide Kode Benteng.

## Key Points

- **Origins**: 1407 landing at Teluk Naga; two settlement waves (15th and 18th century)
- **Name**: From Dutch VOC fort (benteng) built 1670s along Cisadane River
- **Population**: 400,000-480,000 in Tangerang area
- **Languages**: Hokkien, Tangerang Sundanese, Betawi, Cina Benteng creole (no longer fluent in Chinese)
- **Religions**: Confucianism, Taoism, Buddhism, Christianity, Islam
- **Culture**: Manchu-style weddings, Cokek dance, gambang kromong music, kecap (sweet soy sauce) production
- **Landmarks**: Boen Tek Bio temple (1684), Museum Benteng Heritage, Pasar Lama cultural district
- **Identity**: "Hitachi" (hitam tapi Cina / tan but Chinese) — unique hybrid identity
