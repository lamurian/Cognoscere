## Summary

The WHO Psychological First Aid (PFA) guide, published in 2011 by WHO, the War Trauma Foundation, and World Vision International, provides the evidence-based framework for delivering humane, supportive, and practical help to people suffering serious crisis events [@worldhealthorganization2011]. The guide was developed specifically for use in low- and middle-income countries and can be taught to humanitarian workers within one day for immediate use. It is endorsed by 24 international agencies.

The guide covers: listening without pressing the person to talk; assessing a person's needs and concerns; helping ensure basic physical needs are met; providing or mobilizing social support; and protecting people from further harm. It emphasizes support for vulnerable groups including separated children, adolescents, people with disabilities, and those at risk of discrimination or violence [@worldhealthorganization2011].

The project's reference to 'anjuran WHO (2011)' directly maps to this guide. The PFA framework is relevant because it was designed for non-specialist first responders in LMIC settings, making it appropriate for adaptation into a self-service kiosk that delivers immediate coping strategies. The guide reflects 'emerging science and international consensus on how to provide basic support to people in the immediate aftermath of extremely stressful events' [@worldhealthorganization2011].

## Key Points
- Published 2011 by WHO, War Trauma Foundation, and World Vision International
- Designed specifically for LMIC contexts, teachable in one day
- Endorsed by 24 international agencies
- Core principles: listen, assess needs, ensure physical needs, mobilize social support, protect from harm
- Directly referenced by the project as 'anjuran WHO (2011)'
- Provides the evidence foundation for the kiosk's immediate coping aid functionality
