## Summary

Human-like design cues in AI agents measurably increase trust and engagement. In a two-study experiment, participants interacting with an LLM chatbot that used anthropomorphic language showed higher trust across all measured dimensions than with a neutral chatbot, and the chatbot's perceived emotional attunement fully mediated this effect [@oldemburgodemello2026]. A 2x2 experiment with 163 participants found anthropomorphism raised all three trust dimensions and the intention to follow chatbot advice in low-stakes contexts, with trust increasing by over 30% [@kummer2026].

A meta-analysis of 11,053 individuals across 108 independent samples confirms that anthropomorphism generally facilitates customer intention to use AI service agents, though the effect is not uniform and depends on agent characteristics and service context [@blut2021].

The consistent finding is that voice and persona are not cosmetic. They are functional trust mechanisms that shape whether users accept or scrutinize AI output. This is the empirical core of the pro-personality position.

## Key Points

- Anthropomorphic language raised trust across all measures; emotional attunement was the full mediator [@oldemburgodemello2026].
- Trust gains of over 30% and higher advice-following intention in low-stakes contexts [@kummer2026].
- Meta-analysis of 11,053 individuals, 108 samples: anthropomorphism generally facilitates adoption, with contextual moderation [@blut2021].
