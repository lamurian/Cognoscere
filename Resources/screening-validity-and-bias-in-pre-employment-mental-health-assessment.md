## Summary

The diagnostic accuracy of mental health screening tools varies across settings and populations, creating important validity considerations for pre-employment applications. Martin-Key et al. (2022) conducted a systematic review of 28 digital mental health assessment validation studies and found substantial methodological limitations across the evidence base, including high rates of patient selection bias (43% high risk), unclear index test protocols (46% unclear risk), and variable reference standard administration.

Key validity concerns include: (1) screening tools validated in clinical populations may perform differently in occupational populations with lower base rates of mental disorders; (2) sensitivity and specificity values established in healthcare settings may not generalize to recruitment contexts where the cost of false positives (exclusion of qualified candidates) and false negatives (missed support needs) have different implications; and (3) cultural and linguistic adaptations may affect psychometric properties across diverse candidate pools.

The sensitivity-specificity tradeoff in recruitment screening must be interpreted carefully. In low-prevalence occupational populations, even tools with high specificity will produce substantial false positive rates (low positive predictive value). For example, the WSQ (Web Screening Questionnaire) showed specificity 0.89-0.97 but positive predictive values of only 0.02-0.33 in general population screening. This means a significant proportion of positive screens will not correspond to actual disorders, reinforcing the need for clinical follow-up rather than screening-based exclusion decisions.

## Key Points

- 43% of validation studies had high risk of patient selection bias (Martin-Key 2022)
- Sensitivity/specificity from clinical populations may not generalize to occupational settings
- Low base rates in recruitment populations produce low positive predictive values
- WSQ example: specificity 0.89-0.97 but PPV only 0.02-0.33 in general population
- Cultural/linguistic adaptations affect psychometric properties across diverse candidate pools
- False positive rate is critical in recruitment — each exclusion removes a potentially qualified candidate
- Clinical follow-up after positive screen is essential to avoid inappropriate exclusion
- Screening tools are not standalone diagnostic instruments for hiring decisions
