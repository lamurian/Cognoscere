## Summary

Follow-up after a positive mental health screen during recruitment requires a structured pathway that connects candidates with appropriate care rather than using screening results as exclusion criteria. Best practices are organized across three dimensions: clinical escalation, confidentiality management, and decision-making protocols.

Clinical escalation follows a stepped model. For low-risk positive screens (e.g., PHQ-9 score 10-14 with no safety concerns), the candidate receives information about Employee Assistance Programs (EAPs), mental health resources, and the option for a voluntary clinical consultation. For moderate-risk screens (e.g., PHQ-9 15-19 or GAD-7 15-21), a clinical interview with an occupational health professional is recommended before finalizing placement. For high-risk screens (e.g., any suicidal ideation on C-SSRS, severe depression score >=20), immediate crisis intervention referral is required and placement may be deferred pending clinical stabilization.

Confidentiality is paramount. Screening results must be stored in confidential medical files separate from personnel records. Hiring managers should receive only functional information (e.g., 'fit for duty with accommodations' or 'requires accommodation') rather than diagnostic details. The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) and HIPAA guidelines both require that individual screening results not be shared with non-medical personnel.

Decision-making should be accommodation-focused rather than exclusion-focused. The ADA requires employers to consider reasonable accommodations that would enable a candidate to perform essential job functions. A positive mental health screen should trigger an interactive accommodation process, not an automatic rejection. Research from occupational health practice shows that candidates who disclose and receive accommodations show comparable performance and retention rates to those without accommodations.

## Key Points

- Stepped escalation: low-risk (EAP info), moderate-risk (clinical interview), high-risk (crisis intervention)
- Low-risk: PHQ-9 10-14 or GAD-7 10-14 without safety concerns
- Moderate-risk: PHQ-9 15-19 or GAD-7 15-21 — clinical interview before placement
- High-risk: C-SSRS positive for ideation, PHQ-9 >=20, or severe anxiety — immediate crisis referral
- Confidential medical files separate from personnel records
- Hiring managers receive only functional information, not diagnoses
- ADA requires interactive accommodation process, not automatic exclusion
- Accommodated candidates show comparable performance and retention rates
