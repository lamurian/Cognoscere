## Summary

Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL) instruments define the domain structure for self-assessed health status. Two instruments are used most widely: the EQ-5D and the SF-36, each offering a distinct taxonomy of health dimensions.

The **EQ-5D** (EuroQol Group, 1990) defines health across five dimensions: Mobility, Self-Care, Usual Activities (work, study, housework, leisure), Pain/Discomfort, and Anxiety/Depression [@euroqolgroup1990]. Each dimension has 3 (EQ-5D-3L) or 5 (EQ-5D-5L) levels of severity, generating 243 or 3,125 unique health states. These states are converted into a single utility index value (0=dead to 1=full health) for use in cost-effectiveness analysis (QALY calculation).

The **SF-36** (Ware and Sherbourne, 1992) measures eight health domains: Physical Functioning, Role Limitations due to Physical Health, Bodily Pain, General Health Perceptions, Vitality (Energy/Fatigue), Social Functioning, Role Limitations due to Emotional Problems, and Mental Health [@ware1992]. These eight domains aggregate into two component summaries: Physical Component Summary (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS).

## Key Points

- EQ-5D: 5 dimensions (Mobility, Self-Care, Usual Activities, Pain/Discomfort, Anxiety/Depression) with single utility index
- SF-36: 8 domains aggregated into Physical and Mental Component Summaries
- Both instruments are generic (not disease-specific) enabling cross-population comparisons
- EQ-5D is designed for economic evaluation (QALY); SF-36 for descriptive health profiling
- Their domains map to the biopsychosocial model: physical, psychological, and social functioning
- Together they define the most widely used taxonomy of health self-assessment in clinical and population research
