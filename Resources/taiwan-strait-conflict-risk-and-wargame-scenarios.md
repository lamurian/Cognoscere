## Summary

Taiwan Strait tensions represent the most dangerous potential flashpoint for great-power war in the next decade. CSIS conducted 26 wargames examining a Chinese blockade of Taiwan, finding that while China could inflict serious hardship — particularly by targeting Taiwan's energy sector — blockade would not be "a low-risk, low-cost option" for Beijing (@heginbotham2025). The games illustrated risks to China, Taiwan, Japan, and the United States, with potential for the greatest naval battles since World War II.

The CNAS "Dangerous Straits" wargame found that in a Taiwan conflict scenario, neither Beijing nor Washington would likely hold the upper hand after the first week, suggesting protracted conflict with consequences far beyond what Beijing initially anticipated. CSIS and Rhodium Group studies estimate trillions of dollars of global output at risk in a prolonged Taiwan Strait crisis.

A July 2026 Congressional war game simulated a 2027 scenario where China launches massive military exercises around Taiwan, leaving U.S. officials uncertain whether Beijing was being coercive or imposing a de facto blockade. The scenario tested AI-enabled cyberattacks and gray-zone operations, reflecting evolving Chinese military doctrine.

The 2025 DoD China report emphasizes that the Russia-Ukraine war has demonstrated how "mixed competition" shapes military planning. China is studying the conflict to refine its own approaches, with greater coordination between military and civilian instruments. The NDU Strategic Assessment notes that Taiwan's defense preparations — including asymmetric capabilities and hardened infrastructure — remain critical to deterrence (@lynchiii2025).

## Key Points

- CSIS 26-wargame study: Chinese blockade of Taiwan risky, not low-cost
- CNAS wargame: neither side gains upper hand after week one — protracted conflict likely
- Global economic risk: trillions of dollars at stake in Taiwan Strait disruption
- 2027 scenario tested: massive Chinese exercises, AI-enabled cyberattacks, gray-zone operations
- China studying Russia-Ukraine war to refine "mixed competition" doctrine
