## Summary

Disclosure of AI interaction is becoming law. Article 50 of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), applicable from 2 August 2026, requires that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed so that persons are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person [@futureoflifeinstitute2024].

Providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must ensure outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Deployers must disclose deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, and the information must be provided in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of first interaction, conforming to accessibility requirements [@futureoflifeinstitute2024].

This legalizes the transparency plank of the neutrality position: simulated personality is not a private design choice, it is a regulated representation. The 'unless obvious' carve-out for chatbots is a judgment call, not a blanket exemption.

## Key Points

- Users must be informed they are interacting with AI unless it is obvious [@futureoflifeinstitute2024].
- Synthetic content must be machine-readably marked; deepfakes and public-interest AI text must be disclosed [@futureoflifeinstitute2024].
- Disclosure must be clear, distinguishable and accessible at first interaction; in force 2 August 2026.
