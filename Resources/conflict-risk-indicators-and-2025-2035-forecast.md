## Summary

Multiple indicators suggest elevated conflict risk over the next decade, though the probability and nature of conflict remain contested. The NDU Strategic Assessment 2025 frames the current era as dominated by three great powers — the U.S., China, and Russia — in competitive and confrontational dynamics that have replaced cooperative interactions of the post-Cold War era (@lynchiii2025). The transition from cooperation to competition happened rapidly over the past decade.

The Institute for Economics and Peace identifies several structural risk factors: increasing number of state-based armed conflicts (up to 56 in 2023, highest since 1990s), growing military expenditures globally, and the erosion of arms control frameworks. The collapse of INF Treaty and suspension of New START create unregulated nuclear competition. RAND research finds that competition in secondary theaters — Africa, Latin America, Middle East — carries escalation risks where counterterrorism and great-power dynamics intersect (@cohen2023a).

Key conflict scenarios ranked by probability and impact: (1) Taiwan Strait crisis — highest potential for great-power war; (2) NATO-Russia escalation in Eastern Europe — ongoing from Ukraine war spillover; (3) Middle East proxy conflicts — Iran-Israel tensions drawing in great powers; (4) Arctic competition — resource and route disputes between NATO and Russia/China; (5) South China Sea confrontation — territorial disputes with economic implications.

The Munich Security Report's "depolarization" thesis argues that institutional reform could reduce conflict risk, but competing order models make reform unlikely. The most probable trajectory is continued great-power competition short of direct military conflict, punctuated by proxy conflicts, economic coercion, and information warfare. Direct great-power war remains low probability but catastrophically high impact.

## Key Points

- 56 state-based armed conflicts in 2023 — highest since 1990s
- Arms control collapse: INF Treaty gone, New START suspended
- Five ranked scenarios: Taiwan, NATO-Russia, Middle East proxies, Arctic, South China Sea
- Most probable: continued competition short of direct war, proxy conflicts, economic coercion
- Direct great-power war: low probability but catastrophically high impact
