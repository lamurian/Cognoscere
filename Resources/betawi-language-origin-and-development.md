## Summary

Betawi is a Malay-based creole spoken in and around Jakarta, Indonesia. Its origin is debated among linguists: some consider it an inherited Malay vernacular descended from Proto-Malayic, while others argue it developed as a creole. One theory holds that descendants of Chinese men and Balinese women in Batavia converted to Islam and spoke a pidgin that was later creolized, then decreolized with Sundanese and Javanese elements [@grijns1991]. Another view attributes Betawi's development to the isolation of the Batavia settlement from Sundanese and Javanese-speaking interiors by physical barriers (swamp and forest) and administrative boundaries.

The language emerged in the 16th century from Market Malay, absorbing vocabulary from multiple contact languages. The Betawi ancestors came from various ethnicities and cultures, bringing linguistic heritage that formed the basis of the language. The vocabulary mix includes Malay (35%), Javanese/Sundanese (22%), and significant contributions from Arabic, Chinese (Hokkien), Portuguese, and Dutch [@siregar2023].

Betawi replaced the earlier Portuguese creole of Batavia known as Mardijker. The language is now classified as seriously endangered, with traditional varieties mostly spoken by older generations in locations on Jakarta's outskirts such as Kampung Melayu, Pasar Rebo, Pondok Gede, Ulujami, and Jagakarsa.

## Key Points

- Malay-based creole with debated origin: inherited Malay vs. Chinese-Balinese pidgin theory
- Vocabulary: 35% Malay, 22% Javanese/Sundanese, plus Arabic, Chinese, Portuguese, Dutch contributions
- Emerged 16th century from Market Malay in Batavia (Jakarta)
- Replaced earlier Portuguese creole (Mardijker)
- Seriously endangered; traditional varieties spoken mainly by older generations
- Two main dialects: Urban (Betawi Kota) and Suburban (Betawi Ora/Pinggiran)
