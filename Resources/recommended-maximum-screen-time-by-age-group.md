## Summary

Screen time recommendations from major pediatric associations have evolved significantly. In January 2026, the AAP retired its long-standing hourly screen time limits in favor of a holistic 'digital ecosystem' approach that considers content quality, context, and individual child characteristics. However, the WHO (2019) and updated AAP guidance still provide baseline thresholds for younger age groups where evidence is strongest.

For children under 18 months, the recommendation remains zero screen time (excluding video chatting). Children aged 18-24 months may have limited exposure to high-quality content with caregiver co-viewing. Preschoolers aged 3-5 should be limited to approximately one hour per day of high-quality programming. For school-age children (6-12) and adolescents (13-18), the AAP no longer prescribes fixed hourly limits, instead recommending families use the '5 C's' framework and develop individualized Family Media Plans.

The shift from rigid time limits reflects research showing that what children do on screens matters more than how long they use them. Passive scrolling carries different risks than video-chatting with friends or creating digital art. The AAP emphasizes that screen time should be evaluated in context: what activities it displaces, how it affects sleep, whether it supports or undermines social connections, and whether parents model healthy digital behaviors. The key recommendation across all age groups is to prioritize sleep, physical activity, and face-to-face interaction while developing age-appropriate digital literacy.

## Key Points

| Age Group | AAP (2026) | WHO (2019) |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| 0-18 months | Avoid (video chat OK) | Avoid |
| 18-24 months | Limited, co-viewed | Limited, co-viewed |
| 2-5 years | ~1 hour, high-quality | ≤1 hour |
| 6-12 years | No fixed limit (5 C's) | ≤2 hours |
| 13-18 years | No fixed limit (5 C's) | ≤2 hours |

- AAP's 5 C's: Character, Context, Cutoffs, Calm zones, Co-viewing
- Content quality matters more than raw hours for ages 6+
- Sleep, physical activity, and face-to-face interaction are priorities at all ages
- AAP Family Media Plan tool recommended for household planning
- Parental modeling of healthy screen use is essential across all age groups
