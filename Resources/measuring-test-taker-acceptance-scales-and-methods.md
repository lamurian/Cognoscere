## Summary

Three validated instruments provide structured approaches to measuring proctoring acceptance. The Akzept-L questionnaire (Kersting, 2008), used by Denker et al. (2023) with 2,052 participants, measures three core dimensions: face validity, controllability, and absence of strain. It uses 4 items per dimension on a 6-point Likert scale with strong reliability (alpha 0.76-0.82). The scale is supplemented by items measuring concentration (alpha 0.70), privacy (alpha 0.76), and prior information (r = 0.38) [@denker2023]. The Akzept accounts for 15-29% of variance in overall test evaluation.

The Student Online Proctoring Experience Scale (SOPES), developed by Woldeab & Brothen (2026), is a 12-item instrument with three factors derived from EFA (N=321) and confirmed via CFA (N=123): (1) Exam Quality and System Fairness (4 items), (2) Proctoring Anxiety and Concerns (5 items), and (3) Preference for and Benefits of Online Proctoring (3 items). Model fit was acceptable (CFI = 0.90, RMSEA = 0.08). Inter-factor correlations showed Exam Quality was negatively associated with Proctoring Anxiety (r = -0.50) and positively with Proctoring Benefits (r = 0.40) [@woldeab2026].

Jiang et al. (2023) used an extended TAM-based questionnaire with 760 participants, measuring 6 constructs: perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, social presence, place presence, social influence, and online proctoring acceptance (alpha > 0.75 for all scales). The structural model explained 72.8% of variance in online proctoring acceptance [@jiang2023].

Andreou et al. (2021) used a mixed-methods approach: a post-test 6-item questionnaire (alpha = 0.72) with exploratory factor analysis (KMO = 0.63) yielding two factors (appreciation and emotional distress), plus open-ended questions analysed thematically across four themes [@andreou2021].

## Key Points

- Akzept-L: 12 items measuring face validity, controllability, absence of strain
- SOPES: 12 items, three factors (Exam Quality, Proctoring Anxiety, Proctoring Benefits)
- TAM-based instruments explain up to 73% of variance in acceptance
- Mixed-methods approaches combine quantitative scales with qualitative thematic analysis
- All instruments show acceptable reliability (alpha > 0.70)
