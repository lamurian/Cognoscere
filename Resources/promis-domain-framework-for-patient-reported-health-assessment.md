## Summary

The Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) is an NIH Roadmap initiative that developed a standardised domain framework for measuring patient-reported health across clinical populations [@cella2007]. PROMIS organises health into three primary domains — Physical Health, Mental Health, and Social Health — each with multiple subdomains measured by item banks.

**Physical Health** includes physical function (mobility, dexterity, strength), pain (intensity, interference, behaviour), fatigue, sleep disturbance, and sexual function. **Mental Health** includes depression, anxiety, anger, positive affect, cognitive function (executive function, memory), and psychological well-being. **Social Health** includes ability to participate in social roles and activities, social isolation, companionship, and satisfaction with social roles.

The PROMIS-29 v2.0 profile, a widely used short form, covers 7 domains (physical function, fatigue, pain interference, pain intensity, depression, anxiety, sleep disturbance) plus a single pain intensity item [@hays2018]. Each domain is scored on a T-score metric (mean=50, SD=10) referenced to the US general population, enabling cross-condition and cross-study comparability.

## Key Points

- Three primary domains: Physical Health, Mental Health, Social Health
- Each domain has validated subdomains with item banks using item response theory
- PROMIS-29 v2.0 profile covers 7 core domains with T-score standardisation
- Designed for computerised adaptive testing to reduce respondent burden
- Scores referenced to US general population (mean=50, SD=10)
- Applicable across diseases and conditions, making it a universal taxonomy for patient-reported assessment
- Physical health summary (PCS) and mental health summary (MCS) scores are derived from the profile [@hays2018]
