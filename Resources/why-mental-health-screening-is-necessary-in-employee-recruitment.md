## Summary

Mental health screening in recruitment is necessary for three intersecting reasons: economic impact, workforce stability, and duty of care. The economic rationale is driven by the scale of mental health-related productivity loss. The World Health Organization estimates that depression and anxiety cost the global economy $1 trillion per year in lost productivity. From an employer perspective, the costs manifest as absenteeism (days missed), presenteeism (reduced output while at work), turnover (recruitment and training costs for replacements), and workers' compensation claims. Workplace mental health promotion programmes consistently show returns of $1.50 to $7 per dollar invested, making early identification cost-effective.

Workforce stability is threatened by poor person-job fit. When employees enter roles that exceed their psychological coping capacity, the result is accelerated burnout, disengagement, and voluntary turnover. The cost of replacing a single employee ranges from 50% to 200% of annual salary depending on role complexity. Pre-employment mental health assessment helps predict which candidates are likely to thrive versus struggle, reducing the revolving door of talent. Research shows that accommodated employees — those who receive targeted support based on assessment findings — demonstrate comparable performance and retention rates to employees without accommodations.

Duty of care requires employers to take reasonable steps to protect employee wellbeing. Organisations have a legal and ethical obligation to ensure the work environment does not exacerbate existing mental health conditions. Screening at recruitment provides a baseline for ongoing workplace health management and enables early intervention. This is particularly critical for high-stress roles where unmanaged mental health conditions could pose safety risks to the individual, colleagues, or the public.

## Key Points

- Depression and anxiety cost the global economy $1 trillion/year in lost productivity (WHO)
- Workplace mental health programmes return $1.50-$7 per $1 invested
- Employee replacement costs: 50-200% of annual salary depending on role
- Pre-employment screening predicts person-job fit, reducing burnout and turnover
- Accommodated employees show comparable performance and retention to non-accommodated peers
- Duty of care: employers must take reasonable steps to protect employee wellbeing
- Screening provides baseline for ongoing workplace health management
- Particularly critical for high-stress roles with safety implications
