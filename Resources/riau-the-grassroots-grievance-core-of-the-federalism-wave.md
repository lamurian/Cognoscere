## Summary

Riau is the epicenter of the current federalism wave, and the grievances behind it are well-documented and specific. Speakers at the Forum Riau Federal point to the discovery of 724 million barrels of oil in the Pertamina Hulu Rokan working area yielding little benefit for Riau, and to decades of agricultural exports through Dumai port that have not translated into local prosperity [@nur2025]. Constitutional-law scholar Husnu Abadi (UIR) catalogues the deeper pattern: central government extraction of natural resources justified by "the wealth belongs to all Indonesians", non-transparent central-regional revenue sharing, and a string of laws — most recently the Omnibus Law — that pull licensing and other regional authority back to the center [@abadi2025].

The area's grievances have deep roots. The 2001 Kongres Rakyat Riau II already debated independence, federalism, and wider autonomy as options; the 1958 PRRI revolt had regionalist origins in the same region [@abadi2025; @bertrand2019]. The current movement is deliberately more moderate: the DIR push (Aceh-style special autonomy, an Otsus model giving up to 70% of resource revenue and local symbols) is led by the Malay Customary Council, and the BPP DIR campaign explicitly insists its struggle is constitutional and rejects any link to separatism [@abadi2025]. The "federal within NKRI" framing is itself a strategic choice: it borrows the Aceh precedent — which Abadi argues already shifted Indonesia toward "something closer to federalism" — and uses it as a template for other resource-rich regions [@abadi2025].

These grievances align with measurable regional-spatial inequality in Indonesia and with the institutional response tradition: since 1999 the state's answer to regional discontent has been broad autonomy and asymmetric special autonomy rather than federalism — a position the MPR still defends (federalism "not compatible" with Indonesia; autonomy as the middle path against the "Balkan virus") [@edy2012; @abadi2025].

## Key Points

- Specific fiscal grievances: oil revenues (PHR 724M barrels), Dumai export hub, opaque revenue sharing, Omnibus Law recentralization.
- Historical roots: PRRI (1958) and Kongres Rakyat Riau (2001) precedents in the same region.
- Aceh's special autonomy is the explicit model; activists demand parity or "daerah istimewa" status.
- Most organized actors (LAMR/DIR, BPP DIR) insist on constitutional means within NKRI, distancing themselves from separatism.
- The movement is grievance-driven and localized (Riau plus other resource-rich regions), not a uniform national demand.
