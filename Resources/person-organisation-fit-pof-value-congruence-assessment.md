## Summary

Person-Organisation Fit (POF) measures the degree of congruence between an individual's values and those of the organisation. The most widely used measure is the Perceived Person-Organisation Fit scale by Cable and Judge (1996), a brief 3-item self-report instrument that directly assesses subjective perceptions of value alignment.

### Question Items

The Cable and Judge (1996) POF scale uses a 5-point Likert scale (1 = not at all, 5 = completely). The three items are:

1. "To what degree do feel your values match or fit this organization and the current employees in this organization?"
2. "My values match those of the current employees in this organization."
3. "Do you think the values and personality of this organization reflect your own values and personality?"

### Scoring and Interpretation

Scores are the mean of the three items, ranging from 1.00 to 5.00. Higher scores indicate greater perceived fit. The scale is unidimensional with high internal consistency (coefficient alpha = .87). No formal clinical cutoffs exist; scores are interpreted relative to comparison groups or organisational norms.

### Validity Evidence

Meta-analytic evidence from Kristof-Brown, Zimmerman, and Johnson (2005) shows POF predicts job satisfaction (rho = .51), organisational commitment (rho = .51), and turnover intentions (rho = -.35). POF is positively correlated with person-job fit, indicating convergent validity between different fit constructs. The scale has been validated across organisational entrants, current employees, and job choice decision contexts.

## Key Points

- Cable and Judge (1996) POF scale: 3 items, 5-point Likert, alpha = .87
- Measures perceived value congruence between individual and organisation
- Predicts job satisfaction (rho = .51), commitment (rho = .51), turnover intentions (rho = -.35)
- No formal cutoffs; interpreted against normative data
- Distinct from person-job fit, which measures skill-ability match
