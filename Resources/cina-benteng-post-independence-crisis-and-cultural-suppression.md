## Summary

The transition from Dutch colonial rule to Indonesian independence brought severe hardship to the Cina Benteng community. On June 23, 1946, anti-Chinese riots erupted in Tangerang, triggered when a Chinese NICA soldier lowered the Indonesian flag and replaced it with the Dutch flag. Mobs, supported by pro-independence fighters, ransacked Chinese homes and stole ancestral artifacts including meja abu (ash tables used in ancestor worship rituals).

The Pao An Tui — a pro-NICA Chinese youth militia — evacuated surviving Cina Benteng to Batavia. When they eventually returned, they found their homes destroyed and estates confiscated. The violence created lasting trauma that shaped community identity for generations.

Under Suharto's New Order (1967-1998), Chinese cultural expression was systematically suppressed. Chinese-language education was banned, public festivals restricted, and many traditions practiced only in private. The Cina Benteng, already partially assimilated into Betawi and Sundanese culture, became even more culturally invisible. The 1998 Reformasi brought renewed freedom, but also fresh trauma during the May 1998 anti-Chinese riots (Krismon 98), which pushed some families to emigrate.

Today, a cultural revival is underway. Young Cibens (Cina Benteng) are reclaiming their heritage through walking tours, heritage museums, fashion projects, and community organizations like the Cide Kode Benteng Foundation. The challenge is preserving a culture that has already lost its ancestral language while maintaining distinct identity from both mainstream Chinese Indonesians and indigenous Betawi.

## Key Points

- 1946 anti-Chinese riots: homes ransacked, ancestral artifacts stolen
- Pao An Tui militia evacuated survivors to Batavia
- Post-1950: estates confiscated, community impoverished
- Suharto era (1967-1998): Chinese cultural expression banned
- 1998 Reformasi: renewed freedom but fresh Krismon trauma
- Current revival: walking tours, Museum Benteng Heritage, fashion projects
- Identity challenge: preserving culture without ancestral language
