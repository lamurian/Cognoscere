## Summary

Social health instruments measure perceived social support, loneliness, social connectedness, social network size, and interpersonal functioning. This catalog documents 12 widely validated instruments. These instruments map primarily to ICF Activities and Participation (interpersonal interactions and relationships) and Social Capital frameworks.

## Instrument Catalog

**1. MSPSS (Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support)**
Authors: Zimet et al., 1988. 12 items across 3 subscales: Family (4 items), Friends (4 items), Significant Other (4 items). 7-point Likert (1-7). Mean per subscale and total. alpha=0.88-0.95. Translated and validated in 40+ languages. Manual: freely available in original publication.

**2. UCLA Loneliness Scale (Version 3)**
Authors: Russell, 1996. 20 items, 4-point Likert (1-4). Total 20-80. Higher scores = greater loneliness. Cutoffs vary by population; general population mean ~40. alpha=0.89-0.94. Gold standard loneliness measure. Manual: freely available in original publication.

**3. UCLA Loneliness Scale Short Form (ULS-8)**
Authors: Hays & DiMatteo, 1987. 8 items from original scale, 4-point Likert. Total 8-32. alpha=0.84. Suitable for brief assessments and large surveys. Manual: freely available.

**4. MOS Social Support Survey (MOS-SSS)**
Authors: Sherbourne & Stewart, 1991. 19 items across 4 functional subscales: Emotional/Informational (8 items), Tangible (4 items), Affectionate (3 items), Positive Social Interaction (4 items). 5-point Likert (1-5). Mean score per subscale. alpha=0.91-0.97. Manual: freely available.

**5. Lubben Social Network Scale-6 (LSNS-6)**
Authors: Lubben et al., 2006. 6 items assessing family and friend networks. 0-5 scale. Total 0-30. Cutoff <=12 indicates social isolation. alpha=0.77-0.89. Designed for older adults. Manual: freely available.

**6. Social Connectedness Scale (SCS)**
Authors: Lee & Robbins, 1995. 20 items, 6-point Likert (1-6). Total 20-120. Higher scores = stronger connectedness. alpha=0.91-0.94. Measures sense of belonging and interpersonal bonds. Manual: freely available.

**7. Interpersonal Support Evaluation List (ISEL)**
Authors: Cohen et al., 1985. 40 items across 4 subscales: Companionship (10), Tangible (10), Appraisal (10), Belonging (10). 4-point scale (definitely true to definitely false). Total and subscale scores. alpha=0.88-0.90. Manual: freely available.

**8. Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN)**
Authors: Connor et al., 2000. 17 items, 0-4 scale. Total 0-68. Cutoffs: >=20 mild, >=30 moderate, >=40 severe social phobia. alpha=0.94. Manual: freely available.

**9. Inventory of Interpersonal Problems (IIP-32)**
Authors: Barkham et al., 2001. 32 items across 8 subscales, 0-4 scale. Total mean score. alpha=0.84-0.90. Measures interpersonal difficulties across dominance, nurturance, affiliation, and control. Manual: https://www.pearsonclinical.com/

**10. Social Avoidance and Distress Scale (SADS)**
Authors: Watson & Friend, 1969. 28 items (14 social avoidance, 14 distress), yes/no format. Each subscale 0-14. High scores indicate social avoidance and distress. alpha=0.85-0.88. Manual: freely available.

**11. Perceived Social Support from Family and Friends (PSS-Fa and PSS-Fr)**
Authors: Procidano & Heller, 1983. 10 items per scale, 3-point format (yes, no, don't know). Each scored 0-10. Higher = more perceived support. alpha=0.76-0.88. Manual: freely available.

**12. Bergen Social Media Addiction Scale (BSMAS)**
Authors: Andreassen et al., 2016. 6 items, 1-5 scale (very rarely to very often). Total 6-30. Cutoff >=19 suggests problematic social media use. alpha=0.88. Maps social interaction to digital contexts. Manual: freely available.

## Key Points

- MSPSS and MOS-SSS measure perceived social support from different perspectives (global vs. functional)
- UCLA Loneliness Scale captures subjective experience of social isolation
- LSNS-6 specifically targets social network size in older populations
- SCS and ISEL measure broader social connectedness and interpersonal functioning
- Social health instruments map to ICF Activities and Participation (interpersonal interactions, community life)
- Digital-era instruments like BMAS extend social health measurement to online contexts
