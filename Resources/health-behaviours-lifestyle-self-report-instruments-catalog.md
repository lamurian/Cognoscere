## Summary

Health behaviour instruments measure physical activity, dietary habits, sleep quality, substance use, and health-promoting behaviours. This catalog documents 12 widely validated instruments. These instruments partially map to ICF Environmental Factors and Health-Related Quality of Life frameworks, and are central to preventive medicine and public health.

## Instrument Catalog

**1. HPLP-II (Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile II)**
Authors: Walker et al., 1995. 52 items across 6 subscales: Health Responsibility (9), Physical Activity (8), Nutrition (9), Spiritual Growth (9), Interpersonal Relations (9), Stress Management (8). 4-point Likert (never to routinely). Mean per subscale and total. alpha=0.71-0.92. Manual: freely available.

**2. IPAQ (International Physical Activity Questionnaire, Short Form)**
Authors: Craig et al., 2003. 7 items assessing vigorous, moderate, and walking activity over past 7 days. Score: MET-minutes/week (vigorous*8 + moderate*4 + walking*3.3). Categories: low (<600), moderate (600-3000), high (>3000 MET-min/week). alpha=0.78. Manual: https://www.ipaq.ki.se/

**3. IPAQ Long Form**
Authors: Craig et al., 2003. 27 items covering activity at work, transport, domestic/garden, and leisure. Same scoring as short form with greater detail. alpha=0.81. Manual: https://www.ipaq.ki.se/

**4. PSQI (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index)**
Authors: Buysse et al., 1989. 19 items generating 7 component scores: Subjective Sleep Quality, Sleep Latency, Sleep Duration, Habitual Sleep Efficiency, Sleep Disturbances, Use of Sleep Medication, Daytime Dysfunction. Global score 0-21. Cutoff >5 indicates poor sleep quality. alpha=0.70-0.83. Manual: freely available.

**5. AUDIT (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test)**
Authors: WHO, 1989. 10 items, 0-4 scale. Total 0-40. Cutoffs: 0-7 low risk, 8-15 hazardous, 16-19 harmful, >=20 probable dependence. alpha=0.82. Manual: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9241592345

**6. Fagerstrom Test for Nicotine Dependence (FTND)**
Authors: Heatherton et al., 1991. 6 items assessing nicotine dependence. Score 0-10. Cutoffs: 0-2 low, 3-4 medium, 5-10 high dependence. alpha=0.61-0.75. Manual: freely available.

**7. DFEQ (Dutch Eating Behaviour Questionnaire)**
Authors: van Strien et al., 1986. 33 items across 3 subscales: Emotional Eating (13), External Eating (10), Restrained Eating (10). 5-point Likert (never to very often). Subscale means. alpha=0.80-0.92. Manual: freely available.

**8. MEDFICTS (Dietary Screener)**
Authors: Project MARK Research Group, 1997. 8 items assessing dietary fat, fiber, and fruit/vegetable intake. 5-point frequency scale. Score classified as low, moderate, or high dietary fat. alpha=0.75. Manual: freely available.

**9. Rapid Eating Assessment for Participants-Shortened Version (REAP-S)**
Authors: Gans et al., 2006. 13 items assessing eating behaviors and dietary intake. 5-point scale. Score classified as healthful, somewhat healthful, or unhealthful. alpha=0.72. Brief clinical screener. Manual: freely available.

**10. Stanford Self-Efficacy for Managing Chronic Disease Scale**
Authors: Lorig et al., 1989. 6 items, 1-10 scale. Mean score (6-60). alpha=0.91. Predicts health behaviors including exercise, medication adherence, and diet. Manual: http://patienteducation.stanford.edu/

**11. Exercise Self-Efficacy Scale (ESES)**
Authors: McAuley, 1993. 12 items, 0-100% confidence ratings. Mean score. alpha=0.88-0.96. Measures confidence in ability to exercise regularly. Manual: freely available.

**12. Sleep Hygiene Index (SHI)**
Authors: Mastin et al., 2006. 13 items assessing sleep hygiene practices. 5-point Likert (never to always). Total 13-65. Higher = poorer sleep hygiene. alpha=0.72. Brief behavioural screener. Manual: freely available.

## Key Points

- HPLP-II is the most comprehensive health-promoting lifestyle instrument with 6 domains
- IPAQ is the international standard for physical activity surveillance in population surveys
- PSQI bridges health behaviours (sleep) and physical health outcomes
- AUDIT is the WHO's recommended alcohol screening instrument
- Self-efficacy scales (Stanford, ESES) predict actual health behaviour change
- Dietary instruments range from brief screeners (REAP-S) to detailed assessments
