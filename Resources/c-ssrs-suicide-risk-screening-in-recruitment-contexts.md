## Summary

The Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) is a clinician-administered or self-report instrument for assessing suicidal ideation and behavior. The screener version asks about wish to be dead, non-specific active suicidal thoughts, suicidal ideation with method, suicidal ideation with intent, and suicidal ideation with plan, plus actual attempt and interrupted attempt.

The C-SSRS is included in recommended mental health assessment batteries for workplace screening because it provides structured safety risk evaluation. When used in employee recruitment or post-offer assessments, the C-SSRS serves as a critical safety protocol: any endorsement of suicidal ideation (beyond passive wishes to be dead) requires immediate clinical escalation and crisis intervention referral.

In recruitment contexts, the C-SSRS is not used as a hiring gatekeeping tool. Instead, it functions as a safety net within the assessment process. A positive screen triggers a safety protocol that may include immediate clinical interview, crisis resource referral, and safety planning. The goal is to ensure the candidate receives appropriate support, not to exclude them from employment consideration. Employers must have clear clinical escalation pathways before administering any tool that assesses suicide risk.

## Key Points

- Clinician-administered or self-report; 5-level ideation severity plus behavior items
- Screener version covers: wish to be dead, non-specific thoughts, ideation with method, intent, plan
- Functions as safety protocol within broader assessment battery
- Any endorsement beyond passive wish to be dead triggers immediate clinical escalation
- NOT a hiring gatekeeping tool — serves to connect candidates with crisis support
- Requires established clinical escalation pathways before use
- Must be administered by trained professionals or integrated into platforms with clinical oversight
