## Summary

Screen time in children produces measurable effects across physical, cognitive, emotional, and social domains. The evidence base spans multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses published 2020-2025, converging on several consistent findings. Screen exposure exceeding 2 hours per day is associated with obesity, sedentary behavior, depression, myopia, behavioral disturbances, and sleep problems @gonzlezprez2025.

The AAP (2016, 2022) and WHO (2019) recommend no screen time for children under 2, 1 hour/day for ages 2-5, and consistent limits for children over 6 @kar2025. The 2026 AAP guidance replaced fixed hourly limits for children over 6 with a context-based '5 C's' framework. However, epidemiological evidence indicates risk becomes clinically meaningful at 3+ hours daily.

The relationship between screen time and outcomes follows dose-response patterns in most domains. Short-term effects include sleep disruption, mood changes, and reduced attention. Long-term effects include neurodevelopmental changes, obesity risk accumulation, and mental health consequences. Content type and context moderate these effects substantially @souza2025.

## Key Points

- Screen time impacts are bidirectional: excessive use causes harm, but controlled educational use offers benefits
- Effects are strongest in children under 6 due to critical developmental windows
- Parental mediation and co-viewing significantly modify outcomes
- Sleep disruption amplifies impact at all ages
- Socioeconomic disparities affect both exposure levels and intervention access
