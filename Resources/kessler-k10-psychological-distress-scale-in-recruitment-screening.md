## Summary

The Kessler Psychological Distress Scale (K10 and its shorter variant K6) is a 10-item (or 6-item) self-report instrument measuring non-specific psychological distress over the past 30 days. Items assess fatigue, nervousness, restlessness, hopelessness, depression, and feelings of worthlessness on a 5-point scale (1 = none of the time to 5 = all of the time). K10 total range: 10-50. Severity bands: 10-19 likely to be well, 20-24 likely to have a mild disorder, 25-29 likely to have a moderate disorder, 30-50 likely to have a severe disorder.

Kessler and colleagues developed the K6 and K10 for use in large epidemiological surveys. The Australian National Survey of Mental Health and Well-Being validated the K10 against the CIDI (Composite International Diagnostic Interview), finding strong discrimination between clinical and non-clinical populations. The K6 (shorter variant) performs comparably to the K10 in most settings.

The K10 is particularly useful in occupational health screening because it measures general distress rather than specific disorders, making it appropriate as a broad initial screener in recruitment contexts. Its brevity (5 minutes) and established normative data make it practical for large-scale employee assessments. Like other screening tools, a K10 score >=20 indicates the need for clinical follow-up rather than serving as a hiring decision tool.

## Key Points

- K10: 10 items; K6: 6 items; 30-day recall; total 10-50 (K10) or 6-30 (K6)
- K10 bands: 10-19 well, 20-24 mild, 25-29 moderate, 30-50 severe distress
- Validated against CIDI in Australian National Survey of Mental Health and Well-Being
- Measures non-specific psychological distress (not disorder-specific)
- Useful as broad initial screener due to non-specific nature
- 5-minute administration time suitable for large-scale screening
- K10 >=20 indicates need for clinical follow-up
