## Summary

The Resilience Scale for Adults (RSA) measures protective factors presumed to facilitate adaptation to psychosocial adversity, going beyond the individual to include family and social resources. The 33-item version uses a 7-point semantic differential format to reduce response bias [@friborg2003; @friborg2005]. It is one of only two measures among adult resilience scales that assess protective factors across multiple levels [@windle2011].

Internal validity: confirmatory factor analysis confirms a six-factor structure: Perception of Self, Planned Future, Social Competence, Family Cohesion, Social Resources, and Structured Style, with two second-order dimensions (intrapersonal and interpersonal) [@morote2017]. In a Peruvian community sample (n=805), the six-factor CFA fit well (RMSEA=.037, SRMR=.047, CFI=.91, TLI=.90). Internal consistency: Norwegian alphas .67-.81 with total .88; Peruvian subscale alphas .48 (Structured Style) to .80 (Family Cohesion) with total .90; Belgian total .84, Brazilian total .88 [@morote2017]. The Structured Style factor is consistently the weakest subscale (alpha .48-.67) and should be interpreted cautiously.

External validity: test-retest reliability is the highest among the top-rated instruments, with Norwegian subscale retest correlations of .73-.80 and total .84 (all subscales above .70 except social support, .69) [@morote2017; @windle2011]. The RSA total and its factors predict anxiety (30%) and depression (37%) variance beyond demographic variables and stressful life events, with Perception of Self the strongest unique predictor [@morote2017]. It correlates positively with sense of coherence and personality factors and negatively with anxiety and depression across Norwegian, Belgian, Brazilian, Lithuanian, Italian, and Iranian samples [@morote2017]. Women score higher on Social Resources and Social Competence; men higher on Perception of Self [@morote2017].

The RSA scored a top rating (7/18) in the Windle et al. (2011) quality review and achieved the maximum score on internal consistency (33-item version) and test-retest reliability [@windle2011]. The developers allow free use of the scale for research and clinical purposes; items are published in the original papers [@friborg2003; @friborg2005].

## Key Points

- 33 items, 7-point semantic differential format
- Six factors: Perception of Self, Planned Future, Social Competence, Family Cohesion, Social Resources, Structured Style
- CFA fit (Peru, n=805): RMSEA=.037, SRMR=.047, CFI=.91 [@morote2017]
- Internal consistency: total alpha .88-.90; subscales .48-.81; Structured Style weakest
- Test-retest: subscales .73-.80, total .84 (best of top-rated scales) [@morote2017; @windle2011]
- Predicts 30% anxiety and 37% depression variance beyond demographics and life stress [@morote2017]
- Only top-3-rated measure covering family and social protective factors [@windle2011]
- Free for research/clinical use; items published in open papers [@friborg2003]
