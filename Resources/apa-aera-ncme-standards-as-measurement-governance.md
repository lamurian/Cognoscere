## Summary

The Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing, published jointly by the American Educational Research Association (AERA), the American Psychological Association (APA), and the National Council on Measurement in Education (NCME), constitutes the authoritative governance framework for test development and use in the United States and globally. First published in 1954 and most recently updated in 2014, the Standards establish professional and technical standards for tests, testing practices, and the rights and responsibilities of test takers and test users.

The Standards organize validity evidence into five sources: (1) evidence based on test content, (2) evidence based on response processes, (3) evidence based on internal structure, (4) evidence based on relations to other variables, and (5) evidence based on consequences of testing. This framework has become the dominant model for validation practice in psychology and education, replacing older unitary conceptions of validity.

The Standards address the full lifecycle of testing: test design and development, scoring and reporting, test use and interpretation, and rights and responsibilities. They establish that validity is not a property of the test itself but of the interpretations and uses of test scores for particular purposes. This contextual view means that a single instrument may be valid for one purpose but not another, requiring ongoing validation evidence.

The AERA/APA/NCME framework has influenced international standards and is referenced by the COSMIN initiative and other national measurement bodies. It serves as the primary reference for ethical and technical standards in psychological assessment, educational testing, and clinical measurement.

## Key Points

- AERA/APA/NCME Standards are the authoritative governance framework for testing
- Five sources of validity evidence: content, response processes, internal structure, relations to other variables, consequences
- Validity is contextual: property of interpretations, not the test itself
- Addresses full lifecycle: design, development, scoring, reporting, use
- Influenced international standards including COSMIN
