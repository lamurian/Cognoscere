## Summary

Construct proliferation is the accumulation of ostensibly different but potentially identical constructs representing organizational or psychological phenomena. @shaffer2015 identify this as a salient problem in contemporary research, noting that while numerous construct validation procedures exist, relatively few validation studies conduct comprehensive assessments of the discriminant validity of theoretically distinct constructs.

The problem manifests when researchers fail to adequately test whether their new construct is empirically distinguishable from pre-existing, conceptually related constructs. This can occur when important pre-existing constructs are left out of validation studies, or when measurement error falsely implies distinct constructs by artificially lowering observed correlations. The result is a proliferation of constructs that may be measuring the same underlying phenomenon under different names.

@jebb2021 express concern about this problem, noting that scholars are quite adept at theoretically distinguishing a new construct from a prior one, but empirical methods should only be enlisted after theoretical distinctiveness has been established. They recommend that scale developers provide sufficient arguments for both the construct's importance and its distinctiveness.

## Key Points

- Construct proliferation is the accumulation of similar constructs under different names (@shaffer2015)
- Discriminant validity testing is often insufficient in validation studies
- Measurement error can artificially create the appearance of distinct constructs
- Theoretical distinction alone is insufficient; empirical distinctiveness must be demonstrated
- @jebb2021 recommend arguing for both importance and distinctiveness of new constructs
