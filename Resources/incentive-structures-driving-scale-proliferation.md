## Summary

Scale proliferation is driven by structural incentive structures in academic research that reward new instrument development as a publishable contribution. The publish-or-perish system creates a market dynamic where the supply of new scales may exceed genuine measurement demand. Developing and validating a new instrument constitutes a discrete, citable output, making it an attractive research activity even when existing instruments could serve the same purpose (@shaffer2015, @jebb2021).

Several interrelated factors compound this problem. Academic career advancement metrics count publications and citations, incentivizing novelty over replication or refinement of existing instruments. Journal editorial preferences for original instruments over validation studies of existing measures further reinforce this pattern. The result is that researchers face strong professional incentives to create new constructs from existing ones with only minor conceptual distinctions.

The consequences extend beyond academic waste. Construct proliferation creates confusion about what is actually being measured, undermines cross-study comparison, and fragments the evidence base for important psychological and organizational phenomena. When multiple instruments exist for ostensibly the same construct, researchers must choose among them without clear guidance about which provides the most valid measurements, and this choice can substantially affect research findings.

Some scholars have proposed structural solutions, including requiring researchers to justify why existing instruments are insufficient before new development is permitted, establishing registries of validated instruments, and developing consensus guidelines (like COSMIN) that standardize evaluation procedures to reduce redundant validation efforts.

## Key Points

- Publish-or-perish incentives reward new instrument development as a discrete output (@shaffer2015)
- Academic career metrics count publications and citations, incentivizing novelty
- Journal preferences for original instruments over validation studies reinforce proliferation
- Construct proliferation fragments evidence bases and undermines cross-study comparison
- Proposed solutions include justification requirements, registries, and consensus guidelines
