## Summary

Two primary instruments assess psychological resilience: the Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC) and the Brief Resilience Scale (BRS). The CD-RISC measures broader resilience-promoting factors, while the BRS specifically measures bounce-back ability — the speed and ease of recovery from stress.

### CD-RISC

The CD-RISC exists in three authorized versions: 25-item, 10-item, and 2-item scales. The CD-RISC-25 measures five factors: (1) personal competence, high standards, and tenacity; (2) trust in one's instincts, tolerance of negative affect, and strengthening effects of stress; (3) positive acceptance of change and secure relationships; (4) control; and (5) spiritual influences. Each item is rated on a 5-point Likert scale from 0 (not true at all) to 4 (true nearly all the time). Total scores range from 0-100 for the 25-item version and 0-40 for the 10-item version, with higher scores indicating greater resilience. The CD-RISC-10 is now preferred due to its superior unidimensional factor structure (Campbell-Sills & Stein, 2007). No formal clinical cutoffs are published; scores are interpreted relative to normative samples. The CD-RISC-25 scored highest (7/18) in the Windle et al. (2011) quality review, but requires written permission from the copyright holder [@windle2011].

### BRS

The Brief Resilience Scale is a 6-item measure of bounce-back ability developed by Smith et al. (2008). Three items are positively worded and three negatively worded (reverse-scored). Respondents rate agreement on a 5-point scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree). The score is the mean of all six items, ranging from 1.00 to 5.00. Descriptive interpretation bands proposed by the developers (Smith et al., 2013): below 3.00 = low resilience, 3.00-4.30 = normal resilience, above 4.30 = high resilience. These are descriptive bands, not validated clinical cutoffs. Internal consistency ranges from alpha .80 to .91. The BRS correlates moderately with the CD-RISC (r = .59), indicating they measure related but distinct constructs. It is unidimensional once item-wording method effects are controlled and is freely usable with citation [@chmitorz2018; @rodrguezrey2016].

### Comparison

The CD-RISC captures a broader set of resilience resources (competence, trust, acceptance, control, spirituality), while the BRS isolates the recovery process itself. The CD-RISC is the strongest overall measure but requires permission from the copyright holders; the BRS is free with citation and shares a top psychometric rating (7/18) in the Windle et al. (2011) review. For license-free assessment see the dedicated per-instrument profiles below.

## Key Points

- CD-RISC-25: 25 items, 5 factors, 0-100 score range; CD-RISC-10 preferred for unidimensional measurement
- BRS: 6 items, unidimensional, 1.00-5.00 mean score
- BRS interpretation: <3.00 low, 3.00-4.30 normal, >4.30 high (descriptive, not clinical)
- BRS items 2, 4, 6 are reverse-scored (subtract from 6)
- BRS correlates r = .59 with CD-RISC — related but distinct constructs
- CD-RISC requires permission; BRS is free with citation
- Windle et al. (2011): CD-RISC, RSA, and BRS are the top-rated measures; no gold standard exists [@windle2011]
