## Summary

Gaming performance between the Retroid Pocket Nova and Steam Deck differs dramatically based on the type of games being played. For retro console emulation, the Nova offers competitive or superior performance in a pocketable form factor. For modern PC games, the Steam Deck dominates through native x86 execution.

**Retro emulation performance**: The Nova handles PlayStation 2 emulation at 3-4x upscaled resolution with stable frame rates, along with GameCube, Dreamcast, PS1, and N64 at high settings. The 4:3 display provides pixel-perfect output for pre-widescreen-era consoles without black bars. The Nova's Adreno 740 GPU with Turnip drivers delivers strong Vulkan performance for emulation workloads. The Steam Deck can also emulate these systems via RetroArch or standalone emulators, but its larger 16:10 display introduces letterboxing on 4:3 content [@retrododo2026].

**PC gaming via GameNative**: The Nova can run select PC titles through GameNative, an open-source compatibility layer using Box86/Box64 and Proton-derived translation. Performance depends heavily on the game's requirements. Lightweight 2D titles (Terraria, Stardew Valley) run at 60+ FPS. Demanding 3D titles (Valheim, Path of Exile 2) face severe limitations or are effectively unplayable. GameNative 1.2 introduced automatic CPU/GPU power tuning via PID controllers, improving battery and thermal management on the Nova's hardware. The Steam Deck runs the same PC games natively through Proton with no translation overhead, typically delivering 30-60 FPS on modern titles at its native 800p resolution [@sofialindstrm2026].

**Benchmark context**: The Nova scores approximately 3,711 in 3DMark Wild Life Extreme (mobile benchmark), while the Steam Deck scores 3,500-3,936 in 3DMark Time Spy (PC benchmark). These benchmarks use different test suites and are not directly comparable, but they indicate the Nova's mobile GPU is competitive in its class while the Steam Deck's desktop-class GPU handles more demanding workloads. The Retroid Pocket 6 (same SoC class as Nova) achieved 1,518,895 in AnTuTu v10, confirming strong mobile performance [@sofialindstrm2026].

**Battery impact**: Demanding emulation on the Nova drains its 5,000mAh battery in 3-5 hours, while the Steam Deck's larger 50Wh battery sustains 3-12 hours depending on game demands. The Nova supports 27W fast charging for quick top-ups, which partially compensates for the smaller cell.

## Key Points
- Nova excels at retro emulation (PS2 3-4x upscale, GameCube, Dreamcast, N64, PS1)
- Steam Deck excels at modern PC gaming (native x86, Proton compatibility)
- GameNative on Nova: 60+ FPS for lightweight 2D PC games, limited for demanding 3D
- Steam Deck runs same PC games natively with no translation overhead
- Nova's 4:3 display is ideal for retro content; Steam Deck's 16:10 adds letterboxing
- Battery life: Nova 3-5hr heavy use vs Steam Deck 3-12hr range
- Both support fast charging; Nova at 27W, Steam Deck at higher wattage
