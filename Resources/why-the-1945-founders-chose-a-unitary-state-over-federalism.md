## Summary

Indonesia's founding constitution-makers considered federalism and rejected it. During the BPUPK drafting sessions of 1945, the debate over the form of state turned on two arguments documented by Omara and Tauda: the bitter memory of Dutch colonial rule, which made national strength through unity the overriding goal, and Soepomo's “negara integralistik” (integralistic state) theory, which held that state and people form one organic family with no dichotomy between them [@omara2022]. The drafters voted 17 to 2 for a unitary state — only Latuharhary and Maria Ulfah voted for federalism, with Hatta, Rajiman, Sukiman, and Kusuma Atmaja also sympathetic until Soekarno persuaded Hatta [@omara2022].

Article 1(1) of the 1945 Constitution declared: “The State of Indonesia is a unitary state in the form of a republic,” and the Preamble's fourth principle (“Unity of Indonesia”) reinforced it [@omara2022; @bertrand2019]. One factor is striking: conventional federal theory expects plural societies to choose federalism, yet Indonesia deliberately chose a unitary state to bind its diverse archipelago together.

Diversity was not ignored — it was routed through decentralisation. Article 18 divided Indonesia into large and small regions governed "by observing the basis of deliberation... and the rights of origin in special areas," and its elucidation recognised roughly 250 self-governing indigenous communities as special regions [@omara2022]. This design choice — unitary in form, decentralised in administration, with room for special regions — set the pattern for every later response to regional diversity, including the post-1998 autonomy laws [@omara2022; @bertrand2019].

## Key Points

- The founders voted 17–2 for a unitary state in 1945; Soekarno personally persuaded Hatta.
- Colonial trauma and Soepomo's integralistik state (unity of leaders and people) drove the choice.
- A plural, archipelagic society chose unity over federalism, contradicting textbook federal theory.
- Diversity was accommodated through Article 18 decentralisation and recognition of special regions, not through territorial federalism.
