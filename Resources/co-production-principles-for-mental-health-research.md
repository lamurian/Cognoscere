## Summary

Co-production is the approach in which researchers, practitioners and the public work together sharing power and responsibility from start to end of a project, including knowledge generation [@nationalinstituteforhealthandcareresearch2024]. NIHR guidance identifies five key principles: sharing of power, including all perspectives and skills, respecting and valuing the knowledge of all, reciprocity, and building and maintaining relationships. Joint ownership of key decisions differentiates co-production from mere collaboration [@nationalinstituteforhealthandcareresearch2024].

Mental Health Europe's co-creation framework, developed with its Co-creation Task Force of experts by experience, adds operational depth with seven principles: inclusivity, equality, respect, reciprocity, power-sharing, accessibility, and transparency [@mentalhealtheurope2023]. The framework is grounded in a human-rights and psychosocial model, treating people with psychosocial disabilities as subjects of rights rather than objects of care — embodied in the motto 'Nothing about us, without us' [@mentalhealtheurope2023].

Concrete practice is illustrated by the Peace of Mind relapse prevention manual, authored by a person with over 30 years of lived experience and refined through structured PPI review by patients, carers and peer workers before formal evaluation [@khan2025]. The case demonstrates both the power of lived-experience leadership and the necessity of iterative, evidence-informed refinement — reviewers rated content and structure as needing improvement, leading to co-produced revisions [@khan2025].

## Key Points

- **NIHR five principles**: (1) sharing of power — research jointly owned; (2) including all perspectives and skills; (3) respecting and valuing all knowledge equally; (4) reciprocity — everybody benefits; (5) building and maintaining relationships [@nationalinstituteforhealthandcareresearch2024].
- **Key features**: ground rules, continuing dialogue, joint ownership of key decisions, commitment to relationship building, personal growth opportunities, flexibility, continuous reflection, and evaluating impact [@nationalinstituteforhealthandcareresearch2024].
- **MHE seven principles**: inclusivity, equality, respect, reciprocity, power-sharing, accessibility, transparency [@mentalhealtheurope2023].
- **MHE eleven assessment indicators**: cover preparation, implementation and evaluation phases — from inclusive stakeholder mapping and secured resources to group agreements and co-created evaluation [@mentalhealtheurope2023].
- **Practical translation**: inclusive stakeholder mapping, checking what participants need to participate, transparent resource allocation, group agreements on safe environments and conflict management, plain-language materials, and scheduling around participants' circumstances [@mentalhealtheurope2023].
- **Power dynamics**: researchers should shift from 'doers' to facilitators; power sharing does not mean everyone decides everything — roles remain, but key decisions are jointly owned [@nationalinstituteforhealthandcareresearch2024].
- **Co-production is not consultation**: consultation invites input while researchers retain control; co-production requires devolved decision-making and shared ownership of the research itself [@nationalinstituteforhealthandcareresearch2024].
- **Lived-experience-led example**: Peace of Mind manual co-produced with a lived-experience author, refined via PPI feedback on structure, tone, length and inclusivity before clinical piloting [@khan2025].
