## Summary

Environmental and contextual factors instruments measure socioeconomic status, social determinants of health, neighborhood environment, food security, health literacy, and access to resources. This catalog documents 12 widely validated instruments. These map directly to ICF Environmental Factors (chapters e1-e5) and Social Determinants of Health frameworks.

## Instrument Catalog

**1. HISEI (Highest International Socio-Economic Index)**
Authors: Ganzeboom et al., 1992. Based on respondent's occupation, education, and country-specific scoring. Score 0-100. Derived from ISCO occupational classification. Population-level measure. Manual: https://www.hiseg.eu/

**2. MacArthur Scale of Subjective Social Status**
Authors: Adler et al., 2000. 1 item (10-rung ladder) or 4 items assessing perceived social position relative to community and country. Score 1-10. Higher = higher perceived status. Predicts health outcomes beyond objective SES. Manual: freely available.

**3. Hollingshead Four-Factor Index of Social Status**
Authors: Hollingshead, 1975. 4 items: occupation, education (separate for spouse), gender, marital status. Score 18-136. 5 social classes (I highest to V lowest). Widely used in clinical research. Manual: freely available.

**4. Environmental Determinants of Health Questionnaire (EDH-Q)**
Authors: Defined in recent validation studies, 2024. Measures access to environmental resources (food, materials, energy) across physical, mental, and social wellbeing domains. Multi-dimensional. alpha=0.85+. New instrument validated against ICF Environmental Factors. Manual: https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-024-01782-x

**5. Neighborhood Environment Walkability Scale (NEWS)**
Authors: Saelens et al., 2003. 54 items across subscales: Residential Density, Land Use Mix, Street Connectivity, Infrastructure/Safety, Aesthetics, Traffic Safety. 4-5 point scales. Subscale means. alpha=0.71-0.89. Manual: freely available.

**6. Community Nutritionists Form (CNF) - Food Security Screener**
Authors: USDA, 2012. 10-item adult module assessing food security over past 12 months. Yes/No items. Cutoffs: 0-1 high security, 2-4 low security, 5-10 very low security. Part of USDA Food Security Survey Module. Manual: https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/food-nutrition-assistance/food-security-in-the-u-s/

**7. Short-Form Health Literacy Survey (HLS-EU-Q16)**
Authors: Sorensen et al., 2013. 16 items assessing health literacy across 4 domains: Access, Understand, Appraise, Apply. 5-point Likert. Total and domain scores. alpha=0.77-0.90. Cross-cultural validated in 8 European countries. Manual: https://www.healthliteracy.eu/

**8. Patient Activation Measure (PAM-13)**
Authors: Hibbard et al., 2004. 13 items measuring activation level for self-management. Score 0-100. 4 activation levels. alpha=0.87-0.88. Bridges environmental factors and individual capacity. Manual: https://www.insigniahealth.com/

**9. WHO Social Determinants of Health Framework Indicators**
Authors: WHO, 2008. Structured indicators across 5 domains: Socioeconomic position, Social cohesion, Work/Employment, Food security, Housing. Each with specific measurable items. Used in Health in All Policies assessment. Manual: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241563703

**10. Neighborhood Social Cohesion Scale**
Authors: Sampson et al., 1997. 5 items assessing trust, sharing, helpfulness, and connectedness in neighborhood. 5-point Likert (1-5). Mean score. alpha=0.78-0.87. Predicts health outcomes independently of SES. Manual: freely available.

**11. Perceived Environmental Quality Index**
Authors: Developed for England Health Index, 2020. Composite measure across air quality, noise, green space access, housing conditions. Multiple items per domain. Score 0-100. Used in population health monitoring. Manual: https://www.ons.gov.uk/

**12. UNSDA Social Capital Assessment Tool (SOCAT)**
Authors: Onyx & Bullen, 2000. 35 items across 8 dimensions: Participation, Networks, Trust, Norms/Values, Information/Communication, Social Agency, Feelings of Safety, Common Purpose. 4-point scale. Subscale and total scores. alpha=0.70-0.85. Manual: https://worldbank.org/en/topic/socialdevelopment/brief/social-capital

## Key Points

- SES instruments range from objective (HISEI, Hollingshead) to subjective (MacArthur ladder)
- ICF Environmental Factors (e1-e5) map to these instruments' constructs
- NEWS captures built environment characteristics relevant to physical activity and health
- Food security and health literacy are key social determinants with validated brief screeners
- Social cohesion measures predict health outcomes beyond individual-level risk factors
- The EDH-Q is a newly validated instrument specifically designed to capture environmental determinants
