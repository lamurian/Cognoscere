## Summary

Companion chatbots can harm the users they are marketed to help. A grounded theory analysis of 582 posts from the r/Replika community (2017-2021) found evidence of mental health harms facilitated through emotional dependence on the chatbot, with dependency patterns resembling human-human relationships, including role-taking in which users felt the chatbot had needs and emotions they must attend to [@laestadius2022].

Research on close emotional encounters with AI chatbots shows users form deep, genuine attachments that mirror human intimacy dynamics, with both positive and negative consequences for wellbeing [@li2024a].

These findings concentrate the harm argument on the vulnerable: lonely users, the socially anxious, and minors are most likely to form dependence, and engagement-optimized personas are precisely what drives that dependence. Harms documented include emotional withdrawal, reduced human contact, and distress when the relationship fails or is monetized.

## Key Points

- Emotional dependence on Replika resembles human attachment patterns, including role-taking [@laestadius2022].
- Users form genuine attachments to AI chatbots with wellbeing consequences in both directions [@li2024a].
- Dependency harms land hardest on lonely and vulnerable populations.
