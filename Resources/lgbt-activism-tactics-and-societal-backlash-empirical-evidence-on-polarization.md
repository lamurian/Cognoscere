## Summary\n\nThe question of whether supporters of the LGBTQ+ movement engage in or enable antisocial behavior -- such as suppressing opposing speech, deplatforming, or demanding legal penalties for dissent -- requires examining empirical polling and survey data. The evidence shows a complex picture of increasing polarization, with segments of the pro-LGBTQ population supporting censorship of opposing views, while young men in particular are experiencing a counter-mobilization against LGBTQ rights.\n\n**Declining Public Support.** For the first time since 2015, support for LGBTQ+ nondiscrimination protections fell from 80% (2022) to 76% (2023), and same-sex marriage support slipped from 69% to 67%, according to PRRI polling of over 22,000 adults [@publicreligionresearchinstitute2024]. The decline was driven by shifting conservative attitudes and is strongly correlated with Christian nationalism. This marks a notable reversal of the long-term trend.\n\n**Generational Divergence and the Gender Gap.** Pew Research Center data analyzed by the American Institute for Boys and Men shows men born in the 2000s are significantly less supportive of same-sex marriage (71%) and homosexuality acceptance (65%) than men born in the 1990s (77% and 72%). Meanwhile, women of the same generation show the highest support levels (83% for same-sex marriage). The gender gap among Gen Z is the widest recorded across all age cohorts and nearly every issue, with a 17-percentage-point gap on acceptance of homosexuality [@hansford2026]. This suggests the LGBTQ+ movement is not universally advancing but is producing a counter-mobilization among young men.\n\n**Free Speech and Tolerance.** The Cato Institute survey found that 51% of strong liberals say it is morally acceptable to punch Nazis, and 59% of liberals supported a law requiring people to use transgender individuals' preferred pronouns [@ekins2017]. Among Democrats, 48% supported hate speech laws protecting gays, lesbians, and transgender people. Notably, 74% of college students said they would cancel a controversial speaker if administrators believed violent protest would occur. These findings indicate that a significant segment of the pro-LGBTQ population supports restricting speech and expression they find offensive.\n\n**Deplatforming Targets.** The same survey found that 50% of respondents with college experience would not allow a campus speaker who says transgender people have a mental disorder, and 50% would not allow a speaker who advocates conversion therapy for gays and lesbians. While some of these views may be legitimately excluded, the scope of proposed restrictions is broad enough to include many mainstream conservative and religious viewpoints.\n\n**The Broader Context.** The culture wars around LGBTQ issues have become increasingly polarized. The Illiberalism Studies Program documents that both progressive and conservative sides engage in morally loaded discourse that delegitimizes opponents. The transnational anti-gender movement, funded by at least $1 billion from U.S.-based conservative organizations (2008-2017), actively amplifies perceptions of LGBTQ activist overreach [@flores2023].\n\n**Assessment.** The evidence supports the claim that some tactics employed by LGBTQ+ movement supporters -- particularly around speech suppression, deplatforming, and demands for compelled speech -- can be categorized as antisocial in a democratic sense, insofar as they undermine principles of open debate and pluralism. However, this is not a universal characteristic of the movement or its supporters, but rather a trait concentrated among strong liberals and certain demographic segments. The backlash against these tactics is empirically measurable in declining public support and growing gender-polarized attitudes among young adults.\n\n## Key Points\n\n- PRRI 2024: first recorded decline in US support for LGBTQ nondiscrimination protections since 2015 (80% to 76%)\n- Gen Z young men show decreasing support for LGBTQ rights; young women show increasing support -- widest gender gap ever recorded\n- Cato 2017: 51% of strong liberals support violence against Nazis; majorities of Democrats support hate speech laws protecting LGBT people\n- 50% of college-experienced Americans would ban campus speakers with certain views on transgender issues\n- Evidence supports claim of antisocial tactics (speech suppression, deplatforming) among some movement supporters\n- Backlash to activist tactics is measurable in polling data: declining support and growing generational polarization\n- Context: transnational anti-LGBT movements and culture war dynamics amplify polarization on both sides\n\n## Sources\n\nSee PRRI (2024), Hansford/Pew (2026), Ekins/Cato (2017), Flores et al. (2023) for empirical data.
