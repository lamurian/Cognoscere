## Summary

Adolescents aged 13 to 18 represent the heaviest screen-using demographic, averaging 7.5 to 9 hours of daily recreational screen time according to the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry. This age group faces unique risks because screens are deeply integrated into their social lives, identity formation, and emotional regulation. The AAP's 2026 guidance explicitly moved away from hourly limits for teens, recognizing that adolescent digital experiences are shaped by diverse factors beyond hours spent online.

Mental health impacts are the most studied concern for this age group. Jing et al. @jing2025 conducted a meta-analysis of 32 studies involving over 26,000 students and found that problematic social media use was significantly associated with anxiety, depression, loneliness, low self-esteem, and fear of missing out (FoMO). The relationship is bidirectional: adolescents experiencing emotional difficulties may turn to screens for comfort, while excessive screen use can exacerbate existing vulnerabilities.

Cardiometabolic risks intensify during adolescence. Horner et al. @horner2025 found that each additional hour of screen time was associated with a 0.13 standard deviation increase in cardiometabolic risk scores among 18-year-olds, notably higher than the 0.08 SD increase observed in 10-year-olds. A distinctive 'screen-time fingerprint' of blood metabolites was identified through machine learning analysis, suggesting biological impacts beyond behavioral risk factors. Sleep disruption remains a critical pathway, with short sleepers showing markedly higher risk. The AAP recommends the '5 C's' framework: Character, Context, Cutoffs, Calm zones, and Co-viewing/co-playing, emphasizing that parents should model healthy screen behaviors rather than imposing blanket restrictions.

## Key Points

- Average recreational screen time: 7.5-9 hours/day (AACAP data)
- Recommended maximum: no fixed limit; use AAP's 5 C's framework
- Cardiometabolic risk: +0.13 SD per additional hour (higher than children)
- Mental health: significant associations with anxiety, depression, loneliness, FoMO
- 95% of teens 13-17 have a smartphone; YouTube (90%), TikTok (63%), Instagram (61%) most popular
- 72% of teens have used AI companions; some use them for emotional support
- Sleep disruption is the primary mediating pathway for cardiometabolic risk
- 5 C's: Character, Context, Cutoffs, Calm zones, Co-viewing
