## Summary

Indonesia faces a severe shortage of psychologists relative to its population, far below WHO-recommended benchmarks. As of mid-2025, HIMPSI (Himpunan Psikologi Indonesia) reports 14,904 registered psychologists nationwide [@akbar2025], serving a population of approximately 285.7 million [@unitednationsdepartmentofeconomicandsocialaffairspopulationdivision2026]. This yields a ratio of roughly **5.2 psychologists per 100,000 population**, or one psychologist per ~19,200 people.

The shortage is even more acute for clinical psychologists specifically. IPK Indonesia (Ikatan Psikolog Klinis Indonesia) reports 4,442 verified clinical psychologists as of July 2026 [@ikatanpsikologklinisindonesia2026], against a population of ~287.9 million, giving a ratio of approximately **1.5 clinical psychologists per 100,000** (one per ~64,800 people) [@unitednationsdepartmentofeconomicandsocialaffairspopulationdivision2026]. By comparison, HIMPSI reported only ~11,500 total members (including all degree levels) in 2019 [@adam2019], and IPK Indonesia had just 1,143 clinical psychologists in May 2019 — indicating significant growth but from a very low base.

The WHO recommends a ratio of 1 psychiatrist or clinical psychologist per 30,000 population, or approximately 3.3 per 100,000 [@universityofjakartainternational2024]. Indonesia's clinical psychologist density (1.5 per 100,000) falls at roughly half the recommended level. Even counting all registered psychologists (5.2 per 100,000), the figure barely exceeds the WHO standard and masks severe geographic maldistribution — most psychologists concentrate in Java, with provinces like Papua Barat and Sulawesi Barat having only 8 clinical psychologists each [@maheswara2026].

## Key Points

- **1,053 psychiatrists** (Oct 2021, ratio ~0.37 per 100,000) — far below WHO recommendation
- **14,904 registered psychologists** (May 2025, ratio 5.2 per 100,000, or 1 per ~19,200 people)
- **4,442 clinical psychologists** (July 2026, ratio 1.5 per 100,000, or 1 per ~64,800 people)
- WHO standard: 3.3 mental health professionals per 100,000 (1 per 30,000)
- Distribution highly Java-centric: 3,024 of 4,146 clinical psychologists (73%) in Java provinces
- Clinical psychologist count grew from 1,143 (2019) to 4,442 (2026) — 3.9x in 7 years, but still insufficient
