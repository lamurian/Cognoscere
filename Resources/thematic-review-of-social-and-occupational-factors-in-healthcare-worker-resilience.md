## Summary

Brooks et al. (2018) conducted a systematic thematic review of social and occupational factors associated with psychological outcomes in healthcare employees during the SARS crisis. Four literature databases were searched, yielding 22 included studies.[@brooks2018]

The review identified nine key factors affecting psychological wellbeing: occupational role, training/preparedness, high-risk work environments, quarantine, role-related stressors, perceived risk, social support, social rejection/isolation, and impact of SARS on personal or professional life.[@brooks2018]

**Social support as protective factor.** Social support consistently emerged as a protective factor across studies, buffering the psychological impact of infectious disease outbreaks. Conversely, social rejection and isolation were associated with worse psychological outcomes.[@brooks2018]

**Occupational factors.** Healthcare workers in high-risk roles (most patient contact) experienced greater psychological impact. Training and preparedness were associated with better outcomes, while quarantine and role-related stressors were associated with worse outcomes.[@brooks2018]

**Implications.** The authors recommended that healthcare workers should be prepared for the potential psychological impact of outbreaks, employers should encourage supportive workplace environments, and support should be targeted at those most at risk, particularly those with the most patient contact.[@brooks2018]

## Key Points

- 22 studies reviewed on psychological outcomes during SARS
- Nine key factors identified through thematic analysis
- Social support protective; social rejection harmful
- High-risk roles associated with greater psychological impact
- Training and preparedness improve outcomes
