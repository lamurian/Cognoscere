## Summary

The Big Five Inventory-2 (BFI-2) is a 60-item self-report personality measure developed by Soto and John (2017) as a major revision of the original Big Five Inventory. It assesses the five broad dimensions of personality — Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Negative Emotionality (Neuroticism) — each subdivided into three facets, yielding 15 facet scales within a hierarchical structure.

### Question Items

The BFI-2 uses a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Disagree Strongly, 5 = Agree Strongly). Respondents rate how much they agree with statements describing their typical behaviours and feelings. Example items include statements about being organized, sociable, cooperative, curious, and emotionally stable. The full instrument contains 60 items, with 4 items per facet scale.

### Domain and Facet Structure

The five domains and their three facets each are:

- **Openness**: Intellectual Curiosity, Aesthetic Sensitivity, Creative Imagination
- **Conscientiousness**: Organization, Productiveness, Responsibility
- **Extraversion**: Sociability, Assertiveness, Energy Level
- **Agreeableness**: Compassion, Respectfulness, Trust
- **Negative Emotionality**: Anxiety, Depression, Emotional Volatility

### Scoring and Interpretation

Domain scores are computed as the mean of the 12 items loading on each domain (4 items x 3 facets). Facet scores are the mean of the 4 items on each facet. Higher scores indicate higher levels of the trait. No clinical cutoffs exist; scores are interpreted dimensionally against normative distributions. The BFI-2 demonstrates strong internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha 0.82-0.87 across domains) and test-retest reliability over 3 months (0.76-0.84).

### Psychometric Properties

Convergent correlations with corresponding NEO-PI-R dimensions range from r = 0.80 to 0.90. Confirmatory factor analysis supports the hierarchical five-domain, 15-facet structure. Measurement invariance across gender and age groups has been established (Soto & John, 2017).

## Key Points

- 60 items measuring 5 domains and 15 facets of personality
- 5-point Likert scale response format
- Conscientiousness is the strongest personality predictor of job performance (Barrick & Mount, 1991)
- Strong internal consistency (alpha 0.82-0.87) and test-retest stability (0.76-0.84)
- No categorical cutoffs; dimensional interpretation against norms
