## Summary

Person-Job Fit (PJF) measures the compatibility between an individual's characteristics (skills, abilities, knowledge) and the demands of the job or tasks performed. Unlike Person-Organisation Fit, which focuses on value congruence, PJF focuses on the match between what a person can do and what the job requires. Two specific types are assessed: demands-abilities fit (does the person have the skills to meet job demands?) and needs-supplies fit (does the job provide what the person needs?).

### Measurement Approaches

PJF is typically assessed through multiple methods rather than a single standalone scale:

**Structured interviews** with job-analyzed questions directly assess whether candidates possess the required competencies. **Situational judgement tests** present realistic job scenarios and evaluate decision-making quality. **Job knowledge tests** measure domain-specific expertise. **Work sample tests** require candidates to demonstrate actual job tasks.

The Perceived Person-Environment Fit Scale (PPEFS) by Chuang (2015) includes PJF items that assess perceived match between personal capabilities and job requirements, typically using Likert-scale responses.

### Scoring and Interpretation

PJF is scored relative to job requirements determined through job analysis. There are no universal cutoffs; scores are interpreted against the specific competency framework for the role. Meta-analytic evidence shows PJF directly predicts task performance and job satisfaction. Kristof-Brown et al. (2005) reported that PJF correlates positively with organisational commitment and negatively with turnover intentions.

### Validity Evidence

PJF is a strong predictor of task performance because it directly measures whether the person has the capabilities to perform the job. When combined with POF, the two constructs explain complementary variance in organisational outcomes — POF predicts attitude outcomes (satisfaction, commitment), while PJF predicts performance outcomes. Saks and Ashforth (1997) established that PJF perceptions are shaped during organisational entry and evolve with job experience.

## Key Points

- PJF measures match between person capabilities and job demands
- Two types: demands-abilities fit and needs-supplies fit
- Assessed through structured interviews, SJTs, job knowledge tests, work samples
- Directly predicts task performance and job satisfaction
- Complementary to POF: PJF predicts performance, POF predicts attitudes
- No universal cutoffs; interpreted against role-specific competency frameworks
