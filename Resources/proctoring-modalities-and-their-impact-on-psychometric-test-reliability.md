## Summary

Proctoring modalities fall along a spectrum of invasiveness: lockdown browsers (lowest), live human proctoring via webcam, recorded proctoring with post-exam review, and automated AI-based proctoring with real-time flagging (highest) [@linkletter2026; @heinrich2025]. Each modality differentially affects test-taker experience and measurement reliability.

The Scherrer et al. (2025) study used webcam-based video recording (recorded proctoring) and found no significant differences in test duration across conditions, suggesting that this moderate-intensity modality does not substantially alter test-taking behaviour for most participants. However, 15% of participants showed noticeable sources of interference (leaving the room, background noise, technical issues), indicating that even recorded proctoring is imperfect in controlling the testing environment [@scherrer2025].

Research on test anxiety and proctoring reveals a nuanced picture. The Steger et al. (2018) meta-analysis found that reduced test anxiety in unproctored settings could partially explain score differences [@scherrer2025]. One study found that having a live proctor watching was most upsetting for high-anxiety students, and that proctored participants took only half as long to complete exams compared to unproctored ones — potentially indicating increased pressure rather than reduced cheating [@scherrer2025].

Heinrich's (2025) review of 33 proctoring systems found that most offer multiple modalities but provide insufficient detail on how AI processes data and makes decisions. Automated decision-making (where AI terminates exams without human oversight) was identified as particularly risky for fair assessment [@heinrich2025]. Rosalyn and Honorock were noted as providers who address students directly and explain data handling in accessible language, representing more ethically transparent approaches [@heinrich2025].

For psychometric assessment, the choice of modality involves a trade-off: more invasive proctoring better controls cheating but may introduce construct-irrelevant variance through anxiety induction, while less invasive methods preserve ecological validity but leave tests vulnerable to LLM-based cheating [@scherrer2025; @izlar2025].

## Key Points

- Proctoring modalities range from lockdown browsers to AI-automated surveillance
- Live proctoring increases anxiety, particularly for high-anxiety test-takers
- Recorded proctoring shows low cheating rates (4%) but imperfect environmental control
- Automated decision-making (AI terminating exams) poses risks for fair assessment
- No modality perfectly resolves the validity-security trade-off
