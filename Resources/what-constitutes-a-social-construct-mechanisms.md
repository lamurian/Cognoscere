## Summary

Two mechanisms explain how something becomes a social construct, distinguished by Haslanger: causal construction and constitutive construction [@haslanger2012]. Causal construction: social factors play a significant role in causing X to have the features by which it counts as an F — the wristwatch is caused to exist by social activity. Constitutive construction: social-conceptual activity is metaphysically necessary for X to be what it is — one cannot be a senator or a licensed dog without the relevant social recognition [@haslanger2012; @mallon2024]. The SEP summarizes: "X constitutively constructs Y if and only if X's conceptual or social activity regarding an individual y is metaphysically necessary for y to be a Y" [@mallon2024].

Searle's account of institutional facts supplies the canonical constitutive mechanism. Institutional facts exist because a community collectively accepts status functions, expressed by constitutive rules of the form "X counts as Y in C" — for example, tokens produced by the U.S. mint count as money in the United States [@searle1995; @mallon2024]. Searle's key remark: "For social facts, the attitude that we take toward the phenomenon is partly constitutive of the phenomenon. Part of being a cocktail party is being thought to be a cocktail party" [@searle1995].

Berger and Luckmann describe the sociological process: institutionalization occurs "whenever there is a reciprocal typification of habitualized actions by types of actors" [@berger1966; @fraser2025]. Habitualization narrows choices and makes interaction predictable; institutions then appear to later generations as objective, given, self-evident reality. Legitimation, a "second-order objectivation of meaning," makes institutions subjectively plausible and integrates them into symbolic universes [@berger1966; @fraser2025]. Fraser and Turcan add that legitimation and institutionalization are separate processes that can also run in the opposite order (legitimation first, institutionalization second), as with new technologies such as generative AI [@fraser2025].

Hacking identifies a distinctively reflexive constitutive mechanism: "making up people" through looping effects [@hacking1999; @mallon2024]. Classifications such as child abuse, multiple personality disorder, or fugue create new ways to be a person; those classified respond to the classification, changing the kind itself. Because the targets of the social sciences "are on the move," human kinds differ from natural kinds, whose targets are stationary [@hacking1999; @mallon2024].

## Key Points

- Causal construction: social factors cause X to have its features [@haslanger2012]
- Constitutive construction: social-conceptual activity is metaphysically necessary for X to be Y [@haslanger2012; @mallon2024]
- Searle: collective acceptance of status functions via "X counts as Y in C" [@searle1995]
- Berger and Luckmann: institutionalization as reciprocal typification of habitualized actions [@berger1966]
- Legitimation sustains institutions as second-order objectivation [@berger1966; @fraser2025]
- Hacking: looping effects make up people; human kinds are "on the move" [@hacking1999]
