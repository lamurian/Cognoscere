## Summary

Mental health citizen science collects sensitive information — diagnoses, symptoms, personal experiences — which raises ethical and legal issues beyond standard citizen science practice. The systematic review by Todowede et al. identified three broad ethical issue clusters: security, personal information, and safeguarding [@todowede2023]. Security concerns password-protected, secure platforms; personal information concerns data minimization (not collecting IP addresses, avoiding de-anonymisation); safeguarding concerns the risk of harm within the participant community itself [@todowede2023].

Consent is a defining difference from general citizen science. Where no personal or clinical information is collected, standard citizen science practice of no project-specific consent may be appropriate; where personal information is collected, in-person or online informed consent is required [@todowede2023]. Only two of nine reviewed studies reported ethical approval and three reported informed consent — reporting was inconsistent [@todowede2023].

A distinctive safeguarding risk documented across studies: hostile, threatening or negative comments and unsolicited health tips posted by participants on project platforms, posing distress and potential harm to others. Mitigation requires designated facilitators moderating forums and deleting harmful content, and researcher debriefing and training to support moderation [@todowede2023]. De-anonymisation is a specific concern when eligibility requires a mental health diagnosis — data must not be linkable to social media profiles [@todowede2023].

## Key Points

- **Consent**: Project-specific consent required only when personal (e.g., sociodemographic or clinical) information is collected; otherwise standard citizen science no-consent approach may apply [@todowede2023].
- **Data protection**: Follow GDPR and local information governance; anonymise datasets; make explicit any collection of IP addresses; name the platform and data repository in reports [@todowede2023].
- **Co-created data management plan**: Describe collection, analysis, reporting and sharing, plus data ownership and intellectual property, developed with contributors early in the project [@todowede2023].
- **Safeguarding**: Moderate all contributor interactions; remove offensive, threatening posts and unsolicited health advice; provide debriefing and training for moderators [@todowede2023].
- **De-anonymisation risk**: When participation is restricted to people with a target diagnosis, ensure data cannot be linked to social media or other identifying profiles [@todowede2023].
- **Ethical approval**: Obtain and report ethics approval before commencement — only two of nine reviewed studies did so [@todowede2023].
- **Accessible summary**: Provide an accessible plain-language summary of findings for contributors [@todowede2023].
- **Emotional safety**: Mutual respect and power management between researchers and participants are prerequisites; moderation of forums is a documented approach for safely involving community members at all phases [@todowede2023].
- **Contested terminology**: 'Citizen science' may be contentious in mental health (implied acceptance of scientific frames; exclusion of those not identifying as 'citizens'); 'community science' is an emerging alternative [@todowede2023].
