## Summary

General Mental Ability (GMA) tests measure abstract reasoning, learning speed, and problem-solving capacity. They are the single strongest predictors of job performance across all occupations, with Schmidt and Hunter (1998) reporting a corrected validity of r = .51. Raven's Progressive Matrices (RPM) is the most widely used non-verbal GMA proxy, measuring fluid intelligence (Gf) through visual pattern-completion tasks that require no language or cultural knowledge.

### Question Items and Format

RPM presents 3x3 grids of abstract shapes with one cell left blank. The test-taker selects the option that completes the pattern from a set of alternatives. Items are built from five rule families: progressive rotation, size/quantity progression, logical overlay (AND/OR/XOR), distribution of three elements, and figure addition/subtraction. The Standard Progressive Matrices (SPM) contains 60 items arranged across five sets (A-E) of 12 items each, with difficulty increasing within and across sets.

### Versions

- **Coloured Progressive Matrices (CPM)**: 36 items, 3 sets — for children ages 5-11, older adults, and clinical populations
- **Standard Progressive Matrices (SPM)**: 60 items, 5 sets — general population ages 6 through adulthood
- **Advanced Progressive Matrices (APM)**: 48 items, 2 sets — above-average adults, gifted assessment

### Scoring and Interpretation

Raw scores are the count of correct items (0-60 for SPM). These are converted to percentiles using age-based norm tables, then mapped onto IQ-style scales (mean 100, SD 15). No items are negatively marked. The SPM is typically administered with a 40-60 minute time limit in modern practice, though it was originally designed as an untimed power test.

### Interpretation Bands

Percentile ranks from normative tables indicate cognitive ability level. RPM scores correlate strongly with Spearman's g (general intelligence factor) and are used as an efficient proxy for GMA in research settings where full battery administration (e.g., WAIS-IV) is impractical.

## Key Points

- GMA tests have corrected validity of .51 for job performance — the strongest single predictor (Schmidt & Hunter, 1998)
- RPM measures fluid intelligence (Gf) without relying on language or cultural knowledge
- 60 items in SPM format, 5 rule families, raw score converted to percentiles via age norms
- RPM correlates strongly with Spearman's g and is used as a GMA proxy
- No penalty for guessing; time limit of 40-60 minutes in modern administration
