## Summary

Content validity evaluation is the most critical step in scale standardization, determining whether items adequately represent the construct domain. The COSMIN Delphi study established that content validity should be assessed through two components: relevance (do items measure the construct?) and comprehensiveness (do items cover all aspects of the construct?) (@terwee2018). Unlike other measurement properties, content validity is primarily qualitative, based on expert judgment rather than statistical analysis.

The Content Validity Ratio (CVR), developed by @lawshe1975, provides a quantitative method for expert panel item evaluation. Panel members rate each item on a 4-point scale (Highly Relevant to Not Relevant), and the CVR formula calculates the proportion of experts who rate an item as essential, adjusted for chance agreement. Recommended CVR thresholds vary by panel size: 0.99 for 5-6 raters, 0.85 for 8 raters, and 0.62 for 10 raters. Items falling below these thresholds should be rejected.

Expert panel reviews serve multiple purposes: content reviews (relevance, accuracy, completeness), sensitivity reviews (potential item bias), and standard setting (identifying cutoff scores). However, @terwee2018 note that content validity assessment is challenging because there is no gold standard, and different expert panels may reach different conclusions about the same items.

The Delphi process, used in COSMIN development, involves multiple rounds of structured expert consultation with feedback between rounds. This iterative consensus-building approach is considered more rigorous than single-round expert review because it allows experts to reconsider their judgments in light of group responses.

## Key Points

- Content validity has two components: relevance and comprehensiveness (@terwee2018)
- CVR formula quantifies expert panel agreement on item essentiality (@lawshe1975)
- CVR thresholds vary by panel size (0.99 for 5-6 raters to 0.62 for 10 raters)
- Expert reviews serve content, sensitivity, and standard-setting functions
- Delphi process enables iterative consensus-building across expert rounds
- Content validity remains challenging as no gold standard exists
