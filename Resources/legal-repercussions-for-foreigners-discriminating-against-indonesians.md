## Summary

Under Indonesian law, foreign tourists and expatriates who discriminate against Indonesian citizens based on race, ethnicity, or nationality face legal repercussions under two primary statutes: UU No. 40/2008 on Elimination of Racial and Ethnic Discrimination and UU No. 39/1999 on Human Rights. Additionally, immigration laws provide administrative sanctions including deportation and blacklisting.

## Criminal Liability

A foreigner in Indonesia who establishes a club and bans Indonesians from joining could be liable under [@pemerintahrepublikindonesia2008] Article 15 (Pasal 4a): engaging in distinction, exclusion, restriction, or selection based on race/ethnicity/nationality that deprives Indonesians of their rights. This carries a penalty of up to **1 year imprisonment** and/or a fine of up to **Rp 100 million**.

If the ban is publicly communicated through written notices, social media posts, or verbal statements showing racial hatred (Article 4b points 1-3), the penalty escalates to up to **5 years imprisonment** and/or a fine of up to **Rp 500 million** under Article 16.

The definition of "discriminatory acts" under Article 4a covers depriving persons of their social and cultural rights, which includes the right to participate in cultural, social, and economic events. A membership club that excludes Indonesians directly restricts their right to participate in community social activities.

## Immigration Consequences

Beyond criminal penalties, foreigners who violate Indonesian law face immigration sanctions under UU No. 6/2011 on Immigration:
- **Deportation** -- Foreigners who disrupt public order or violate laws can be deported
- **Blacklisting** -- Deported foreigners are placed on a deterrence list preventing re-entry
- **Visa/Stay Permit Revocation** -- The Immigration Directorate can revoke visas or residence permits

Recent Bali immigration enforcement has been aggressive: Bali officials have urged swift deportation of unruly foreigners, and there have been multiple high-profile cases of foreigners deported and blacklisted for misconduct [@socialexpat2025].

## Parallel Cases in Bali

Multiple incidents have occurred where Bali businesses allegedly barred Indonesian patrons. In December 2025, IL Salotto, an upscale bar in Uluwatu, faced allegations of denying entry to Indonesian patrons while allowing their foreign clients. Legal observers noted such practices could contravene Indonesian laws against racial and ethnic discrimination [@heybalinewsroom2025].

## Enforcement Considerations

Enforcement of UU 40/2008 has been limited in practice. The academic literature notes that the law's provisions are "comparatively narrow" and the "penalty scale may be insufficient to deter" [@latifatuzzahro2025]. However, the KUHP 2023 (Articles 244-245) strengthens criminal liability by incorporating discrimination directly into the Criminal Code, signaling greater enforcement intent.

Victims can file reports with:
1. Komnas HAM (National Human Rights Commission)
2. Local police (Polri)
3. Immigration authorities (for foreign nationals)

## Key Points

- UU 40/2008 applies to foreigners within Indonesian jurisdiction
- Direct ban on Indonesians from a club violates Article 15 (1 year imprisonment)
- Public expression of racial discrimination risks Article 16 (5 years imprisonment, Rp 500 million fine)
- Foreigners face additional deportation and blacklisting under immigration law
- KUHP 2023 Articles 244-245 further strengthen anti-discrimination provisions
- Parallel cases in Bali show growing public scrutiny of discriminatory practices
- Enforcement has historically been limited but is increasing with public attention
