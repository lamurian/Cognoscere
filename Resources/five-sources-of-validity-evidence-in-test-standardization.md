## Summary

The 2014 APA/AERA/NCME Standards define five distinct sources of validity evidence that must be accumulated to support score interpretations from any standardized instrument. This framework replaced the older tripartite model (content, criterion, construct validity) with a unified concept of validity supported by multiple sources of evidence (@knekta2019).

The five sources are: (1) Evidence based on test content — examines the relationship between a test's content and the construct it intends to measure, typically through expert review and alignment analysis. (2) Evidence based on response processes — documents the cognitive and behavioral processes respondents use when answering items, often through cognitive interviews or think-aloud protocols. (3) Evidence based on internal structure — evaluates whether the relationships among items, domains, and total scores reflect the construct's hypothesized structure, assessed through factor analysis and dimensionality testing. (4) Evidence based on relations to other variables — examines correlations between test scores and external criteria or other constructs, including convergent and discriminant validity. (5) Evidence based on consequences of testing — evaluates the social consequences of score interpretations and uses, including potential for bias and impact on test takers.

@knekta2019 emphasize that many researchers incorrectly treat validity as a single property (e.g., reporting only Cronbach's alpha), when in fact all five sources should be considered. The Standards framework requires that validity evidence be accumulated across multiple studies and contexts, not established in a single validation study.

This five-source framework has become the dominant paradigm in psychological and educational measurement, influencing journal reporting requirements, institutional review board expectations, and professional certification standards.

## Key Points

- Five sources: content, response processes, internal structure, relations to other variables, consequences (@knekta2019)
- Validity is unified: not three types but one property supported by multiple evidence sources
- Each source requires distinct methods (expert review, cognitive interviews, factor analysis, correlation studies, consequence analysis)
- Single-study validation is insufficient; evidence must accumulate across studies
- Framework is dominant paradigm in psychological and educational measurement
