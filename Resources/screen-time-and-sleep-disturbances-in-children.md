## Summary

Sleep disturbance is one of the most consistently documented effects of screen time in children. A systematic review found negative associations between screen time and sleep in 39 of 43 studies, with more night awakenings, longer sleep latency, and poorer sleep quality in children aged 1-5 years @gonzlezprez2025.

Meta-analysis revealed a negative correlation in infants (r=-0.07; 95% CI -0.12 to -0.03) and children aged 1-2 years (r=-0.13; 95% CI -0.21 to -0.04), though no significant relationship was found in preschoolers aged 3-4 years @gonzlezprez2025. Children exposed to screens for more than 2 hours/day had significantly higher risk of reduced sleep duration (OR 2.283; 95% CI 2.132-2.445) @gonzlezprez2025.

Bedtime media device use showed strong associations with inadequate sleep quantity (OR 2.17; 95% CI 1.42-3.32), poor sleep quality (OR 1.46; 95% CI 1.14-1.88), and excessive daytime sleepiness (OR 2.72; 95% CI 1.32-5.61) @kar2025. The mechanisms include blue light exposure suppressing melatonin production and increasing alertness @souza2025.

## Key Points

- 39 of 43 studies show negative association between screen time and sleep
- 2+ hours/day doubles risk of reduced sleep duration
- Bedtime device use most harmful: 2.17x risk of inadequate sleep
- Blue light suppresses melatonin, delaying sleep onset
- Sleep disruption amplifies other screen time impacts at all ages
