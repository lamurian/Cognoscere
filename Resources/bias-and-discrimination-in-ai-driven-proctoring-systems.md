## Summary

AI-driven proctoring systems embed biases that can disproportionately harm marginalized test-takers, an issue directly relevant to ethical psychometric assessment. Facial detection algorithms in proctoring software have been shown to fail disproportionately in recognising Black faces, sometimes preventing students of colour from accessing exams or disproportionately flagging them for suspected cheating [@coghlan2021; @linkletter2026]. As Tanksley documented, audits of Proctorio revealed an inability to see Black faces as human, resulting in discriminatory flagging that increases exposure to disciplinary action [@linkletter2026].

Ableist assumptions are also embedded in these systems. Proctoring software flags so-called behavioural abnormalities based on normative assumptions about body movement, gaze direction, and speech patterns, which can penalise neurodivergent test-takers or those with physical conditions that affect their appearance or movements [@linkletter2026; @heinrich2025]. The algorithmic impact assessments advocated by Linkletter would require scrutiny of such biases before deployment, but most institutions do not currently conduct them.

For digital self-administered psychometric instruments, these biases are doubly problematic. Not only do they unfairly penalise certain groups during the assessment process, but they also threaten measurement invariance — the psychometric property that ensures a test measures the same construct across different groups. If proctoring systems interact differently with test-takers based on their appearance or behaviour, this introduces systematic error that undermines the validity of psychometric scores.

## Key Points

- Facial detection algorithms fail to accurately recognise people of colour, leading to disproportionate flagging
- Ableist assumptions about normal behaviour penalise neurodivergent and disabled test-takers
- These biases threaten psychometric measurement invariance and score validity
- Algorithmic impact assessments should be required before deploying proctoring for psychological assessment
