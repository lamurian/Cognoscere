## Summary

Citizen science in Indonesian healthcare spans a spectrum from contributory (citizens provide data) to collaborative (citizens help design/refine) to co-created (citizens lead research). This typology, adapted from Bonney et al. (2009) and Shirk et al. (2012), maps major Indonesian initiatives. The country's decentralized health system, vast geography, and strong community structures (gotong royong, PKK, Posyandu) create fertile ground for all three categories.

## Key Points

### Contributory: Citizens as Data Collectors
- **Satu Rumah Satu Jumantik (G1R1J)**: Households report dengue larvae indices via kader; data flows upward to Puskesmas
- **PeduliLindungi/LaporCOVID**: Citizens report symptoms, test results, vaccination status via app; government analyzes
- **SP4N-LAPOR!**: National complaint system repurposed for COVID-19 reporting; citizens submit feedback
- **SatuSBM (PMI/IFRC CBS)**: Red Cross volunteers report unusual health events via mobile app to supervisors and health authorities
- **TB SOBAT TB app**: Kader screen contacts and report via mobile app to Puskesmas

### Collaborative: Citizens as Partners in Design/Analysis
- **Participatory Action Research for Dengue in Samarinda (GEMATRI)**: Community co-designed murals, training, policy advocacy; researchers facilitated
- **Desa Siaga TB (TB Alert Villages)**: Village cadres, leaders, and health workers co-design screening, education, and support protocols
- **Community-based NCD screening (Posbindu PTM)**: Cadres co-design schedules, materials, referral pathways with Puskesmas
- **Empowering Community Health Cadres in North Sumatra**: Cadres identified local challenges, co-designed training curriculum and referral tools

### Co-created: Citizens as Research Leaders
- **LaporCOVID-19 (Yayasan Warga Berdaya)**: Civil society coalition built chatbot platform, defined advocacy priorities, published policy briefs
- **Puspa program (COVID-19)**: Community groups mapped high-risk populations and monitored cases with health office support
- **Village-level malaria surveillance in Eastern Indonesia**: Communities design monitoring aligned with local ecology and traditions
